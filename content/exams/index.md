*Exams · the self-test layer of the site · Midterm 1 (Lectures 1–11) and Midterm 2 (Unit 3 and the lectures after it) · fourteen past exams typed with folded solutions*

Past exams are the best measuring stick the course offers: they show what a fluent student is expected to do in two hours, without notes beyond a handwritten sheet. They are not the best place to *learn* a topic — the lectures, the concept hubs and the problem families are — so this site keeps them as one layer. Take an exam once you have worked through a unit, see what it exposes, and go back to the pages that fix it.

## How to learn from a past exam

> [!recipe] Self-test, then repair
> 1. **Finish the unit first:** its lecture pages, their folded questions and the homework. An exam taken too early only tells you what you have not read yet.
> 2. **Take one recent exam under exam conditions:** a timer, closed book, only the handwritten sheet the exam allows, no calculator. If the course has not yet reached everything an exam covers, take only the problems on the lectures you have done.
> 3. **Grade it with the folded solutions** and write down, for every lost point, its [[problems/index|problem family]].
> 4. **Repair:** read that family's recipe and trap boxes, redo the problem without looking, then do one of the family's fresh practice problems.
> 5. **Use the other exams as a problem bank by family** rather than as more full runs; each family page lists every past instance.
> 6. **Write your sheet by hand** before the real exam. Choosing what goes on it is a review in itself; the [[exams/midterm-1/cheat-sheet|Midterm 1 cheat sheet]] shows what one looks like.
>
> The official keys are mostly right. Where one is not, the exam page says so in a warning box, and [[0-toolkit/05-errata|errata]] lists every slip found.
