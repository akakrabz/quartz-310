## Problem 1 · True/False (15 pts)

> [!question] Problem 1
> Answer **True** or **False** to each of the following statements:
>
> (a) $x[n] = \cos(\omega_0 n)$ is an eigenfunction of a stable LTI system.
>
> (b) Two different sequences of same length can have the same DFT.
>
> (c) The ideal reconstruction filter is causal.
>
> (d) Assume $x[n]$ is a finite-duration sequence of length 20, and $y[n]$ is obtained by zero-padding $x[n]$ to length 32. That is, $y[n] = x[n]$, for $n = 0, 1, \dots, 19$, and $y[n] = 0$, $n = 20, 21, \dots, 31$. Let $\{X[m]\}_{m=0}^{19}$ and $\{Y[m]\}_{m=0}^{31}$ be the DFT of $\{x[n]\}_{n=0}^{19}$ and $\{y[n]\}_{n=0}^{31}$, respectively, then $X[10] = Y[16]$.
>
> (e) Increasing the sampling period shrinks the corresponding DTFT.

> [!success]- Solution 1
> - **(a) False.** Only $e^{j\omega_0 n}$ is an eigenfunction of every LTI system; a one-sample delay returns $\cos(\omega_0 n - \omega_0)$.
> - **(b) False.** The DFT is invertible.
> - **(c) False.** $\frac{\sin(\pi t/T)}{\pi t/T}$ is nonzero for $t \lt 0$.
> - **(d) True.** $X[10] = X_d\!\left(\frac{2\pi\cdot 10}{20}\right) = X_d(\pi)$, $Y[16] = X_d\!\left(\frac{2\pi\cdot 16}{32}\right) = X_d(\pi)$.
> - **(e) False.** $\omega = \Omega T$: larger $T$ **stretches** the spectrum (only the height $\frac1T$ shrinks). Key: "The corresponding DTFT would be expanded."

> [!trap] Where points go
> - (e): "shrinks" refers to the frequency axis, not the $\frac1T$ height.

## Problem 2 · A frequency response with impulses (15 pts)

> [!question] Problem 2
> A causal LTI system is described by the difference equation: $y[n] = y[n-2] + x[n] - x[n-1]$.
>
> (a) Determine the system's transfer function $H(z)$.
>
> (b) Determine the system's unit impulse response $h[n]$.
>
> (c) Determine the system's frequency response $H_d(\omega)$; is $H_d(\omega) = H(z)\big|_{z=e^{j\omega}}$? If not, explain why.

> [!success]- Solution 2(a)–(b)
> **(a)** $Y(z)(1 - z^{-2}) = X(z)(1 - z^{-1})$, $1 - z^{-2} = (1 - z^{-1})(1 + z^{-1})$:
> $$
> \boldsymbol{H(z) = \frac{1 - z^{-1}}{1 - z^{-2}} = \frac{1}{1 + z^{-1}},\qquad \lvert z\rvert \gt 1\ \text{(causal)}}
> $$
> Pole at $z = 1$ cancels; $z = -1$ stays, on the unit circle.
>
> **(b)** $\boldsymbol{h[n] = (-1)^n u[n]}$.

> [!success]- Solution 2(c)
> **No.** ROC $\lvert z\rvert \gt 1$ misses the unit circle. Modulate $u[n] \leftrightarrow \frac{1}{1 - e^{-j\omega}} + \pi\sum_k\delta(\omega - 2\pi k)$ by $(-1)^n = e^{j\pi n}$:
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> \boldsymbol{H_d(\omega)} &\boldsymbol{{}= \frac{1}{1 - e^{-j(\omega - \pi)}} + \pi\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\delta(\omega - \pi - 2\pi k)}\\
> &\boldsymbol{{}= \frac{1}{1 + e^{-j\omega}} + \pi\sum_{k}\delta(\omega - \pi - 2\pi k)}
> \end{aligned}
> $$
> Equals $H(e^{j\omega})$ except for the impulses at $\omega = \pi$ (mod $2\pi$). Not BIBO stable ([[concepts/marginal-stability|marginal stability]]).

> [!trap] Where points go
> - Cancel first: the impulse sits at $\omega = \pi$, not $0$.

## Problem 3 · DFT symmetry, shift and modulation (10 pts)

> [!question] Problem 3
> Let $X[k]$ be the 8 point DFT of $x[n] = \{\underset{\uparrow}{1}, 3, 2, 0, 1, 3, 2, 0\}$.
>
> (a) Compute $X[k]$ for $k = 0, 2, 4, 6$.
>
> (b) Use properties of DFT to determine $Y[k]$ for $k = 0, 2, 4, 6$, where the corresponding sequence $y[n]$ is $y[n] = x[\langle 3 - n\rangle_8]\cdot(j)^n$.

> [!success]- Solution 3(a)
> $x[n+4] = x[n]$:
> $$
> X[k] = \sum_{n=0}^{7}x[n]e^{-j\frac{2\pi}{8}kn} = \left(1 + (-1)^k\right)\left(1 + 3e^{-j\frac{\pi}{4}k} + 2e^{-j\frac{\pi}{2}k}\right)
> $$
> $$
> \begin{gathered}
> \boldsymbol{X[0] = 12,\quad X[2] = 2(1 - 3j - 2) = -2 - 6j,}\\
> \boldsymbol{X[4] = 2(1 - 3 + 2) = 0,\quad X[6] = 2(1 + 3j - 2) = -2 + 6j}
> \end{gathered}
> $$

> [!success]- Solution 3(b)
> $W_8 = e^{-j2\pi/8}$:
> - $z[n] = x[\langle 3-n\rangle_8] = x[\langle -(n-3)\rangle_8] \leftrightarrow Z[k] = W_8^{3k}X[\langle -k\rangle_8]$;
> - $j^n = e^{j\frac{2\pi}{8}2n} = W_8^{-2n}$: $y[n] = z[n]W_8^{-2n} \leftrightarrow Y[k] = Z[\langle k-2\rangle_8]$.
>
> $Z[0] = X[0] = 12$, $Z[2] = W_8^{6}X[6] = j(-2 + 6j) = -6 - 2j$, $Z[4] = W_8^{12}X[4] = 0$, $Z[6] = W_8^{18}X[2] = -j(-2 - 6j) = -6 + 2j$:
> $$
> \begin{gathered}
> \boldsymbol{Y[0] = Z[6] = -6 + 2j,\quad Y[2] = Z[0] = 12,}\\
> \boldsymbol{Y[4] = Z[2] = -6 - 2j,\quad Y[6] = Z[4] = 0}
> \end{gathered}
> $$

> [!trap] Where points go
> - Reversal also reverses $k$: $X[\langle -k\rangle_8]$. $j^n$ shifts the DFT by **+2** bins.

> $$
> H_a(\Omega) = \begin{cases}1 - \dfrac{\lvert\Omega\rvert}{4000\pi}, & \lvert\Omega\rvert \le 4000\pi\\[4pt] 0, & \lvert\Omega\rvert \gt 4000\pi\end{cases}

## Problem 6 · Linear vs circular convolution (15 pts) (not on Fall 2026 MT2: circular convolution computation)

> [!question] Problem 6
> Consider the two finite-length sequences: $x = \{\underset{\uparrow}{-2}, 3, 0, 3\}$ and $h = \{\underset{\uparrow}{-2}, 5, 1, 5, -3\}$.
>
> (a) Let $Y_d(\omega) = X_d(\omega)H_d(\omega)$, where $X_d(\omega)$, $H_d(\omega)$ denote the DTFT of $x[n]$, $h[n]$. Compute $y[n]$.
>
> (b) Let $Y[k] = X[k]H[k]$, where $X[k]$, $H[k]$ denote the 6-point DFT of $x[n]$, $h[n]$. Find $y[n]$.
>
> (c) **True or False**: Zero padding both sequences to length $N = 7$ is adequate to guarantee that linear and circular convolutions coincide.

> [!success]- Solution 6
> **(a)** Linear convolution, length $4 + 5 - 1 = 8$:
> $$
> \boldsymbol{y[n] = \{\underset{\uparrow}{4},\ -16,\ 13,\ -13,\ 36,\ -6,\ 15,\ -9\}}
> $$
> Check: $\sum y = 24 = \left(\sum x\right)\left(\sum h\right) = 4 \cdot 6$.
>
> **(b)** 6-point circular: wrap $y[6], y[7]$ onto $n = 0, 1$:
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> \boldsymbol{y_6[n]} &\boldsymbol{{}= \{4 + 15,\ -16 - 9,\ 13,\ -13,\ 36,\ -6\}}\\
> &\boldsymbol{{}= \{\underset{\uparrow}{19},\ -25,\ 13,\ -13,\ 36,\ -6\}}
> \end{aligned}
> $$
> **(c) False.** Need $N \ge 4 + 5 - 1 = 8$; at $N = 7$, $y[7] = -9$ wraps onto $y[0]$.

> [!trap] Where points go
> - $y_N[n] = \sum_r y[n + rN]$: add the overflow to the front, don't drop it.

## Problem 7 · Where the DFT peaks land (15 pts)

> [!question] Problem 7
> A continuous-time signal $x_c(t) = \cos(10\pi t)$ is sampled at a rate of 100 Hz for 5 seconds to produce a discrete-time signal $x[n]$ with length $L = 500$.
>
> (a) Let $X[k]$ be the $L$-point DFT of $x[n]$. At what value(s) of $k$ will $X[k]$ have the greatest magnitude?
>
> (b) Suppose that $x[n]$ is zero-padded to a total length of $N = 1024$. At what value(s) of $k$ does the $N$-point DFT have the greatest magnitude?

> [!success]- Solution 7
> $x[n] = \cos\left(\frac{10\pi}{100}n\right) = \cos\left(\frac{\pi}{10}n\right)$; DTFT peaks at $\frac{\pi}{10}$ and $2\pi - \frac{\pi}{10} = \frac{19\pi}{10}$.
>
> **(a)** $\frac{2\pi k}{500} = \frac{\pi}{10}$ at $k = 25$ (25 whole cycles), mirror $500 - 25 = 475$:
> $$
> \boldsymbol{k = 25 \text{ and } k = 475}\qquad(\lvert X[25]\rvert = \lvert X[475]\rvert = 250,\ \text{all other bins } 0)
> $$
> **(b)** $\frac{2\pi k}{1024}$: $k = 51.2$ and $972.8$, between bins; nearest:
> $$
> \boldsymbol{k = 51 \text{ and } k = 973}
> $$
> ($\lvert X[51]\rvert \approx 245.6$, $\lvert X[52]\rvert \approx 193.2$, $\lvert X[50]\rvert \approx 129.4$.)

> [!trap] Where points go
> - Padding refines the grid but puts no bin on the true frequency; the peak drops below $L/2 = 250$.
