## Problem 1 · True/False (12 pts)

> [!question] Problem 1
> Select **True** or **False** to each of the following statements:
>
> (a) The DTFT of a length-5 signal $x[n]$ is given by $X_d(\omega) = 3 + \cos(2\omega)$. The DFT of $x[n]$ is then $X[k] = 3 + \cos\left(\frac{4\pi}{5}k\right)$.
>
> (b) Suppose we have a length-4 DFT $\{X[k]\}_{k=0}^{3} = \{4, 0, 0, 0\}$. The signal $\{x[n]\} = \mathrm{DFT}^{-1}\{X[k]\} = \{\cdots, 0, 0, \underset{\uparrow}{1}, 1, 1, 1, 0, 0, \cdots\}$.
>
> (c) An LTI system has frequency response $H_d(\omega)$ where $\left\lvert H_d\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right)\right\rvert = 2$ and $\angle H_d\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right) = \frac{\pi}{4}$. The input signal $x[n] = \cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2}n\right)$ will produce the output $y[n] = 2\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2}n + \frac{\pi}{4}\right)$.
>
> (d) A digital signal $x[n] = 3\delta[n-2]$ is given to an ideal D/A converter with sampling period $T = \frac14$ s to recover analog signal $x_a(t)$. The recovered signal is $x_a(t) = 3\,\mathrm{sinc}\left(4\pi\left(t - \frac12\right)\right)$, where $\mathrm{sinc}(x) = \frac{\sin(x)}{x}$.
>
> (e) Let $x_a(t) = \cos^2(40\pi t)$. We sample $x_a(t)$ to produce $x[n] = x_a(nT)$. There will be no aliasing present in $x[n]$ if $T = \frac{1}{50}$ s.
>
> (f) A digital signal $x[n]$ has DTFT $X_d(\omega) = e^{-j\frac52\omega}$. The signal $x[n] = \delta\left[n - \frac52\right]$.

> [!success]- Solution 1
> - **(a) True.** The DFT samples the DTFT: $X[k] = X_d\big(\frac{2\pi k}{5}\big) = 3 + \cos\frac{4\pi k}{5}$. (Here $x[n] = 3\delta[n] + \frac12\delta[n-2] + \frac12\delta[n+2]$; the sampling rule holds for any length-$N$ signal.)
> - **(b) False.** $x[n] = \frac14\sum_{k=0}^{3}X[k]e^{j\frac{2\pi k}{4}n} = \frac14\cdot4 = 1$ at **every** $n$: the inverse DFT is $4$-periodic (or lives on $0 \le n \le 3$ only), never zero outside. Same trap as [[exams/midterm-2/past-exams/spring-2025|SP2025 MT2]] #1(b).
> - **(c) False.** $A\cos(\omega_0n) \to A\lvert H_d(\omega_0)\rvert\cos(\omega_0n + \angle H_d(\omega_0))$ needs $H_d(-\omega_0) = H_d^*(\omega_0)$, i.e. a **real** $h[n]$ — not given. $y[n] = \frac12H_d(\frac{\pi}{2})e^{j\frac{\pi}{2}n} + \frac12H_d(-\frac{\pi}{2})e^{-j\frac{\pi}{2}n}$, and $H_d(-\frac{\pi}{2})$ is not given. Compare the Hermitian check in [[exams/midterm-2/past-exams/fall-2024|FA2024 MT2]] #4.
> - **(d) True.** Ideal D/A: $x_a(t) = \sum_n x[n]\,\mathrm{sinc}\big(\frac{\pi}{T}(t - nT)\big)$ with the course sinc. $\frac{\pi}{T} = 4\pi$, $nT = \frac24 = \frac12$: $3\,\mathrm{sinc}\big(4\pi(t - \frac12)\big)$.
> - **(e) False.** $\cos^2(40\pi t) = \frac12 + \frac12\cos(80\pi t)$: a 40 Hz line, and $f_s = 50$ Hz $\lt 80$ Hz. $\omega = 80\pi\cdot\frac{1}{50} = \frac{8\pi}{5} \gt \pi$ folds to $\frac{2\pi}{5}$ (10 Hz).
> - **(f) False.** $\delta[n - \frac52]$ is not a sequence. Inverse DTFT: $x[n] = \frac{\sin\pi(n - \frac52)}{\pi(n - \frac52)} = \frac{2(-1)^{n+1}}{\pi(2n - 5)}$, nonzero at every $n$ ($x[2] = x[3] = \frac{2}{\pi}$): a sinc centred between samples.

> [!trap] Where points go
> - No penalty stated: answer all six. (c) is the subtle one — "LTI" alone does not make $h[n]$ real.
> - (a) vs (b): the DFT samples the DTFT; the inverse DFT is periodic. (e): square the cosine first — the 40 Hz line, not 20 Hz, sets the Nyquist rate (80 Hz).

> (a) Determine the impulse response of the system $h[n]$. Your answer must be real-valued for full credit.
>
> (b) Determine the output $y[n]$ of the system given the input $x[n] = 4 + \cos\left(\frac{\pi}{6}n\right) + 3j^n + (-1)^n$.

> [!success]- Solution 2
> **(a)** Quick route: the magnitude is two ideal low-passes stacked (cutoffs $\frac{3\pi}{4}$ and $\frac{\pi}{4}$) and the phase $-\omega$ is a one-sample delay:
> $$
> H_d(\omega) = \big(\mathrm{LPF}_{3\pi/4}(\omega) + \mathrm{LPF}_{\pi/4}(\omega)\big)e^{-j\omega}.
> $$
> Table pair $\mathrm{LPF}_{\omega_c} \leftrightarrow \frac{\sin\omega_cn}{\pi n} = \frac{\omega_c}{\pi}\mathrm{sinc}(\omega_cn)$, then $n \to n-1$:
> $$
> \begin{gathered}
> \boldsymbol{h[n] = \frac34\,\mathrm{sinc}\Big(\frac{3\pi}{4}(n-1)\Big) + \frac14\,\mathrm{sinc}\Big(\frac{\pi}{4}(n-1)\Big)}\\
> = \frac{\sin\frac{3\pi}{4}(n-1) + \sin\frac{\pi}{4}(n-1)}{\pi(n-1)}
> \end{gathered}
> $$
> $h[1] = \frac34 + \frac14 = 1$; real, as it must be ($H_d(-\omega) = H_d^*(\omega)$: even magnitude, odd phase).
>
> Key's route: inverse DTFT as two integrals,
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> h[n] &= \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-3\pi/4}^{3\pi/4}e^{j\omega(n-1)}\,d\omega + \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi/4}^{\pi/4}e^{j\omega(n-1)}\,d\omega\\
> &= \frac{\sin\frac{3\pi}{4}(n-1)}{\pi(n-1)} + \frac{\sin\frac{\pi}{4}(n-1)}{\pi(n-1)},
> \end{aligned}
> $$
> (key: "alternatively, use table pairs + delay property").
>
> **(b)** $h[n]$ real, so the real-sinusoid rule is licensed. Read the four eigenvalues off the plots: $H_d(0) = 2$; $H_d(\frac{\pi}{6}) = 2e^{-j\pi/6}$ ($\frac{\pi}{6} \lt \frac{\pi}{4}$); $j^n = e^{j\frac{\pi}{2}n}$ with $H_d(\frac{\pi}{2}) = 1\cdot e^{-j\pi/2} = -j$ ($\frac{\pi}{4} \lt \frac{\pi}{2} \le \frac{3\pi}{4}$); $(-1)^n = e^{j\pi n}$ with $H_d(\pi) = 0$.
> $$
> \boldsymbol{y[n] = 8 + 2\cos\Big(\frac{\pi}{6}n - \frac{\pi}{6}\Big) + 3e^{j\frac{\pi}{2}(n-1)}}
> $$
> ($3e^{j\frac{\pi}{2}(n-1)} = -3j\cdot j^n = 3j^{\,n-1}$.) Shortcut check: $-\omega$ is a pure one-sample delay, so each surviving term is the input term delayed by 1 and scaled by its gain: $2\cdot4$, $2\cos\frac{\pi}{6}(n-1)$, $1\cdot3j^{n-1}$, $0\cdot(-1)^{n-1}$.

> [!trap] Where points go
> - $\frac{\pi}{2}$ sits on the outer step (gain 1), not the inner one (gain 2); $\pi$ is outside both (gain 0), so the $(-1)^n$ term dies.
> - "Real-valued for full credit": sines or sincs of $(n-1)$, not $e^{\pm j\omega_c(n-1)}$ pieces; the weights $\frac34$, $\frac14$ are $\frac{\omega_c}{\pi}$ (course sinc $\frac{\sin x}{x}$).
> - The delay belongs to both pieces: $(n-1)$ inside both sincs, not just one.

## Problem 3 · A non-causal FIR: magnitude, phase with jumps and wraps (12 pts)

> [!question] Problem 3
> A non-causal discrete-time LTI system with input $x[n]$ and output $y[n]$ is given by the following difference equation.
> $$
> y[n] = x[n+3] - x[n]
> $$
> (a) Determine the frequency response of this system $H_d(\omega)$.
>
> (b) Plot the magnitude response $\lvert H_d(\omega)\rvert$ and phase response $\angle H_d(\omega)$ for $-\pi \le \omega \le \pi$. Make sure to carefully label your plots.

> [!success]- Solution 3
> **(a)** $h[n] = \delta[n+3] - \delta[n]$, so $H_d(\omega) = e^{j3\omega} - 1$. Factor out half the advance:
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> \boldsymbol{H_d(\omega)} &\boldsymbol{{}= e^{j3\omega} - 1 = e^{j\frac{3\omega}{2}}\big(e^{j\frac{3\omega}{2}} - e^{-j\frac{3\omega}{2}}\big)}\\
> &\boldsymbol{{}= 2j\,e^{j\frac{3\omega}{2}}\sin\frac{3\omega}{2} = 2e^{j\left(\frac{\pi}{2} + \frac{3\omega}{2}\right)}\sin\frac{3\omega}{2}}
> \end{aligned}
> $$
> **(b)** Magnitude: $\lvert H_d(\omega)\rvert = 2\left\lvert\sin\frac{3\omega}{2}\right\rvert$ — zero at $0, \pm\frac{2\pi}{3}$; peaks of 2 at $\pm\frac{\pi}{3}, \pm\pi$.
>
> Phase: $\angle H_d = \frac{\pi}{2} + \frac{3\omega}{2} + \angle\sin\frac{3\omega}{2}$, where $\angle\sin\frac{3\omega}{2} = 0$ for $\sin\frac{3\omega}{2} \gt 0$ ($0 \lt \omega \lt \frac{2\pi}{3}$ and $-\pi \lt \omega \lt -\frac{2\pi}{3}$) and $\pm\pi$ where it is negative; then wrap into $[-\pi, \pi]$. Ramps of slope $+\frac32$ (an advance of 1.5 samples, group delay $-\frac32$):
>
> | interval | phase | ends |
> |---|---|---|
> | $(0, \frac{\pi}{3})$ | $\frac{\pi}{2} + \frac{3\omega}{2}$ | $\frac{\pi}{2} \to \pi$ |
> | $(\frac{\pi}{3}, \frac{2\pi}{3})$ | $\frac{\pi}{2} + \frac{3\omega}{2} - 2\pi$ ($2\pi$ wrap at $\frac{\pi}{3}$) | $-\pi \to -\frac{\pi}{2}$ |
> | $(\frac{2\pi}{3}, \pi]$ | $\frac{\pi}{2} + \frac{3\omega}{2} + \pi - 2\pi$ ($\pi$ jump at $\frac{2\pi}{3}$) | $\frac{\pi}{2} \to \pi$ |
>
> Negative $\omega$: odd mirror ($h$ real) — $-\pi \to -\frac{\pi}{2}$, $\frac{\pi}{2} \to \pi$, $-\pi \to -\frac{\pi}{2}$ on the three intervals. Net: $\pi$ jumps (dashed) at $0$, $\pm\frac{2\pi}{3}$, where the magnitude touches zero; $2\pi$ wraps at $\pm\frac{\pi}{3}$, where the ramp leaves the strip. Spot checks: $H_d(\pm\pi) = H_d(\pm\frac{\pi}{3}) = -2$ (phase $\pm\pi$, the wrap points); $H_d(\frac{\pi}{6}) = j - 1$, phase $\frac{3\pi}{4}$.

> A/D: $\omega = \Omega T_1$, folded into $(-\pi, \pi]$. D/A: $\Omega = \omega/T_2$. Tones: 10 Hz ($20\pi$) and 45 Hz ($90\pi$).
>
> **(a)** $T_1 = \frac{1}{100}$: $\omega_1 = \frac{20\pi}{100} = \frac{\pi}{5}$, $\omega_2 = \frac{90\pi}{100} = \frac{9\pi}{10}$, both $\lt \pi$ — no aliasing. $T_2 = \frac{1}{20}$: $\Omega = 20\omega$.

> \boldsymbol{y_a(t) = \sin(4\pi t) + \sin(18\pi t)}
> \end{gathered}
> $$

>
> **(b)** $T = \frac{1}{60}$: $\omega_1 = \frac{20\pi}{60} = \frac{\pi}{3}$; $\omega_2 = \frac{90\pi}{60} = \frac{3\pi}{2} \gt \pi$, aliased: $\frac{3\pi}{2} - 2\pi = -\frac{\pi}{2}$, so $\sin(\frac{3\pi}{2}n) = -\sin(\frac{\pi}{2}n)$. D/A at $\frac{1}{60}$: $-\frac{\pi}{2}\cdot60 = -30\pi$.
> $$
> \begin{gathered}
> \boldsymbol{x[n] = \sin\Big(\frac{\pi}{3}n\Big) - \sin\Big(\frac{\pi}{2}n\Big)}\\
> \boldsymbol{y_a(t) = \sin(20\pi t) - \sin(30\pi t)}
> \end{gathered}
> $$
> 45 Hz at $f_s = 60$ Hz folds to $60 - 45 = 15$ Hz, sign flipped.
>
> **(c)** Need the 10 Hz tone intact and the 45 Hz tone gone (they cannot cancel or merge into one unit-amplitude sine). Intact: $20\pi T \lt \pi$, $T \lt \frac{1}{20}$ s. > $$
> \boldsymbol{T \in \Big\{\frac{1}{90},\ \frac{1}{45},\ \frac{1}{30},\ \frac{2}{45}\Big\}\ \text{s — any two}}
> $$
> $k = 5$ ($T = \frac{1}{18}$ s) kills the 45 Hz tone but $20\pi T = \frac{10\pi}{9} \gt \pi$: 10 Hz folds to $-\frac{8\pi}{9}$, output $-\sin(16\pi t)$. Key lists the same four and strikes $\frac{1}{18}$.

> - (a): $\Omega = \omega/T_2$, not $\omega/T_1$.
> - (b): the sign. $\sin(\frac{3\pi}{2}n) = -\sin(\frac{\pi}{2}n)$, so $-\sin(30\pi t)$, not $+$. Compare [[exams/midterm-2/past-exams/spring-2025|SP2025 MT2]] #6(c).
> - (c): a Nyquist-safe $T$ such as $\frac{1}{100}$ keeps the 45 Hz tone — the output would be both tones. Any $T$ that works must make $90T$ an integer.

## Problem 6 · Which bins, and how much padding (10 pts)

> [!question] Problem 6
> Let $\{x[n]\}_{n=0}^{31}$ be a real-valued length-32 signal with DFT $\{X[k]\}_{k=0}^{31}$ and DTFT $X_d(\omega)$.
>
> (a) Which of the following relationships are true? (Circle True or False)
>
> i. $X[0] = X_d(2\pi)$ · ii. $X[2] = X_d\left(\frac{\pi}{8}\right)$ · iii. $X[4] = X_d\left(\frac{\pi}{8}\right)$ · iv. $X[6] = X_d^*\left(-\frac{3\pi}{8}\right)$ · v. $X[8] = X_d^*\left(-\frac{\pi}{2}\right)$
>
> (b) We zero pad $x[n]$ with $L$ zeros to obtain $\{y[n]\}_{n=0}^{31+L}$ with DFT $\{Y[k]\}_{k=0}^{31+L}$. We want to ensure that $Y[k]$ evaluates $X_d\left(\frac{\pi}{3}\right)$ and $X_d\left(\frac{4\pi}{9}\right)$ for some values of $k$. Which of the following values of $L$ will accomplish this? (Circle True or False)
>
> i. $L = 4$ · ii. $L = 16$ · iii. $L = 22$ · iv. $L = 40$ · v. $L = 48$

> [!success]- Solution 6
> **(a)** $X[k] = X_d(\omega_k)$ with $\omega_k = \frac{2\pi k}{32} = \frac{\pi k}{16}$; $X_d$ is $2\pi$-periodic; $x$ real ⇒ $X_d(-\omega) = X_d^*(\omega)$.
> - i. **True**: $X[0] = X_d(0) = X_d(2\pi)$.
> - ii. **True**: $\omega_2 = \frac{\pi}{8}$.
> - iii. **False**: $\omega_4 = \frac{\pi}{4} \ne \frac{\pi}{8}$ (that would need $N = 64$).
> - iv. **True**: $\omega_6 = \frac{3\pi}{8}$ and $X_d(\frac{3\pi}{8}) = X_d^*(-\frac{3\pi}{8})$.
> - v. **True**: $\omega_8 = \frac{\pi}{2}$ and $X_d(\frac{\pi}{2}) = X_d^*(-\frac{\pi}{2})$.
>
> **(b)** Padded length $M = 32 + L$, $Y[k] = X_d\big(\frac{2\pi k}{M}\big)$. Quick route — divisibility: need integers $k_1, k_2$ with $\frac{2\pi k_1}{M} = \frac{\pi}{3}$ and $\frac{2\pi k_2}{M} = \frac{4\pi}{9}$, i.e. $k_1 = \frac{M}{6}$ and $k_2 = \frac{2M}{9}$, so $6 \mid M$ and $9 \mid M$: **$18 \mid M$**.
>
> | $L$ | $M$ | $M/6$ | $2M/9$ | |
> |---|---|---|---|---|
> | 4 | 36 | 6 | 8 | **True** |
> | 16 | 48 | 8 | $10.67$ | **False** |
> | 22 | 54 | 9 | 12 | **True** |
> | 40 | 72 | 12 | 16 | **True** |
> | 48 | 80 | $13.33$ | $17.78$ | **False** |
>
> Key's route: write the bin spacing for each $M$ ($\frac{\pi}{18}, \frac{\pi}{24}, \frac{\pi}{27}, \frac{\pi}{36}, \frac{\pi}{40}$) and test whether $\frac{\pi}{3}$ and $\frac{4\pi}{9}$ are integer multiples — same verdicts.

> [!trap] Where points go
> - (b): $L = 16$ passes the $\frac{\pi}{3}$ test ($48/6 = 8$) and fails $\frac{4\pi}{9}$ — test both targets. Zero padding never changes $X_d$, only where it is sampled.
> - (a) iii: bin spacing is $\frac{\pi}{16}$, so bin 4 is $\frac{\pi}{4}$. The conjugates in iv–v need "real-valued" — the problem says so.

> We may then compute the inverse DFT of $Y[k]$ to obtain the length-$2N$ signal $\{y[n]\}_{n=0}^{2N-1}$. Using the definitions of the DFT and inverse DFT and any necessary DFT properties, show that
> $$
> y[2n] = x[n],\qquad y[2n+1] = 0,
> $$
> for $n = 0, 1, \ldots, N-1$.

> [!success]- Solution 7
> $Y[k] = X[k]$ for $0 \le k \le N-1$ and $Y[k] = X[k-N]$ for $N \le k \le 2N-1$. Inverse length-$2N$ DFT, split at $k = N$, substitute $k = m + N$ in the upper half:
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> y[n] &= \frac{1}{2N}\sum_{k=0}^{2N-1}Y[k]e^{j\frac{2\pi k}{2N}n}\\
> &= \frac{1}{2N}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}X[k]e^{j\frac{\pi k}{N}n} + \frac{1}{2N}\sum_{m=0}^{N-1}X[m]e^{j\frac{\pi(m+N)}{N}n}\\
> &= \frac{1}{2N}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}X[k]e^{j\frac{\pi k}{N}n}\big(1 + e^{j\pi n}\big)
> = \frac{1 + (-1)^n}{2N}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}X[k]e^{j\frac{\pi k}{N}n}.
> \end{aligned}
> $$
> - Odd index: $1 + (-1)^{2n+1} = 0$, so $\boldsymbol{y[2n+1] = 0}$.
> - Even index $2n$ with $0 \le n \le N-1$: $1 + (-1)^{2n} = 2$ and $e^{j\frac{\pi k}{N}2n} = e^{j\frac{2\pi k}{N}n}$, so
> $$
> \boldsymbol{y[2n] = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}X[k]e^{j\frac{2\pi k}{N}n} = x[n]}
> $$
> by the inverse length-$N$ DFT. ∎
>
> Key's route: $y[2n]$ and $y[2n+1]$ as two separate computations from the start (same substitution; $e^{j\pi\cdot2n} = 1$ adds the halves, $e^{j\pi(2n+1)} = -1$ cancels them). Property view: repeating a DFT $\leftrightarrow$ upsampling by 2 in time, the dual of "zero-padding in time $\leftrightarrow$ denser samples of $X_d$" (#6(b)).

> [!trap] Where points go
> - Not re-indexing the second half: for $N \le k \le 2N-1$, $Y[k] = X[k-N]$ and the exponent picks up $e^{j\pi n}$ — that factor is the whole proof.
> - The $\frac{1}{2N}$: the two halves each carry $\frac{1}{2N}$ and add to $\frac{1}{N}$ only for even $n$.
> - "Using the definitions": a one-line appeal to an upsampling property is not what is asked.

> (a) Match the DFT plots to one of the four DFTs, i.e. DFT I, DFT II, DFT III, or DFT IV, by circling one DFT for each plot. (Note: you must justify your choices or show work to receive full credit.)
>
> (b) Estimate the unknown signal spectral parameters $A_1$, $A_2$, $\Omega_1$, $\Omega_2$ from the DFT plots. (Note: reasonably close answers will receive full credit and you must show work to receive full credit.)

> [!success]- Solution 8(a)
> Count bins, then look at the lobes.
> - Plot (a): the only plot with **16** bins ⇒ **DFT I** (16 samples, length-16 DFT).
> - Plot (b): side lobes almost gone, main lobes wider ⇒ Hamming window ⇒ **DFT IV**.
> - Plots (c), (d): 32 bins each. (c) is plot (a) with the gaps filled: every stem of (a) reappears at the even bins of (c) ($4.0, 6.4, 6.1$ at $k = 6, 8, 10$) and the odd bins sample the **same DTFT** in between — same 16 data points, same $\frac{AN}{2}$ scale with $N = 16$ (the new $k = 7$ stem reaches ≈ 8 only because it lands closer to the lobe's peak) ⇒ **DFT II**. (d) peaks ≈ 16 and ≈ 8, twice as tall and narrower — twice the data ($N = 32$, main lobe $\frac{4\pi}{32}$) ⇒ **DFT III**.
>
> $$
> \boldsymbol{\text{(a) DFT I}\qquad\text{(b) DFT IV}\qquad\text{(c) DFT II}\qquad\text{(d) DFT III}}
> $$
> Key's words: (a) "only length-16 plot"; (b) "very low side lobes → Hamming"; (c) "same heights of main lobes but denser DTFT values → zero padding"; (d) "higher and narrower main lobes → more points from $x[n]$".

> [!success]- Solution 8(b)
> Key reads plot (c) (DFT II: 16 samples, 32 bins): peaks at $k_1 = 7$ (height ≈ 8) and $k_2 = 10$ (height ≈ 6).
> - Frequencies: $\omega_k = \frac{2\pi k}{32}$ gives $\omega_1 = \frac{7\pi}{16}$, $\omega_2 = \frac{5\pi}{8}$; $\Omega = \frac{\omega}{T} = 100\,\omega$:
> $$
> \begin{gathered}
> \boldsymbol{\Omega_1 \approx \frac{175\pi}{4}\ \text{rad/s}\ (\approx 21.9\ \text{Hz})}\\
> \boldsymbol{\Omega_2 \approx \frac{125\pi}{2}\ \text{rad/s}\ (= 31.25\ \text{Hz})}
> \end{gathered}
> $$
> - Amplitudes: a cosine of amplitude $A$ on a bin gives $\lvert X[k]\rvert = \frac{AN}{2}$ with $N$ the number of real samples, 16 here: $A_1 \approx \frac{2\cdot8}{16}$, $A_2 \approx \frac{2\cdot6}{16}$:
> $$
> \boldsymbol{A_1 \approx 1,\qquad A_2 \approx \tfrac34}
> $$
> Cross-check on plot (d) (32 samples): $k = 7$ reads ≈ 16 $= \frac{A_1\cdot32}{2}$, $A_1 = 1$ again; the second tone straddles bins 10–11 (≈ 8, ≈ 6), so it lies between $\frac{5\pi}{8}$ and $\frac{11\pi}{16}$ and one bin under-reads $A_2$. This page's plots were regenerated with $A_1 = 1$, $A_2 = 0.7$, $\Omega_1 = 45\pi$ (22.5 Hz), $\Omega_2 = 65\pi$ (32.5 Hz), the values that reproduce the exam's stems; the key's estimates are within 4 % on $\Omega$; $A_2$ reads $\frac34$ rather than $0.7$ because tone 1's first side lobe leaks ≈ 2 into bin 10 (tone 2 alone gives ≈ 5.4 there) — "reasonably close answers receive full credit", with the $\frac{2\pi k}{M}$, $\frac{\omega}{T}$, $\frac{AN}{2}$ steps shown.

> [!trap] Where points go
> - (c) vs (d): zero padding adds no data — plot (a)'s stems reappear at the even bins of (c), and the finer grid merely lands closer to the DTFT's peak ($8.2$ at $k = 7$ vs $6.4$ at $k = 4$ of (a)). Doubling the data doubles the peak.
> - $N$ in $\frac{AN}{2}$ is the number of **nonzero** samples: using 32 for plot (c) halves both amplitudes.
> - Bin to frequency: $\omega = \frac{2\pi k}{M}$, $M$ = DFT length (32 for (b)–(d), 16 for (a)), then $\Omega = \omega/T$. Hamming scales the peak by 0.54: never read amplitudes off plot (b).
