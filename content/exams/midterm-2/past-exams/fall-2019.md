## Problem 1 · True/False (20 pts)

> [!question] Problem 1
> Mark "True" or "False" for the following statements.
>
> (a) Zero padding a finite-length signal can change its DTFT.
>
> (b) Suppose that $x[n]$ is bandlimited to $\frac{\pi}{4}$. Then, its DTFT $X_d(\omega) = 0$ for $\lvert\omega\rvert \gt \frac{\pi}{4}$.
>
> (c) The DTFT of the finite-length signal $x[n] = \cos\left(\frac{\pi}{32}n\right)$, $n = 0, 1, \dots, 15$ is given by the formula $X_d(\omega) = \pi\left[\delta\left(\omega - \frac{\pi}{32}\right) + \delta\left(\omega + \frac{\pi}{32}\right)\right]$.
>
> (d) An LSI system has transfer function $H(z)$. Suppose that the output of the system to the input $x[n] = \cos(\omega_0 n + \phi)$ is $y[n] = \lvert H(e^{j\omega_0})\rvert\cos\left(\omega_0 n + \phi + \angle H(e^{j\omega_0})\right)$ for any $\omega_0, \phi$, where $H(e^{j\omega}) = H(z)\big|_{z=e^{j\omega}}$. Then, the system $h[n]$ can be either real or complex.
>
> (e) The DTFT $X_d(\omega)$ of a discrete-time signal $x[n]$ is periodic only when $x[n]$ has infinite length.
>
> (f) The 64-point DFT of the finite-length signal $x[n] = \cos\left(\frac{\pi}{16}n\right)$, $0 \le n \le 63$ has only two nonzero elements.
>
> (g) Linear convolution can be computed as a circular convolution via zero-padding.
>
> (h) Circular convolution can be applied to two finite duration sequences with arbitrary lengths.
>
> (i) Let $\{x[n]\}_{n=0}^{5} = \{1, -1, -2, 3, 4, -3\}$. Consider the corresponding 6-point DFT $\{X[k]\}_{k=0}^{5}$. Then, $X[0] = 0$.
>
> (j) FFT is just an efficient algorithm to evaluate DFT.

> [!success]- Solution 1
> - **(a) False.** Appended zeros add $0 \cdot e^{-j\omega n}$ terms; $X_d(\omega)$ is unchanged. Padding changes the **DFT** (finer sampling of the same $X_d$).
> - **(b) False.** $X_d$ is $2\pi$-periodic: $X_d(2\pi) = X_d(0)$, nonzero e.g. for an ideal lowpass. "Bandlimited to $\frac{\pi}{4}$" means $X_d(\omega) = 0$ for $\frac{\pi}{4} \lt \lvert\omega\rvert \le \pi$.
> - **(c) False.** Impulses need an infinitely long cosine. Sixteen samples give two shifted Dirichlet kernels, nonzero almost everywhere.
> - **(d) False.** Output is $\tfrac12\left[H_d(\omega_0)e^{j(\omega_0 n+\phi)} + H_d(-\omega_0)e^{-j(\omega_0 n+\phi)}\right]$; it equals the stated form for all $\omega_0, \phi$ only if $H_d(-\omega_0) = H_d^*(\omega_0)$, i.e. $h[n]$ **real**.
> - **(e) False.** $e^{-j(\omega+2\pi)n} = e^{-j\omega n}$ for every integer $n$: every DTFT is $2\pi$-periodic.
> - **(f) True.** $\frac{\pi}{16} = \frac{2\pi \cdot 2}{64}$: two whole cycles, so $X[2] = X[62] = 32$, other 62 bins 0.
> - **(g) True.** Pad both to $N \ge L_x + L_h - 1$ (see #11).
> - **(h) False** (key's answer). $N$-point circular convolution needs two length-$N$ sequences; pad first.
> - **(i) False.** $X[0] = \sum_n x[n] = 1 - 1 - 2 + 3 + 4 - 3 = 2$.
> - **(j) True.** Same DFT values in about $N\log_2 N$ operations instead of $N^2$.
>
> Key marks (g), (h), (j) "Not responsible for on our Midterm 2!".

> [!trap] Where points go
> - (b), (e): the DTFT repeats every $2\pi$; "$\lvert\omega\rvert \gt \frac{\pi}{4}$" includes $\omega = 2\pi$.
> - (c): a finite piece of a sinusoid has a smooth spectrum with sidelobes, no impulses.

## Problem 2 · DFT bins in hertz (6 pts)

> [!question] Problem 2
> A real continuous-time signal $x_c(t)$ is bandlimited to frequencies below 20 kHz, i.e., $X_a(\Omega) = 0$ for $\lvert\Omega\rvert \ge 2\pi(20000)$. Suppose that $x_a(t)$ is sampled with sampling frequency $F_s = 40$ kHz to produce $x[n] = x_a(nT_s)$ and 1000 samples are extracted corresponding to $n = 0, 1, \dots, 999$. Let $\{X[k]\}_{k=0}^{999}$ be the 1000-point DFT of $\{x[n]\}_{n=0}^{999}$.
>
> (a) To what continuous-time frequencies do the indices $k = 300$ and $k = 800$ correspond? Provide your answers in Hz.
>
> (b) What is the spacing between the DFT samples in Hz (continuous-time frequency spacing)?

> [!success]- Solution 2
> Bin $k$ sits at $\omega_k = \frac{2\pi k}{N}$; $\omega = \Omega T$ converts. Bins past $N/2$ are negative frequencies ($\omega_k \equiv \omega_k - 2\pi$).
>
> **(a)** $\omega_{300} = \frac{2\pi \cdot 300}{1000} = \frac{3\pi}{5}$, $\Omega_{300} = \frac{3\pi}{5}\cdot 40000 = 24000\pi$ rad/s:
> $$
> \boldsymbol{F_{300} = 12\ \text{kHz}}
> $$
> $\omega_{800} = \frac{8\pi}{5} \equiv -\frac{2\pi}{5}$, $\Omega_{800} = -16000\pi$ rad/s:
> $$
> \boldsymbol{F_{800} = -8\ \text{kHz}}
> $$
> (mirror of the +8 kHz component at $k = 200$).
>
> **(b)** $\Delta\omega = \frac{2\pi}{N} = \frac{\pi}{500}$, $\Delta\Omega = 80\pi$ rad/s:
> $$
> \boldsymbol{\Delta F = \frac{F_s}{N} = 40\ \text{Hz}}
> $$

> [!trap] Where points go
> - 32 kHz for $k = 800$ forgets that the upper half of the DFT is negative frequencies (key: "No aliasing!", every bin maps into $(-20, 20)$ kHz).

## Problem 3 · DTFT values without the DTFT (9 pts)

> [!question] Problem 3
> Consider the following signal:
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> x[n] &= \delta[n+3] - 3\delta[n+2] + 5\delta[n+1] - 7\delta[n]\\
> &\quad + 5\delta[n-1] - 3\delta[n-2] + \delta[n-3]
> \end{aligned}
> $$
> Compute the following quantities:
>
> (a) $X_d(0)$
>
> (b) $X_d(\pi)$
>
> (c) $\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}X_d(\omega)\,d\omega$

> [!success]- Solution 3
> $x = \{1,\ -3,\ 5,\ \underset{\uparrow}{-7},\ 5,\ -3,\ 1\}$.
>
> **(a)** $X_d(0) = \sum_n x[n] = 1 - 3 + 5 - 7 + 5 - 3 + 1 = \boldsymbol{-1}$.
>
> **(b)** $e^{-j\pi n} = (-1)^n$: $X_d(\pi) = -1 - 3 - 5 - 7 - 5 - 3 - 1 = \boldsymbol{-25}$.
>
> **(c)** Inverse DTFT at $n = 0$, $x[0] = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}X_d(\omega)\,d\omega$:
> $$
> \boldsymbol{\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}X_d(\omega)\,d\omega = 2\pi x[0] = -14\pi}
> $$
> Check: $X_d(\omega) = -7 + 10\cos\omega - 6\cos 2\omega + 2\cos 3\omega$ gives $-1$, $-25$, and integral $2\pi \cdot (-7)$.

> [!trap] Where points go
> - (c) is $2\pi$ times a sample; answering $-7$ drops the $2\pi$.

## Problem 4 · A linear phase on the DFT (5 pts)

> [!question] Problem 4
> Let $\{X[k]\}_{k=0}^{7}$ be the 8-point DFT of $x[n] = \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8\}$. Determine the sequence $\{y[n]\}_{n=0}^{7}$ whose DFT is $Y[k] = e^{-j\frac{\pi}{4}k}X[k]$, $k = 0, 1, \dots, 7$.

> [!success]- Solution 4
> $x[\langle n - m\rangle_N] \leftrightarrow e^{-j\frac{2\pi}{N}km}X[k]$; $e^{-j\frac{\pi}{4}k} = e^{-j\frac{2\pi}{8}k\cdot 1}$, so $m = 1$:
> $$
> \boldsymbol{y[n] = x[\langle n-1\rangle_8] = \{\underset{\uparrow}{8},\ 1,\ 2,\ 3,\ 4,\ 5,\ 6,\ 7\}}
> $$

> [!trap] Where points go
> - Shift is **circular**: a linear delay gives 9 samples $\{0, 1, \dots, 8\}$.

## Problem 5 · Inverse DTFT of a linear phase (8 pts)

> [!question] Problem 5
> Give a closed-form expression for $x[n]$, valid for all $n$, given that its DTFT is $X_d(\omega) = e^{-j\frac{\omega}{3}}$ for $\lvert\omega\rvert \le \pi$. Your answer should not include complex numbers.

> [!success]- Solution 5
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> x[n] &= \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}e^{-j\frac{\omega}{3}}e^{j\omega n}\,d\omega = \frac{1}{2\pi}\left[\frac{e^{j\omega(n-\frac13)}}{j(n-\frac13)}\right]_{-\pi}^{\pi}\\[4pt]
> &= \frac{1}{\pi(n-\frac13)}\cdot\frac{e^{j\pi(n-\frac13)} - e^{-j\pi(n-\frac13)}}{2j}
> \end{aligned}
> $$
> $$
> \boldsymbol{x[n] = \frac{\sin\left(\pi\left(n - \frac13\right)\right)}{\pi\left(n - \frac13\right)}}
> $$
> $n - \frac13 \ne 0$ for integer $n$. Equivalently $x[n] = (-1)^{n+1}\dfrac{3\sqrt3}{2\pi(3n-1)}$; $x[0] = \frac{3\sqrt3}{2\pi} \approx 0.83$. A fractional (⅓-sample) delay: the band-limited sinc sampled off its peak.

> [!trap] Where points go
> - $\delta[n - \frac13]$ is wrong: the delay property needs integer shifts.

## Problem 6 · Can the output be real? (5 pts)

> [!question] Problem 6
> A sequence $x[n]$ with DTFT $X_d(\omega) = e^{-j\frac{\omega^2}{3}}$ is input to an LSI system with unit-pulse response $h[n] = \left(\frac13\right)^n u[n]$. Can the output sequence $y[n]$ be real? Explain.

> [!success]- Solution 6
> **No.** Real $\iff$ $Y_d(-\omega) = Y_d^*(\omega)$.
>
> - $X_d(-\omega) = e^{-j\frac{\omega^2}{3}}$ but $X_d^*(\omega) = e^{+j\frac{\omega^2}{3}}$: differ wherever $\sin\frac{\omega^2}{3} \ne 0$, so $x[n]$ is complex.
> - $h$ real, $H_d(\omega) = \dfrac{1}{1 - \frac13 e^{-j\omega}}$, $H_d(-\omega) = H_d^*(\omega)$:
> $$
> Y_d(-\omega) - Y_d^*(\omega) = \left[X_d(-\omega) - X_d^*(\omega)\right]H_d^*(\omega).
> $$
> - $\lvert H_d(\omega)\rvert \ge \frac{1}{1 + 1/3} = \frac34 \ne 0$, so the bracket survives: $\boldsymbol{y[n]}$ **is complex**.

> [!warning] Key gap
> The key stops at "$x[n]$ is complex, therefore $y[n]$ cannot be real". That needs the third bullet: a filter that is zero where $x$'s non-symmetric part lives could return a real output.

## Problem 7 · Sinusoids through $1 + z^{-4}$ (10 pts)

> [!question] Problem 7
> Consider an LSI system characterized by the transfer function $H(z) = 1 + z^{-4}$. Compute the system response, $y[n]$, to the input
> $$
> x[n] = 3 + 4\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{4}n\right) + e^{j\frac{\pi}{2}n}.
> $$

> [!success]- Solution 7
> FIR, so $H_d(\omega) = H(e^{j\omega})$:
> $$
> H_d(\omega) = 1 + e^{-j4\omega} = e^{-j2\omega}\left(e^{j2\omega} + e^{-j2\omega}\right) = 2\cos(2\omega)\,e^{-j2\omega}.
> $$
> - $H_d(0) = 2$: $3 \mapsto 6$.
> - $H_d(\pm\frac{\pi}{4}) = 2\cos\frac{\pi}{2}\,e^{\mp j\pi/2} = 0$: cosine removed.
> - $H_d(\frac{\pi}{2}) = 2\cos(\pi)\,e^{-j\pi} = (-2)(-1) = 2$: $e^{j\frac{\pi}{2}n} \mapsto 2e^{j\frac{\pi}{2}n}$.
> $$
> \boldsymbol{y[n] = 6 + 2e^{j\frac{\pi}{2}n}}
> $$
> Check: $y[n] = x[n] + x[n-4]$; $\cos(\frac{\pi}{4}(n-4)) = -\cos(\frac{\pi}{4}n)$ cancels, $e^{j\frac{\pi}{2}(n-4)} = e^{j\frac{\pi}{2}n}$ doubles.

> [!trap] Where points go
> - $2\cos(2\omega)$ is an amplitude: $-2$ at $\frac{\pi}{2}$, times $e^{-j\pi} = -1$ gives $+2$.
> - The lone complex exponential stays $2e^{j\frac{\pi}{2}n}$, not a cosine.

## Problem 8 · D/A conversion of $\delta[n-3]$ (10 pts)

> [!question] Problem 8
> The discrete-time signal $x[n] = \delta[n-3]$ goes through a digital-to-analog (D/A) converter with interpolating interval $T$. Determine and sketch $x_a(t)$ for the following cases (label your graphs carefully):
>
> (a) The D/A is a ZOH
>
> (b) The D/A is an ideal D/A

> [!success]- Solution 8
> $x_a(t) = \sum_n x[n]\,p(t - nT)$; one sample $x[3] = 1$ gives one pulse at $t = 3T$. (Key crosses out (a): "ZOH is not on our Midterm 2!")
>
> **(a) ZOH**, $p(t) = 1$ for $0 \le t \lt T$:
> $$
> \boldsymbol{x_a(t) = \begin{cases}1, & 3T \le t \lt 4T\\ 0, & \text{otherwise}\end{cases}}
> $$
> **(b) Ideal D/A**, $p(t) = \dfrac{\sin(\pi t/T)}{\pi t/T}$:
> $$
> \boldsymbol{x_a(t) = \frac{\sin\left(\pi(t - 3T)/T\right)}{\pi(t-3T)/T}}
> $$
> Peak 1 at $t = 3T$, zero at every other multiple of $T$.

>
> | approach | $N$ | zeros added to $x$ | zeros added to $h$ |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | DFT | 22 | **7** | **14** |
> | radix-2 FFT | 32 (next power of 2) | **17** | **24** |

> [!trap] Where points go
> - $N \ge L_x + L_h - 1$, not $\max(L_x, L_h)$; same $N$ for both, so different numbers of zeros.
