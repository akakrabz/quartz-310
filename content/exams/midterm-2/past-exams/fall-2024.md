## Problem 1 · True/False with reasons (12 pts)

*Topic: DTFT existence and periodicity ([[3-fourier-analysis/14-dtft-properties|Lecture 14]]); ideal D/A and zero-padding: Lectures 17+ (not yet in these notes).*

> [!question] Problem 1
> Select **True** or **False** for each statement. Please explain your reasoning in **no more than two sentences**.
>
> (a) The z-transform of $x[n] = 2^nu[n]$ is given by $X(z) = \dfrac{1}{1-2z^{-1}}$, $\lvert z\rvert \gt 2$. Therefore, the DTFT of $x[n]$ is given by $X_d(\omega) = \dfrac{1}{1-2e^{-j\omega}}$.
>
> (b) The DTFT $X_d(\omega)$ of a finite-length signal $x[n]$ is $2\pi$-periodic.
>
> (c) A discrete-time signal $x[n] = 2\delta[n-5]$ is passed to an ideal D/A converter with sampling period $T$ to recover $x_a(t)$. The recovered signal is given by $x_a(t) = 2\delta(t - 5T)$.
>
> (d) A length-$N$ signal $x[n]$ with DTFT $X_d(\omega)$ and DFT $X[k]$ is zero-padded with $N$ zeros to length $2N$ to form $y[n]$. Then, $X[k] = Y[2k]$ and $X_d(\omega) = Y_d(\omega)$.

> [!success]- Solution 1
> - **(a) False.** The ROC $\lvert z\rvert \gt 2$ does not contain the unit circle: $2^nu[n]$ is not absolutely summable, so its DTFT does not exist. Substituting $z = e^{j\omega}$ is allowed only when the ROC contains $\lvert z\rvert = 1$.
> - **(b) True.** Every DTFT that exists is $2\pi$-periodic, since $e^{-j(\omega+2\pi)n} = e^{-j\omega n}$ for integer $n$ ([[3-fourier-analysis/14-dtft-properties|Lecture 14]]). Finite length only guarantees that it exists.
> - **(c) False.** An ideal D/A interpolates with sincs: $x_a(t) = 2\,\dfrac{\sin\big(\pi(t-5T)/T\big)}{\pi(t-5T)/T}$, which the key writes as $2\,\mathrm{sinc}\big(\frac{\pi}{T}(t-5T)\big)$ with $\mathrm{sinc}\,x = \frac{\sin x}{x}$. It is 2 at $t = 5T$, 0 at the other sample instants and nonzero in between ($x_a(5.5T) = \frac{4}{\pi}$).
> - **(d) True.** Padding does not change the DTFT sum, so $Y_d = X_d$, and the $2N$-point DFT samples it twice as densely: $Y[2k] = Y_d\big(\frac{2\pi\cdot2k}{2N}\big) = X_d\big(\frac{2\pi k}{N}\big) = X[k]$.

> [!trap] Where points go
> - **(c)** The same statement with $4\delta[n-2]$ is [[exams/midterm-2/past-exams/fall-2023|FA2023 MT2]] #1(e); [[exams/midterm-2/past-exams/spring-2025|SP2025 MT2]] #1(c) asks for a value of the interpolated signal. Mind the two sinc conventions (this key: $\frac{\sin x}{x}$; the course tables: $\frac{\sin\pi x}{\pi x}$). Writing the ratio out avoids the issue.
> - **(d)** Both halves need a reason: "the DFT gets twice as many points" says nothing about the DTFT.

> [!success]- Solution 2
> **Method 1 (integrate over $[-\pi, \pi]$).** Two bands:
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> x[n] &= \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{-\pi/2} e^{j\omega n}d\omega + \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\pi/2}^{\pi} e^{j\omega n}d\omega
> = \frac{e^{-j\frac{\pi}{2}n} - e^{-j\pi n} + e^{j\pi n} - e^{j\frac{\pi}{2}n}}{2\pi jn}\\
> &= \frac{\sin(\pi n)}{\pi n} - \frac{\sin(\frac{\pi}{2}n)}{\pi n}
> = \boldsymbol{\delta[n] - \frac{\sin(\frac{\pi}{2}n)}{\pi n}}
> \end{aligned}
> $$
> "All-pass minus low-pass": for integer $n$, $\frac{\sin(\pi n)}{\pi n} = \delta[n]$.
>
> **Method 2 (integrate over one period, $[0, 2\pi]$).** The two bands are one band $[\frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{3\pi}{2}]$ centred at $\pi$:
> $$
> x[n] = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\pi/2}^{3\pi/2} e^{j\omega n}d\omega = e^{j\pi n}\,\frac{\sin(\frac{\pi}{2}n)}{\pi n} = \boldsymbol{(-1)^n\,\frac{\sin(\frac{\pi}{2}n)}{\pi n}}
> $$
> — modulation by $e^{j\pi n} = (-1)^n$ moves a low-pass of half-width $\frac{\pi}{2}$ to $\pi$.
>
> **Method 3 (two shifted copies of a narrower low-pass).** With $Y_d$ the ideal low-pass of half-width $\frac{\pi}{4}$, $y[n] = \frac{\sin(\frac{\pi}{4}n)}{\pi n}$, and $X_d(\omega) = Y_d(\omega - \frac{3\pi}{4}) + Y_d(\omega + \frac{3\pi}{4})$:
> $$
> x[n] = \boldsymbol{2\cos\left(\tfrac{3\pi}{4}n\right)\frac{\sin(\frac{\pi}{4}n)}{\pi n}}
> $$
> All three are the same real sequence: $x[0] = \frac12$ (the band covers half the period), $x[\pm1] = -\frac{1}{\pi}$, $x[\pm2] = 0$, $x[\pm3] = \frac{1}{3\pi}$. The key writes them with $\mathrm{sinc}\,x = \frac{\sin x}{x}$, e.g. $\delta[n] - \frac12\mathrm{sinc}(\frac{\pi}{2}n)$, and accepts any of them (checked: all three agree with a numerical inverse DTFT).

> [!trap] Where points go
> - "Real-valued" is part of the grade: stopping at $\frac{e^{-j\frac{\pi}{2}n} - e^{-j\pi n} + e^{j\pi n} - e^{j\frac{\pi}{2}n}}{2\pi jn}$, or pairing the exponentials so that no $\sin$ appears, loses it.
> - $n = 0$: every form is $\frac00$ there. The value is the average of $X_d$ over one period, $\frac12$.

## Problem 3 · A symmetric 3-tap FIR (12 pts)

*Topic: frequency response and magnitude response — [[3-fourier-analysis/15-frequency-response|Lecture 15]], [[3-fourier-analysis/16-magnitude-and-phase-response|Lecture 16]] · [[problems/magnitude-phase-and-group-delay|magnitude, phase and group delay]].*

> [!question] Problem 3
> Suppose we have an LTI system with impulse response $h[n] = \delta[n+2] + \delta[n] + \delta[n-2]$.
>
> (a) Find the frequency response $H_d(\omega)$ of this system at $\omega = 0$, $\omega = \frac{\pi}{2}$ and $\omega = \pi$.
>
> (b) Plot the magnitude response $\lvert H_d(\omega)\rvert$ for $-\pi \le \omega \le \pi$. Make sure to carefully label your plot.

> \boldsymbol{H_d(0) = 3,\qquad H_d\left(\tfrac{\pi}{2}\right) = 1 + 2\cos\pi = -1,\qquad H_d(\pi) = 1 + 2\cos2\pi = 3}

## Problem 4 · Is $h[n]$ real? Then sinusoids in, sinusoids out (12 pts)

*Topic: Hermitian symmetry and the LTI response to sinusoids — [[3-fourier-analysis/14-dtft-properties|Lecture 14]], [[3-fourier-analysis/15-frequency-response|Lecture 15]] · [[problems/lti-response-to-sinusoids|LTI response to sinusoids]].*

> [!question] Problem 4
> The frequency response of an LTI system is given by $H_d(\omega) = \lvert\omega\rvert\,e^{-j\pi\sin(\omega)}$ for $\lvert\omega\rvert \le \pi$.
>
> (a) Is the impulse response $h[n]$ of this system real-valued? Justify your reasoning.
>
> (b) Determine the output $y[n]$ when the input is $x[n] = 3 + \cos\left(\frac{\pi n}{6}\right) + j^n$.

> [!success]- Solution 4
> **(a) Yes.** $h[n]$ is real exactly when $H_d(-\omega) = H_d^*(\omega)$:
> $$
> H_d(-\omega) = \lvert-\omega\rvert\,e^{-j\pi\sin(-\omega)} = \lvert\omega\rvert\,e^{j\pi\sin\omega} = H_d^*(\omega)
> $$
> (even magnitude $\lvert\omega\rvert$, odd phase $-\pi\sin\omega$).
>
> **(b)** Evaluate at the three input frequencies, with $j^n = e^{j\frac{\pi}{2}n}$:
> $$
> H_d(0) = 0,\qquad H_d\left(\tfrac{\pi}{6}\right) = \tfrac{\pi}{6}e^{-j\pi/2},\qquad H_d\left(\tfrac{\pi}{2}\right) = \tfrac{\pi}{2}e^{-j\pi} = -\tfrac{\pi}{2}.
> $$
> The constant is blocked. Because $h$ is real (part a), the cosine keeps its frequency, is scaled by $\lvert H_d\rvert$ and shifted by $\angle H_d$. The exponential is multiplied by $H_d(\frac{\pi}{2})$:
> $$
> \boldsymbol{y[n] = \frac{\pi}{6}\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{6}n - \frac{\pi}{2}\right) - \frac{\pi}{2}\,j^n = \frac{\pi}{6}\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{6}n\right) - \frac{\pi}{2}\,e^{j\frac{\pi}{2}n}}
> $$
> (checked: convolving the input with $h[n]$ from a numerical inverse DTFT gives the same samples)

> [!trap] Where points go

## Problem 6 · Which analog frequencies does bin $k$ see? (8 pts)

*Topic: spectral analysis with the DFT, sampling — Lectures 17+ (not yet in these notes).*

> [!question] Problem 6
> Suppose that we sample a continuous-domain signal $x_c(t)$ (which is not necessarily band-limited) with sampling frequency $f_s = 1000$ Hz to produce $x[n] = x_c(n/f_s)$. Then $N = 1024$ samples $\{x[n]\}_{n=0}^{N-1}$ are extracted as input to the length-$N$ DFT to produce the output $\{X[k]\}_{k=0}^{N-1}$. Which continuous-domain frequencies of $x_c(t)$ in Hz are captured in the DFT output $X[k]$ for a given index $k$? Clearly justify your answer. Note that due to possible aliasing, multiple continuous-domain frequencies $\Omega$ can map to the same discrete-domain frequency $\omega \in [0, 2\pi]$.

> [!success]- Solution 6
> Bin $k$ samples the DTFT at $\omega_k = \frac{2\pi k}{N}$, and the DTFT is $2\pi$-periodic, so the bin is shared by every $\omega = \frac{2\pi k}{N} + 2\pi m$. Sampling maps $\Omega \mapsto \omega = \Omega T = \frac{2\pi f}{f_s}$:
> $$
> \frac{2\pi f}{f_s} = \frac{2\pi(k + mN)}{N}\iff \boldsymbol{f_k = \frac{1000\,k}{1024} + 1000\,m\ \text{Hz},\qquad m \in \mathbb{Z}}
> $$
> For example, bin $k = 3$ sees $2.93$ Hz, $1002.93$ Hz, $-997.07$ Hz, … (checked: a complex exponential at each such frequency puts all of its energy in bin $k$).

> [!trap] Where points go
> - Negative $m$ gives negative frequencies. For a real signal, a cosine at $1000m - \frac{1000k}{1024}$ Hz also reaches bin $k$ through its negative-frequency half. Answering only $\frac{1000k}{1024}$ Hz ignores the aliasing the question asks about.
> - The formula lists the frequencies that fall **exactly** on bin $k$; a frequency between bins leaks into all of them ([[exams/midterm-2/past-exams/fall-2023|FA2023 MT2]] #6).

## Problem 7 · The smallest $N$ that hits two frequencies (6 pts)

*Topic: the DFT as samples of the DTFT — Lectures 17+ (not yet in these notes).*

> [!question] Problem 7
> Suppose we have a length-$N$ discrete-time signal $x[n]$ with DTFT $X_d(\omega)$ and DFT $X[k]$. We know that $X[k]$ for this particular $x[n]$ is able to capture the values $X_d\left(\frac{2\pi}{5}\right)$ and $X_d\left(\frac{\pi}{3}\right)$. Determine the smallest possible value of $N$.

> [!success]- Solution 7
> The DFT holds the values $X_d\big(\frac{2\pi k}{N}\big)$. $\frac{2\pi}{5} = \frac{2\pi k}{N}$ needs $k = \frac{N}{5}$ to be an integer, and $\frac{\pi}{3} = \frac{2\pi k}{N}$ needs $k = \frac{N}{6}$ to be an integer. So $N$ is a common multiple of 5 and 6:
> $$
> \boldsymbol{N = \mathrm{lcm}(5, 6) = 30}\qquad(\text{bins } k = 6 \text{ and } k = 5)
> $$

> [!trap] Where points go
> - $\frac{\pi}{3} = \frac{2\pi}{6}$ needs a multiple of **6**, not of 3: $N = 15$ fails, since $\frac{2\pi k}{15} = \frac{\pi}{3}$ gives $k = 2.5$.

## Problem 8 · Circular reversal plus shift (8 pts)

*Topic: DFT properties (circular time reversal and circular shift) — Lectures 17+ (not yet in these notes).*

> [!question] Problem 8
> A length-5 signal $\{x[n]\}_{n=0}^{4} = \{3,\ 0,\ -1,\ 2,\ 4\}$ has DFT $\{X[k]\}_{k=0}^{4} = \{X_0, X_1, X_2, X_3, X_4\}$. Consider the signal $\{y[n]\}_{n=0}^{4} = \{0,\ 3,\ 4,\ 2,\ -1\}$. Determine $\{Y[k]\}_{k=0}^{4}$ in terms of $X_0, X_1, X_2, X_3, X_4$.

> [!success]- Solution 8
> Spot the pattern: reverse $x$ circularly, $x[\langle -n\rangle_5] = \{3,\ 4,\ 2,\ -1,\ 0\}$, then shift it circularly right by one: $\{0,\ 3,\ 4,\ 2,\ -1\} = y$. So $y[n] = x[\langle -(n-1)\rangle_5]$. Reversal sends $X[k] \to X[\langle -k\rangle_5]$, and a circular delay by 1 multiplies by $e^{-j2\pi k/5}$:
> $$
> Y[k] = X[\langle -k\rangle_5]\,e^{-j\frac{2\pi k}{5}}\quad\Rightarrow\quad \boldsymbol{Y = \left\{X_0,\ X_4e^{-j\frac{2\pi}{5}},\ X_3e^{-j\frac{4\pi}{5}},\ X_2e^{-j\frac{6\pi}{5}},\ X_1e^{-j\frac{8\pi}{5}}\right\}}
> $$
> Other orders (shift left by 1, then reverse; or write the delay as a left shift by 4) give the same $Y[k]$ (checked: `np.fft.fft(y)` against the formula).

> [!trap] Where points go
> - Circular reversal keeps $x[0]$ in place: $x[\langle -n\rangle_5] = \{x_0, x_4, x_3, x_2, x_1\}$, not the plain flip $\{x_4, x_3, x_2, x_1, x_0\}$.
> - The sign of the exponent: a **delay** by $m$ is $e^{-j2\pi km/N}$, an advance is $e^{+j2\pi km/N}$. [[exams/midterm-2/past-exams/spring-2025|SP2025 MT2]] #8 runs the same property backwards.
