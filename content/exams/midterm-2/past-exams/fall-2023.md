## Problem 1 · True/False (16 pts)

> [!question] Problem 1
> Select **True** or **False** for each statement.
>
> (a) A discrete-time signal $x[n]$ with z-transform $X(z)$ always has a finite DTFT $X_d(\omega)$ given by $X(z)$ evaluated at $z = e^{j\omega}$, i.e. evaluate $X(z)$ along the unit circle.
>
> (b) Let $x[n]$ be a discrete-time signal with DTFT $X_d(\omega) = e^{-j\omega/2}$, $-\pi \le \omega \le \pi$. The corresponding signal is $x[n] = \delta\big[n - \tfrac12\big]$.
>
> (c) The DTFT of a real-valued signal is always Hermitian symmetric.
>
> (d) Every continuous-time signal has a minimum sampling frequency to avoid aliasing during analog-to-digital conversion.
>
> (e) A discrete-time signal is given by $x[n] = 4\delta[n-2]$. We pass $x[n]$ to an ideal D/A converter with sampling period $T$ to recover $x_a(t)$. The recovered continuous-time signal is $x_a(t) = 4\delta(n - 2T)$.
>
> (f) Let $\{x[n]\}_{n=0}^{N-1}$ be a length-$N$ signal with DTFT $X_d(\omega)$ and $\{y[n]\}_{n=0}^{M+N-1}$ be $x[n]$ zero-padded with $M$ zeros. The DTFT of $y[n]$, $Y_d(\omega)$, is equal to $X_d(\omega)$.
>
> (g) The DFT of $\{x[n]\}_{n=0}^{23} = \cos\left(\tfrac{\pi}{8}n\right)$ will only have two non-zero values.
>
> (h) The DTFT and DFT of a length-14 sequence $x[n]$ are given by $X_d(\omega)$ and $X[k]$, respectively. We know then that $X[11] = X_d\left(-\tfrac{3\pi}{7}\right)$.

> [!success]- Solution 1
> - **(a) False.** Needs the ROC to contain the unit circle. $x[n] = 2^nu[n]$: $X(z) = \frac{1}{1-2z^{-1}}$, $\lvert z\rvert \gt 2$, no DTFT ($\sum\lvert x[n]\rvert = \infty$).
> - **(b) False.** $n$ is an integer. Inverse DTFT: $x[n] = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}e^{j\omega(n-\frac12)}d\omega = \dfrac{\sin\big(\pi(n-\frac12)\big)}{\pi(n-\frac12)}$, $x[0] = x[1] = \frac{2}{\pi}$.
> - **(c) True.** Real $x[n]$: $X_d(-\omega) = X_d^*(\omega)$.
> - **(d) False.** Only a **band-limited** signal has a Nyquist rate; e.g. a rectangular pulse aliases at every rate.
> - **(e) False.** One sample → one sinc: $x_a(t) = 4\,\dfrac{\sin\big(\pi(t-2T)/T\big)}{\pi(t-2T)/T}$.
> - **(f) True.** Appended zeros add nothing to $\sum_n x[n]e^{-j\omega n}$.
> - **(g) False.** Period 16; 24 samples hold 1.5 periods ($k = 1.5$ needed): leakage, all 24 values nonzero.
> - **(h) True.** $X[11] = X_d\big(\frac{22\pi}{14}\big) = X_d\big(\frac{11\pi}{7}\big) = X_d\big(\frac{11\pi}{7} - 2\pi\big) = X_d\big(-\frac{3\pi}{7}\big)$.

> [!trap] Where points go
> - (a): "has a z-transform" ≠ "has a DTFT" (same as [[exams/midterm-2/past-exams/fall-2024|FA2024 MT2]] #1(a)).
> - (g): two nonzero bins only for a whole number of periods ([[exams/midterm-2/past-exams/spring-2025|SP2025 MT2]] #9).

## Problem 2 · $X_d(0)$ and $X_d(\pi)$ without the transform (6 pts)

> [!question] Problem 2
> Suppose that $x[n] = u[n+2] - u[n-3]$, where $u[n]$ is the step sequence. Calculate $X_d(0)$ and $X_d(\pi)$.

> [!success]- Solution 2
> $x[n] = \{1,\ 1,\ \underset{\uparrow}{1},\ 1,\ 1\}$:
> $$
> X_d(0) = \sum_n x[n] = \boldsymbol{5},\qquad X_d(\pi) = \sum_n (-1)^nx[n] = 1 - 1 + 1 - 1 + 1 = \boldsymbol{1}.
> $$
> Check: $X_d(\omega) = \dfrac{\sin(5\omega/2)}{\sin(\omega/2)}$ → 5 at 0, $\frac{\sin(5\pi/2)}{\sin(\pi/2)} = 1$ at $\pi$.

## Problem 3 · Sampling a sine: which $\Omega_0$? (6 pts)

> [!question] Problem 3
> Suppose we sample $x_a(t) = \sin(\Omega_0t)$ at sampling frequency $f_s = 400$ Hz to obtain $x[n] = \sin\left(\frac{\pi}{2}n\right)$. Which of the following are possible values of $\Omega_0$? (Circle all that apply.)
>
> (a) $-600\pi$ · (b) $-400\pi$ · (c) $200\pi$ · (d) $400\pi$ · (e) $800\pi$ · (f) $1200\pi$

> [!success]- Solution 3
> $x[n] = \sin\left(\frac{\Omega_0}{400}n\right)$; need $\frac{\Omega_0}{400} = \frac{\pi}{2} + 2\pi k$:
> $$
> \Omega_0 = 200\pi + 800\pi k = \dots,\ -600\pi,\ 200\pi,\ 1000\pi,\ \dots\quad\Rightarrow\quad\textbf{(a) and (c)}
> $$
> Others give $\frac{\Omega_0}{400} \in \{-\pi, \pi, 2\pi, 3\pi\}$: all samples 0.

> [!trap] Where points go
> - (a): $-600\pi/400 = -\frac{3\pi}{2} \equiv \frac{\pi}{2}$ works; $-200\pi$ would give $-\sin(\frac{\pi}{2}n)$ (sign matters for a sine).

## Problem 4 · A frequency response with an even phase (18 pts)

> [!question] Problem 4
> Consider an LTI system with frequency response
> $$
> H_d(\omega) = \left(\frac12 + \cos\omega\right)e^{j\lvert\omega\rvert},\qquad -\pi \le \omega \le \pi .
> $$
> (a) Plot the magnitude response $\lvert H_d(\omega)\rvert$ and phase response $\angle H_d(\omega)$. Label your axes carefully.
>
> (b) Compute the output of this system to the input signal $x[n] = 4j - 2e^{j\left(\frac{2\pi}{3}n + \frac{\pi}{4}\right)} + 3\sin\left(\frac{3\pi}{4}n\right)$.

> [!success]- Solution 4(b)
> Even phase ⇒ $h[n]$ **complex**; no real-sinusoid shortcut. Use $e^{j\omega_0n} \to H_d(\omega_0)e^{j\omega_0n}$:
> $$
> H_d(0) = \tfrac32,\qquad H_d\left(\tfrac{2\pi}{3}\right) = 0,\qquad H_d\left(\pm\tfrac{3\pi}{4}\right) = \left(\tfrac12 - \tfrac{\sqrt2}{2}\right)e^{j3\pi/4} = \tfrac{\sqrt2 - 1}{2}\,e^{-j\pi/4}.
> $$
> - $4j \to 4j\cdot\frac32 = 6j$.
> - Exponential at $\frac{2\pi}{3}$: removed.
> - $3\sin(\frac{3\pi}{4}n) = \frac{3}{2j}\big(e^{j\frac{3\pi}{4}n} - e^{-j\frac{3\pi}{4}n}\big)$; both see $\frac{\sqrt2-1}{2}e^{-j\pi/4}$ ($H_d$ even).
> $$
> \boldsymbol{y[n] = 6j + \frac{3(\sqrt2-1)}{4j}\,e^{j\left(\frac{3\pi}{4}n - \frac{\pi}{4}\right)} - \frac{3(\sqrt2-1)}{4j}\,e^{-j\left(\frac{3\pi}{4}n + \frac{\pi}{4}\right)}}
> $$
> Key's form: $y[n] = 6j + \frac{3(\sqrt2-1)}{4}e^{j(\frac{3\pi}{4}n - \frac{3\pi}{4})} - \frac{3(\sqrt2-1)}{4}e^{-j(\frac{3\pi}{4}n + \frac{3\pi}{4})}$. Compact:
> $$
> y[n] = 6j + \frac{3(\sqrt2-1)}{2}\,e^{-j\pi/4}\,\sin\left(\frac{3\pi}{4}n\right).
> $$

> [!trap] Where points go
> - (a): negative lobes of $A(\omega)$ are a $\pi$ phase jump, not negative magnitude.
> - (b): the real-sinusoid rule needs Hermitian $H_d$; it gives a different, real answer. $4j$ is a constant, not $j^n$.

> (b) Now suppose we want to **high-pass** filter $x_c(t)$ by passing only the frequencies above 5 kHz in $x_c(t)$ into $y_c(t)$. Find the **largest** sampling period $T$ and the **corresponding** $H_d(\omega)$ for which the above system can perform the desired high-pass filtering operation.

> [!success]- Solution 5
> $X_d(\omega) = \frac1T\sum_k X_c\big(\frac{\omega - 2\pi k}{T}\big)$; $f$ Hz lands at $\omega = 2\pi fT$.
>
> **(a)** Only $\lvert f\rvert \le 5$ kHz must stay un-aliased. Passband edge $2\pi\cdot5000\,T$ ≤ start of copy at $2\pi$, $2\pi - 2\pi\cdot10^4\,T$:
> $$
> 2\pi\cdot5000\,T \le 2\pi - 2\pi\cdot10^4\,T \iff T \le \frac{1}{15000}\ \text{s} \approx 66.67\ \mu\text{s},
> $$
> so $\boldsymbol{T = \frac{1}{15000}}$ s (15 kHz, below the 20 kHz Nyquist rate), $\omega_c = 2\pi\cdot5000\cdot\frac{1}{15000} = \frac{2\pi}{3}$:
> $$
> \boldsymbol{H_d(\omega) = \begin{cases}1, & \lvert\omega\rvert \le \frac{2\pi}{3}\\ 0, & \frac{2\pi}{3} \lt \lvert\omega\rvert \le \pi\end{cases}}
> $$
> **(b)** The kept band touches $\pi$, where aliasing starts, and the D/C must rebuild up to 10 kHz: Nyquist, $\boldsymbol{T = \frac{1}{20000}}$ s $= 50\ \mu$s. 5 kHz → $\omega = 2\pi\cdot5000\cdot\frac{1}{20000} = \frac{\pi}{2}$:
> $$
> \boldsymbol{H_d(\omega) = \begin{cases}0, & \lvert\omega\rvert \le \frac{\pi}{2}\\ 1, & \frac{\pi}{2} \lt \lvert\omega\rvert \le \pi\end{cases}}
> $$

## Problem 7 · The DFT conjugation property (18 pts)

> [!question] Problem 7
> The three parts of this problem are related.
>
> (a) Let $\{b[n]\}_{n=0}^{N-1}$ be a length-$N$ **complex-valued** sequence and let $\{B[k]\}_{k=0}^{N-1}$ be the corresponding DFT of $b[n]$. The conjugation property of the DFT states
> $$
> \{b^*[n]\}_{n=0}^{N-1} \overset{\text{DFT}}{\longleftrightarrow} \{B^*[N-k]\}_{k=0}^{N-1}.
> $$
> Prove that the conjugation property holds. *Hint:* use the definition of the DFT, apply conjugation, and replace the integer $k$.
>
> (b) Specify the value $B^*[N]$ in terms of the original DFT $\{B[k]\}_{k=0}^{N-1}$.
>
> (c) We now use the conjugation property to compute the DFTs of two real-valued signals from a single DFT of a complex-valued signal. Let $\{x[n]\}_{n=0}^{N-1}$ and $\{y[n]\}_{n=0}^{N-1}$ be two real-valued sequences with DFTs $X[k]$ and $Y[k]$. Let $z[n] = x[n] + jy[n]$ with DFT $Z[k]$. Given that
> $$
> x[n] = \tfrac12\big(z[n] + z^*[n]\big),\qquad y[n] = \tfrac{1}{2j}\big(z[n] - z^*[n]\big),
> $$
> use the linearity of the DFT and the property in part (a) to write $X[k]$ and $Y[k]$ in terms of the single DFT $Z[k]$.

> [!success]- Solution 7
> **(a)** Pull the conjugate out, multiply by $e^{-j\frac{2\pi N}{N}n} = e^{-j2\pi n} = 1$:
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> \mathrm{DFT}\{b^*[n]\}[k] &= \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} b^*[n]\,e^{-j\frac{2\pi k}{N}n} = \Big(\sum_{n=0}^{N-1} b[n]\,e^{j\frac{2\pi k}{N}n}\Big)^*\\
> &= \Big(\sum_{n=0}^{N-1} b[n]\,e^{j\frac{2\pi k}{N}n}\,e^{-j\frac{2\pi N}{N}n}\Big)^*\\
> &= \Big(\sum_{n=0}^{N-1} b[n]\,e^{-j\frac{2\pi(N-k)}{N}n}\Big)^* = B^*[N-k].
> \end{aligned}
> $$
> **(b)** $N$-periodic in $k$: $\boldsymbol{B^*[N] = B^*[0]}$.
>
> **(c)** $\mathrm{DFT}\{z^*[n]\} = Z^*[N-k]$, so
> $$
> \boldsymbol{X[k] = \tfrac12\big(Z[k] + Z^*[N-k]\big),\qquad Y[k] = \tfrac{1}{2j}\big(Z[k] - Z^*[N-k]\big)}
> $$
> with $Z^*[N]$ read as $Z^*[0]$.

> [!trap] Where points go
> - (c): $Y[k]$ carries $\frac{1}{2j}$; $X[k] \ne \mathrm{Re}\{Z[k]\}$ ($X[k]$ is complex).
