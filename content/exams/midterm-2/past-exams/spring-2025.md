## Problem 1 · True/False, +2/−1/0 (12 pts)

*Topic: inverse DTFT ([[3-fourier-analysis/13-fourier-analysis-and-the-dtft|Lecture 13]]); inverse DFT, ideal D/A, zero-padding, DFT of a sampled cosine, windows: Lectures 17+ (not yet in these notes).*

> [!question] Problem 1
> Mark **True** or **False** for the following statements. *Grading: correct answer +2, incorrect answer −1, no answer 0.*
>
> (a) If $x[n]$ is the inverse DTFT of $X_d(\omega) = 1 + 2e^{-j\omega} + 3e^{-j2\omega} + 4e^{-j3\omega}$, then $x[n]$ must be zero for $n \lt 0$ and $n \gt 3$.
>
> (b) If $x[n]$ is the inverse DFT of $X[k] = \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$, $0 \le k \le 3$, then $x[n]$ must be zero for $n \lt 0$ and $n \gt 3$.
>
> (c) Let $x[n] = \delta[n-2] + 2\delta[n-4]$. We recover $x_a(t)$ by performing ideal digital-to-analog conversion with sampling period $T = 1$ s. Then $x_a(t) = 0$ at $t = 3$ s.
>
> (d) Let $x[n]$ be a length-$N$ signal with DTFT $X_d(\omega)$. We zero-pad $x[n]$ to length $3N$ to obtain $y[n]$ with DTFT $Y_d(\omega)$. We may say that $X_d(\omega) = Y_d(3\omega)$.
>
> (e) An analog signal $x_a(t) = \cos(50\pi t)$ is sampled at $f_s = 50$ Hz for 1 second to obtain $\{x[n]\}_{n=0}^{49}$. The DFT of $x[n]$, $X[k]$, is non-zero for only one value of $k$ for $0 \le k \le 49$.
>
> (f) Applying the Hamming window for spectral analysis leads to lower sidelobes and narrower main lobes in spectral components.

> [!success]- Solution 1
> - **(a) True.** The DTFT is unique, and this $X_d$ is the DTFT of $\{\underset{\uparrow}{1},\ 2,\ 3,\ 4\}$, read off term by term with $\delta[n-n_0] \leftrightarrow e^{-j\omega n_0}$.
> - **(b) False.** The inverse-DFT formula $x[n] = \frac1N\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}X[k]e^{j\frac{2\pi k}{N}n}$ is $N$-periodic in $n$; evaluated outside $0 \le n \le 3$ it repeats ($x[4] = x[0] = \frac52$). A DFT pair describes one period of a periodic sequence.
> - **(c) True.** $x_a(t) = \mathrm{sinc}(t-2) + 2\,\mathrm{sinc}(t-4)$ with $\mathrm{sinc}\,u = \frac{\sin\pi u}{\pi u}$. At $t = 3$ both sincs sit on a zero ($\sin(\pm\pi) = 0$): ideal interpolation passes through every sample, and $x[3] = 0$.
> - **(d) False.** Zero-padding leaves the DTFT unchanged: $Y_d(\omega) = X_d(\omega)$. (Inserting two zeros **between** samples, i.e. upsampling, would give $Y_d(\omega) = X_d(3\omega)$; appending zeros at the end does nothing to the DTFT.)
> - **(e) True.** $x[n] = \cos(50\pi n/50) = \cos(\pi n) = (-1)^n = e^{j\frac{2\pi\cdot25}{50}n}$: a single exponential exactly on bin 25, so $X[25] = 50$ and the other 49 bins are 0. (The $\pm25$ Hz halves of the cosine alias onto the same bin.)
> - **(f) False.** The Hamming window trades: much lower sidelobes (about $-42$ dB against $-13$ dB for the rectangular window) for a main lobe about **twice as wide** (checked: 64-point windows, zero-padded FFTs).

> [!trap] Where points go
> - **The −1:** a blank is worth 0 and a pure 50/50 guess averages $+0.5$, so guessing is not penalized on average; the −1 bites on confident mistakes like (b) and (f).
> - **(a) vs (b):** the same four numbers, but a DFT lives on a circle. The inverse DTFT of a finite sum is finite; the inverse DFT is periodic.
> - **(c)** is about the interpolated signal **between** samples: the zero at $t = 3$ comes from each sinc vanishing at every integer other than its own centre.

## Problem 4 · Four inputs, and the trouble at $\omega = \pi$ (10 pts)

*Topic: Hermitian symmetry and the LTI response to exponentials and sinusoids — [[3-fourier-analysis/14-dtft-properties|Lecture 14]], [[3-fourier-analysis/15-frequency-response|Lecture 15]] · [[problems/lti-response-to-sinusoids|LTI response to sinusoids]].*

> [!question] Problem 4
> The frequency response of an LSI system is
> $$
> H_d(\omega) = j\omega\,e^{j\pi\sin\omega},\qquad \lvert\omega\rvert \le \pi .
> $$
> Compute the response $y[n]$ of this system to the input signal
> $$
> x[n] = 5 + 2e^{j\frac{\pi}{4}n} + \sin\left(\frac{\pi}{2}n + \frac{\pi}{4}\right) + (-1)^n .
> $$

> [!success]- Solution 4 (official answer)
> **Is $h[n]$ real?** $H_d^*(\omega) = -j\omega\,e^{-j\pi\sin\omega}$ and $H_d(-\omega) = -j\omega\,e^{-j\pi\sin\omega}$ agree, so $h[n]$ is real and the real-sinusoid rule applies to the sine.
>
> Values: $H_d(0) = 0$; $H_d(\frac{\pi}{4}) = j\frac{\pi}{4}e^{j\pi\frac{\sqrt2}{2}} = \frac{\pi}{4}e^{j\frac{\pi}{2}(1+\sqrt2)}$; $H_d(\frac{\pi}{2}) = j\frac{\pi}{2}e^{j\pi} = \frac{\pi}{2}e^{-j\pi/2}$; and for $(-1)^n = e^{j\pi n}$ the key uses $H_d(\pi) = j\pi\,e^{j\pi\sin\pi} = j\pi$. The constant is blocked by $H_d(0) = 0$:
> $$
> \boldsymbol{y[n] = \frac{\pi}{2}e^{j\left(\frac{\pi}{4}n + \frac{\pi}{2}(1+\sqrt2)\right)} + \frac{\pi}{2}\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{2}n - \frac{\pi}{4}\right) + \pi e^{j\left(\pi n + \frac{\pi}{2}\right)}}
> $$
> The key also writes the first term as $\frac{\pi}{2}\,j\,e^{j(\frac{\pi}{4}n + \frac{\pi\sqrt2}{2})}$ and the last as $j\pi(-1)^n$ (checked: eigenfunction evaluation, and the first three terms against a convolution with a numerically inverted $h[n]$). The last term is the doubtful one: see the caveat below.

> [!warning] Answer-key caveat — #4, the $(-1)^n$ term
> The given $H_d$ is **discontinuous at $\omega = \pm\pi$**: $H_d(\pi) = j\pi$ but $H_d(-\pi) = -j\pi$, while a DTFT must have $H_d(-\pi) = H_d(\pi)$. Since $(-1)^n = e^{j\pi n} = e^{-j\pi n}$, the two ends give opposite answers, $\pm j\pi(-1)^n$. Both clash with the Hermitian check: $h[n]$ is real and $(-1)^n = \cos(\pi n)$ is real, so this part of the output must be real, yet $j\pi(-1)^n$ is purely imaginary. The real-sinusoid rule gives $\pi\cos(\pi n + \frac{\pi}{2}) = -\pi\sin(\pi n) = 0$; so does splitting $\cos(\pi n) = \frac12e^{j\pi n} + \frac12e^{-j\pi n}$ and giving each half its own end, $\frac12(j\pi) + \frac12(-j\pi) = 0$. That is what the actual system does: the real $h[n]$ obtained by inverting $H_d$ decays only like $\frac{1}{n}$ (the price of the jump), and the symmetric partial sums of $\sum_k (-1)^k h[k]$ are real and tend to 0, the midpoint of $j\pi$ and $-j\pi$ (checked with $h[n]$ in closed form: $-0.006$, $-0.0006$, $-0.00006$ for $\lvert k\rvert \le K = 10^3, 10^4, 10^5$, about $-\frac{2\pi}{K}$). Expect the key's $j\pi(-1)^n$ to be graded as the answer; if you write 0, say why in one line.

> [!trap] Where points go
> - Splitting $\sin(\frac{\pi}{2}n + \frac{\pi}{4})$ into exponentials is fine, but then $H_d(-\frac{\pi}{2}) = -j\frac{\pi}{2}e^{-j\pi} = j\frac{\pi}{2}$, the conjugate of $H_d(\frac{\pi}{2})$, as Hermitian symmetry promises; evaluating it as $-j\frac{\pi}{2}$ again is the usual sign slip.
> - $2e^{j\frac{\pi}{4}n}$ is a single exponential: multiply by $H_d(\frac{\pi}{4})$ and keep the factor 2 (giving $\frac{\pi}{2}$, not $\frac{\pi}{4}$).

> (a) Let $x_a(t) = \sin(100\pi t)$ and $x[n] = \sin\left(\frac{\pi}{2}n\right)$. Which of the following sampling periods $T$ are consistent with this information? (Circle all that apply.)
>
> i. $\frac{1}{400}$ s · ii. $\frac{1}{200}$ s · iii. $\frac{1}{100}$ s · iv. $\frac{1}{40}$ s · v. $\frac{1}{20}$ s
>
> (b) Let $x_a(t) = \sin(40\pi t)$ and $y_a(t) = \sin(40\pi t)$. Which of the following sampling periods $T$ are consistent with this information? (Circle all that apply.)
>
> i. $\frac{1}{100}$ s · ii. $\frac{1}{60}$ s · iii. $\frac{1}{40}$ s · iv. $\frac{1}{20}$ s · v. $\frac{1}{10}$ s
>
> (c) Let $x_a(t) = \sin(30\pi t) + \sin(100\pi t)$ and $y_a(t) = \sin(10\pi t) + \sin(30\pi t)$. Determine the sampling period $T$.

> [!success]- Solution 6
> **(a)** $x[n] = \sin(100\pi Tn)$ must equal $\sin(\frac{\pi}{2}n)$, so $100\pi T = \frac{\pi}{2} + 2\pi k$:
> - ii. $T = \frac{1}{200}$: $100\pi T = \frac{\pi}{2}$ ✓
> - iv. $T = \frac{1}{40}$: $100\pi T = \frac{5\pi}{2} \equiv \frac{\pi}{2}$ ✓ (aliased, but the samples are identical)
> - i. gives $\frac{\pi}{4}$; iii. and v. give $\pi$ and $5\pi$, where $\sin(m\pi n) = 0$.
>
> Answer: $\textbf{ii and iv}$.
>
> **(b)** Getting the same sinusoid back means no aliasing: 20 Hz must lie below $\frac{f_s}{2}$, so $f_s \gt 40$ Hz and $T \lt \frac{1}{40}$ s. Answer: $\textbf{i and ii}$. At exactly $T = \frac{1}{40}$ the samples are $\sin(\pi n) = 0$ and the output is 0; iv and v put the samples at multiples of $2\pi$ (zeros again).
>
> **(c)** The 15 Hz term survives unchanged, so $f_s \gt 30$ Hz. The 50 Hz term must come out at 5 Hz **with a plus sign**: $\big(100\pi T - 2\pi k\big)\frac1T = 10\pi$, i.e. $90\pi T = 2\pi k$ and $T = \frac{k}{45}$. Only $k = 1$ keeps $f_s \gt 30$ Hz:
> $$
> \boldsymbol{T = \frac{1}{45}\ \text{s}}\qquad\big(f_s = 45\ \text{Hz}:\ 50\ \text{Hz} \mapsto \omega = \tfrac{20\pi}{9} \equiv \tfrac{2\pi}{9} \mapsto 5\ \text{Hz}\big)
> $$
> The other fold, $f_s = 55$ Hz, maps 50 Hz to $\omega = -\frac{2\pi}{11}$ and outputs $-\sin(10\pi t)$: wrong sign. For $k \ge 2$ the 15 Hz term would alias too (checked: a scan of $f_s$ from 1 to 400 Hz in 0.01 Hz steps finds only 45 Hz).

> [!trap] Where points go
> - **(a)** Rejecting iv "because it aliases": the question asks which $T$ produce these **samples**, and aliasing is allowed.
> - **(b)** $T = \frac{1}{40}$ s, exactly the Nyquist rate, fails for a **sine**: every sample lands on a zero.
> - **(c)** For a sine the sign of the folded frequency matters; for a cosine it would not.

## Problem 7 · DFT index gymnastics (12 pts)

*Topic: DFT properties (circular shift, modulation), zero-padding and the symmetry of a real signal's DFT — Lectures 17+ (not yet in these notes).*

> [!question] Problem 7
> For all parts of this question, let $\{x[n]\}_{n=0}^{7} = \{1,\ 2,\ -3,\ -4,\ 5,\ 6,\ -2,\ 2\}$ be a length-8 signal with DFT $\{X[m]\}_{m=0}^{7} = \{X_0, X_1, X_2, X_3, X_4, X_5, X_6, X_7\}$.
>
> (a) Compute $X_0$.
>
> (b) The DFT of another length-8 signal $\{y[n]\}_{n=0}^{7}$ is given by $\{Y[m]\}_{m=0}^{7} = \{-X_5,\ X_6,\ -X_7,\ X_0,\ -X_1,\ X_2,\ -X_3,\ X_4\}$. Determine the signal $y[n]$.
>
> (c) Suppose we zero-pad $x[n]$ with 52 zeros to obtain $\{z[n]\}_{n=0}^{59}$ with corresponding DFT $\{Z[m]\}_{m=0}^{59}$. Which of the following relations are true? (Circle all that apply.)
>
> i. $X[0] = Z[0]$ · ii. $X[1] = Z[1]$ · iii. $X[7] = Z[59]$ · iv. $X[2] = Z[15]$ · v. $X[4] = Z^*[30]$ · vi. $X[6] = Z^*[15]$

> [!success]- Solution 7
> **(a)** $X_0 = \sum_n x[n] = 1 + 2 - 3 - 4 + 5 + 6 - 2 + 2 = \boldsymbol{7}$.
>
> **(b)** $Y$ is a shifted, sign-alternated copy of $X$: $Y[m] = (-1)^{m+1}X[\langle m-3\rangle_8]$. Take it in two steps with $V[m] = (-1)^mX[m]$:
> - $(-1)^m = e^{-j\frac{2\pi\cdot4}{8}m}$ is a circular **delay by 4**: $v[n] = x[\langle n-4\rangle_8] = \{5,\ 6,\ -2,\ 2,\ 1,\ 2,\ -3,\ -4\}$.
> - $Y[m] = V[\langle m-3\rangle_8]$ (because $(-1)^{m-3} = (-1)^{m+1}$) is a **frequency shift by 3**: $y[n] = e^{j\frac{2\pi\cdot3}{8}n}v[n] = e^{j\frac{3\pi}{4}n}v[n]$.
> $$
> \boldsymbol{y[n] = e^{j\frac{3\pi}{4}n}\,x[\langle n-4\rangle_8] = \left\{5,\ 6e^{j\frac{3\pi}{4}},\ 2j,\ 2e^{j\frac{\pi}{4}},\ -1,\ 2e^{-j\frac{\pi}{4}},\ -3j,\ 4e^{j\frac{\pi}{4}}\right\}}
> $$
> The key leaves the exponents unreduced, $\{5, 6e^{j\frac{3\pi}{4}}, -2e^{j\frac{3\pi}{2}}, 2e^{j\frac{9\pi}{4}}, -1, 2e^{j\frac{15\pi}{4}}, -3e^{j\frac{18\pi}{4}}, -4e^{j\frac{21\pi}{4}}\}$: the same numbers.
>
> **(c)** $X[m] = X_d(\frac{2\pi m}{8})$ and $Z[m] = X_d(\frac{2\pi m}{60})$ sample the same DTFT on two grids, and $x$ is real, so $X_d(-\omega) = X_d^*(\omega)$.
> - i. both are $X_d(0)$ ✓
> - ii. $X_d(\frac{\pi}{4})$ vs $X_d(\frac{\pi}{30})$ ✗; iii. $X_d(\frac{7\pi}{4})$ vs $X_d(\frac{59\pi}{30})$ ✗
> - iv. $\frac{2\pi\cdot2}{8} = \frac{\pi}{2} = \frac{2\pi\cdot15}{60}$ ✓
> - v. $X[4] = X_d(\pi) = Z[30]$, and $X_d(\pi) = \sum_n(-1)^nx[n]$ is real, so it also equals $Z^*[30]$ ✓
> - vi. $X[6] = X_d(\frac{3\pi}{2}) = X_d(-\frac{\pi}{2}) = X_d^*(\frac{\pi}{2}) = Z^*[15]$ ✓
>
> Answer: $\textbf{i, iv, v, vi}$ (checked: `np.fft.fft(x)` and `np.fft.fft(x, 60)`).

> [!trap] Where points go
> - **(b)** Two conventions to keep apart: multiplying $X[m]$ by $e^{-j2\pi mk/N}$ delays $x$ by $k$, while shifting $X$ to $X[\langle m-k\rangle_N]$ multiplies $x$ by $e^{+j2\pi kn/N}$. The wrong sign gives $e^{-j\frac{3\pi}{4}n}$.
> - **(c) iii.** $X[7]$ and $Z[59]$ are both "the last bin", but at different frequencies ($\frac{7\pi}{4}$ vs $\frac{59\pi}{30}$). **vi** needs the conjugate symmetry of a real signal's spectrum.

## Problem 8 · A phase ramp in the DFT (6 pts)

*Topic: DFT circular-shift property — Lectures 17+ (not yet in these notes).*

> [!question] Problem 8
> You are given two sequences $x[n] = \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8\}$ and $y[n] = \{y_0, y_1, y_2, y_3, y_4, y_5, y_6, y_7\}$, and their DFTs are related by $Y[m] = X[m]e^{j\frac{3\pi}{4}m}$. Determine $y[n]$ based on the values of $x[n]$.

> [!success]- Solution 8
> $e^{j\frac{3\pi}{4}m} = e^{-j\frac{2\pi m}{8}\cdot(-3)}$ is a circular shift by $-3$, an **advance** by 3: $y[n] = x[\langle n+3\rangle_8] = x[\langle n-5\rangle_8]$,
> $$
> \boldsymbol{y[n] = \{\underset{\uparrow}{4},\ 5,\ 6,\ 7,\ 8,\ 1,\ 2,\ 3\}}
> $$
> (checked: `np.fft.ifft(np.fft.fft(x) * np.exp(1j*3*np.pi/4*np.arange(8)))`)

> [!trap] Where points go
> - A positive exponent is an advance, so $y[0] = x[3] = 4$. The delay answer $\{6, 7, 8, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5\}$ is the sign slip.
