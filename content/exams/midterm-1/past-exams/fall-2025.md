## Problem 1 · True/False (12 pts)

> [!question] Problem 1
> Select **True** or **False** for each statement.
>
> (a) The impulse response of a given LTI system is known to be a bounded sequence. This system must be BIBO stable.
>
> (b) The convolution of two causal signals always results in a causal signal. (A causal signal is defined as a signal that is zero for all negative time values.)
>
> (c) A BIBO stable LTI system has a transfer function with two poles at $z = \frac14$ and $z = 3$. The impulse response of this system must be two-sided.
>
> (d) An LTI system with a left-sided impulse response can never be causal.
>
> (e) An unbounded signal is passed as input to a BIBO stable LTI system. The resulting output is always an unbounded signal.
>
> (f) The parallel connection of two unstable LTI systems always forms another unstable LTI system.

> [!success]- Solution 1
> - **(a) False.** Stability needs $\sum_n \lvert h[n]\rvert < \infty$, not boundedness. $h = u[n]$ (the accumulator) is bounded by 1, but $x = u[n]$ gives $y = (n+1)u[n]$.
> - **(b) True.** If $x[k] = 0$ for $k<0$ and $h[m] = 0$ for $m<0$, then $y[n] = \sum_{k=0}^{n} x[k]h[n-k]$, an empty sum for $n<0$.
> - **(c) True.** Stable ⇒ the ROC contains $\lvert z\rvert = 1$ ⇒ ROC $= \tfrac14 < \lvert z\rvert < 3$, an annulus ⇒ $h = A(\tfrac14)^n u[n] - B\,3^n u[-n-1]$, two-sided.
> - **(d) True.** A left-sided $h$ (in the Lecture 7 sense: an infinite-length sequence extending to $n\to-\infty$) has nonzero samples at negative $n$, so the output reacts before the impulse arrives. See the note below.
> - **(e) False.** Pole–zero cancellation: $h = \delta[n] - 2\delta[n-1]$ is FIR (stable), $x = 2^n u[n]$ is unbounded, and $Y(z) = (1-2z^{-1})\cdot\frac{1}{1-2z^{-1}} = 1$, so $y = \delta[n]$.
> - **(f) False.** In parallel the impulse responses add: $h_1 = u[n]$ and $h_2 = \delta[n] - u[n]$ are both unstable, but $h_1 + h_2 = \delta[n]$ is stable.

> [!note] A subtlety in #1(d) — not an erratum
> The key circles **True**, and with the Lecture 7 meaning of "left-sided" (an *infinite-length* sequence with $x[n] = 0$ for $n > n_0$, i.e. one that extends to $n\to-\infty$) that is simply correct. Read literally as "$x[n] = 0$ for $n > n_0$" the term would also cover finite sequences such as $\delta[n]$, which is causal — so if you ever feel the need to argue, write the assumption down. On the exam, answer True.

> [!trap] Where points go
> - **Bounded $h$ ≠ stable.** The test is absolute summability of $h$ ([[concepts/bibo-stability|BIBO stability]]).
> - **"Always" statements** ((e), (f)) die to one counterexample; for (e) the standard counterexample is a zero of $H$ cancelling the input's unstable pole ([[concepts/pole-zero-cancellation|pole–zero cancellation]]).
> - **(c)** does not say "causal": stability *chooses* the ROC, and an annulus means two-sided.

## Problem 2 · System properties (12 pts)

> [!question] Problem 2
> For each system with input $x[n]$ and output $y[n]$, indicate by "yes" or "no" whether it is Linear, Shift-Invariant, Causal, Stable.
>
> 1. $y[n] = \lvert n\rvert\,x[n]$
> 2. $y[n] = \lvert x[n] - x[n-1]\rvert$
> 3. $y[n] = x[n] * 2^n u[-n]$

> [!success]- Solution 2
>
> | system | Linear | Shift-inv. | Causal | Stable |
> |---|---|---|---|---|
> | $\lvert n\rvert x[n]$ | **Yes** | **No** | **Yes** | **No** |
> | $\lvert x[n]-x[n-1]\rvert$ | **No** | **Yes** | **Yes** | **Yes** |
> | $x[n]*2^n u[-n]$ | **Yes** | **Yes** | **No** | **Yes** |
>
> - Row 1: multiplying by a fixed sequence is linear; the gain $\lvert n\rvert$ depends on time, so a shifted input is not simply a shifted output; memoryless ⇒ causal; $x = u[n]$ (bounded) gives $y = n\,u[n]$ (unbounded).
> - Row 2: $x \to -x$ gives the *same* output, not $-y$ ⇒ nonlinear; no explicit $n$ ⇒ SI; uses only $x[n], x[n-1]$ ⇒ causal; $\lvert x\rvert \le B \Rightarrow \lvert y\rvert \le 2B$ ⇒ stable.
> - Row 3: a convolution is LTI with $h[n] = 2^n u[-n]$; $h[-1] = \tfrac12 \ne 0$ ⇒ not causal; $\sum_{n\le 0} 2^n = \sum_{m\ge 0} 2^{-m} = 2 < \infty$ ⇒ stable.

> [!trap] Where points go
> - **Row 3:** "$2^n$ blows up, so unstable" — but $u[-n]$ keeps only $n \le 0$, where $2^n$ *decays*. Always check which side the step lives on.
> - **Row 1:** a time-varying *gain* keeps the system linear but makes it shift-varying, and an unbounded gain like $\lvert n\rvert$ also makes it unstable. More rows like these: [[exams/midterm-1/system-property-bank|system-property bank]].

## Problem 3 · Convolution (10 pts)

> [!question] Problem 3
> Compute $y[n] = (x*h)[n]$.
>
> (a) $x[n] = \{\underset{\uparrow}{1},\ 2,\ 3,\ 0,\ -1,\ -2,\ -3\}$, $\quad h[n] = \{1,\ 0,\ \underset{\uparrow}{1}\}$
>
> (b) $x[n] = n\,u[n+1]\,u[-n+1]$, $\quad h[n] = n\left(\tfrac13\right)^n\cos(n)$

> [!success]- Solution 3(a)
> The arrow is on the **last** entry of $h$: $h[n] = \delta[n+2] + \delta[n]$, so $y[n] = x[n+2] + x[n]$. First index $0 + (-2) = -2$, length $7 + 3 - 1 = 9$.
>
> | $n$ | −2 | −1 | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 |
> |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
> | $x[n+2]$ | 1 | 2 | 3 | 0 | −1 | −2 | −3 | 0 | 0 |
> | $x[n]$ | 0 | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 0 | −1 | −2 | −3 |
> | $y[n]$ | 1 | 2 | 4 | 2 | 2 | −2 | −4 | −2 | −3 |
>
> $$
> \boldsymbol{y[n] = \{1,\ 2,\ \underset{\uparrow}{4},\ 2,\ 2,\ -2,\ -4,\ -2,\ -3\}}
> $$

> [!success]- Solution 3(b)
> $u[n+1]\,u[-n+1] = 1$ only for $-1 \le n \le 1$, so $x = \{-1,\ \underset{\uparrow}{0},\ 1\} = -\delta[n+1] + \delta[n-1]$. Convolving with shifted deltas shifts $h$:
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> y[n] &= -h[n+1] + h[n-1]\\
> &= \boldsymbol{-(n+1)\left(\tfrac13\right)^{n+1}\cos(n+1) + (n-1)\left(\tfrac13\right)^{n-1}\cos(n-1)},\quad \text{all } n .
> \end{aligned}
> $$

> [!trap] Where points go
> - **Arrow position.** $h$ starts at $n = -2$, so the output starts at $n = -2$ and $y[0] = 4$, not $y[0] = 1$.
> - **(b):** replace *every* $n$ in $h$ — the factor $n$, the exponent, and the cosine argument. $\cos(n)$ has no $\pi$; leave it alone. $h$ has no $u[n]$, so the answer holds for all $n$.

```python
import numpy as np
x, nx0 = [1, 2, 3, 0, -1, -2, -3], 0     # arrow on the first entry
h, nh0 = [1, 0, 1], -2                    # arrow on the LAST entry -> starts at n = -2
y = np.convolve(x, h)
ny0 = nx0 + nh0                           # first index of y
print("y starts at n =", ny0, ":", y)
print("y[0] =", y[0 - ny0])
```

```text
y starts at n = -2 : [ 1  2  4  2  2 -2 -4 -2 -3]
y[0] = 4
```

## Problem 4 · Modified moving average (10 pts)

> [!question] Problem 4
> The moving average system is a discrete-time system where $L$ recent input samples are averaged to produce the system output at each time index, for finite, positive integer $L$. Consider the below modification of the moving average system:
> $$
> y[n] = \frac1L\sum_{k=0}^{L-1} x[n - Sk].
> $$
> Above, $S$ is a positive integer.
>
> (a) Determine if the above modified moving average system is (i) linear and (ii) time-invariant. Circle one option for each part (Linear / Non-linear, Time-invariant / Time-varying); no proofs or justification necessary.
>
> (b) Let $L = 3$ and $S = 4$. Determine the impulse response $h[n]$.
>
> (c) True or False, with a brief explanation: the moving average system in this problem is always BIBO stable.

> [!success]- Solution 4
> **(a)** A fixed weighted sum of delayed inputs, no explicit $n$: **linear** and **time-invariant**.
>
> **(b)** $y[n] = \tfrac13\big(x[n] + x[n-4] + x[n-8]\big)$; put $x = \delta$:
> $$
> \boldsymbol{h[n] = \tfrac13\big(\delta[n] + \delta[n-4] + \delta[n-8]\big) = \{\underset{\uparrow}{\tfrac13},\ 0,\ 0,\ 0,\ \tfrac13,\ 0,\ 0,\ 0,\ \tfrac13\}}
> $$
>
> **(c) True.** No feedback terms ⇒ FIR; $\sum_n \lvert h[n]\rvert = L\cdot\frac1L = 1 < \infty$ for every $L$ and $S$.

> [!trap] Where points go
> - The delays are $Sk = 0, 4, 8$, not $0, 1, 2$ — $h$ has length $S(L-1)+1 = 9$ with zeros in between.
> - Forgetting the $\tfrac1L$. "Stable because it averages" is not an explanation; "FIR ⇒ $\sum\lvert h\rvert$ finite" is.

## Problem 5 · z-transforms (15 pts)

> [!question] Problem 5
> Determine the z-transform and region of convergence.
>
> (a) $x[n] = e^{j\frac{\pi}{3}n}\,u[n+4]$
>
> (b) $x[n] = u[n] - u[n-8]$
>
> (c) $x[n] = \cos^2\!\left(\tfrac{\pi}{4}n\right)u[n]$

> [!success]- Solution 5(a)
> Make the exponent carry the same shift as the step: $x[n] = e^{-j4\pi/3}\,e^{j\frac{\pi}{3}(n+4)}\,u[n+4]$. Then $a^n u[n] \leftrightarrow \frac{1}{1-az^{-1}}$ and an advance by 4 multiplies by $z^4$:
> $$
> \boldsymbol{X(z) = \frac{e^{-j4\pi/3}\,z^{4}}{1 - e^{j\pi/3}z^{-1}},\qquad \text{ROC: } 1 < \lvert z\rvert < \infty}
> $$
> ($e^{-j4\pi/3} = e^{j2\pi/3}$.) Check: $\sum_{n=-4}^{\infty} (e^{j\pi/3}z^{-1})^n$ is a geometric series with first term $(e^{j\pi/3}z^{-1})^{-4}$. It needs $\lvert z\rvert > 1$, and the four samples at $n = -4,\dots,-1$ contribute positive powers of $z$, so $z = \infty$ is excluded.

> [!warning] Answer-key erratum — #5(a)
> The handwritten key writes the denominator as $1 - e^{j\frac{\pi}{3}n}z^{-1}$ — the $n$ is a slip (a z-transform cannot depend on $n$). The correct denominator is $1 - e^{j\pi/3}z^{-1}$.

> [!success]- Solution 5(b)
> $x = \{\underset{\uparrow}{1},1,1,1,1,1,1,1\}$ on $n = 0,\dots,7$:
> $$
> \boldsymbol{X(z) = \sum_{k=0}^{7} z^{-k} = 1 + z^{-1} + \dots + z^{-7} = \frac{1 - z^{-8}}{1 - z^{-1}},\qquad \text{ROC: all } z \ne 0}
> $$

> [!success]- Solution 5(c)
> $\cos^2\theta = \left(\frac{e^{j\theta}+e^{-j\theta}}{2}\right)^2 = \tfrac12 + \tfrac14 e^{j2\theta} + \tfrac14 e^{-j2\theta}$ with $\theta = \tfrac{\pi}{4}n$:
> $x[n] = \left(\tfrac12 + \tfrac14 e^{j\frac{\pi}{2}n} + \tfrac14 e^{-j\frac{\pi}{2}n}\right)u[n]$, so
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> \boldsymbol{X(z)} &\boldsymbol{= \frac{1/2}{1 - z^{-1}} + \frac{1/4}{1 - jz^{-1}} + \frac{1/4}{1 + jz^{-1}}}\\
> &\boldsymbol{= \frac{1/2}{1-z^{-1}} + \frac{1/2}{1 + z^{-2}},\qquad \text{ROC: } \lvert z\rvert > 1}
> \end{aligned}
> $$

> [!trap] Where points go
> - **(a)** Writing $\frac{z^4}{1-e^{j\pi/3}z^{-1}}$ without the constant $e^{-j4\pi/3}$ — the time shift property needs $e^{j\frac{\pi}{3}(n+4)}$, so the leftover factor must be paid back. Also: ROC upper limit $\infty$ is *excluded*.
> - **(b)** Writing ROC $\lvert z\rvert > 1$ because the closed form has $1-z^{-1}$ downstairs. That pole is cancelled; a finite causal sequence converges for all $z \ne 0$.
> - **(c)** Squaring doubles the frequency ($\tfrac{\pi}{2}$, not $\tfrac{\pi}{4}$), and the constant $\tfrac12$ term (pole at $z = 1$) is easy to drop.

## Problem 6 · LCCDE → transfer function → response (16 pts)

> [!question] Problem 6
> A **causal** LTI system is described by
> $$
> y[n] = \tfrac43\,y[n-1] + \tfrac43\,y[n-2] + x[n] - x[n-2].
> $$
> (a) Determine the transfer function $H(z)$, its poles, zeros, and ROC.
>
> (b) Determine the response $y[n]$ to $x[n] = 3\delta[n] + 2\delta[n-1]$.

> [!success]- Solution 6(a)
> $Y(z)\left(1 - \tfrac43 z^{-1} - \tfrac43 z^{-2}\right) = X(z)\left(1 - z^{-2}\right)$, and $z^2 - \tfrac43 z - \tfrac43 = (z-2)(z+\tfrac23)$:
> $$
> \boldsymbol{H(z) = \frac{1 - z^{-2}}{1 - \frac43 z^{-1} - \frac43 z^{-2}} = \frac{(1-z^{-1})(1+z^{-1})}{(1-2z^{-1})(1+\frac23 z^{-1})}}
> $$
> **Poles $z = 2,\ -\tfrac23$; zeros $z = 1,\ -1$; ROC $\lvert z\rvert > 2$** (causal ⇒ outside the outermost pole; the system is not stable).

> [!success]- Solution 6(b)
> $X(z) = 3 + 2z^{-1} = 3\left(1 + \tfrac23 z^{-1}\right)$ — it cancels the pole at $-\tfrac23$:
> $$
> Y(z) = \frac{3(1 - z^{-2})}{1 - 2z^{-1}} = \frac{3}{1-2z^{-1}} - \frac{3z^{-2}}{1-2z^{-1}},\quad \lvert z\rvert > 2
> $$
> $$
> \begin{gathered}
> \boldsymbol{y[n] = 3(2)^n u[n] - 3(2)^{n-2}u[n-2]}\\
> \;=\; 3\delta[n] + 6\delta[n-1] + 9\cdot 2^{n-2}u[n-2].
> \end{gathered}
> $$
> (Equivalently $y = 3h[n] + 2h[n-1]$.)

> [!trap] Where points go
> - **Sign flip** when moving $y$ terms left: the denominator is $1 - \tfrac43 z^{-1} - \tfrac43 z^{-2}$.
> - **Cancel first.** Spotting $3 + 2z^{-1} = 3(1+\tfrac23 z^{-1})$ turns (b) into two lines; missing it means a three-term PFE of an improper $Y(z)$.
> - The pole at $2$ survives, so $y$ grows — that is correct, not an arithmetic error.

## Problem 7 · Stable two-sided system and its recursions (15 pts)

> [!question] Problem 7
> The transfer function of a **BIBO stable** LTI system is
> $$
> H(z) = \frac{1 - z^{-1}}{(1 + 2z^{-1})(1 + \frac23 z^{-1})}.
> $$
> (a) Determine the impulse response $h[n]$.
>
> (b) The system can be implemented as $H(z) = H_1(z) + H_2(z)$, i.e. $Y(z) = H_1(z)X(z) + H_2(z)X(z)$. Determine BIBO stable difference equations that implement $H_1(z)$ and $H_2(z)$. Specify the direction of recursive computation (causal or anti-causal implementation).

> [!success]- Solution 7(a)
> Poles $-2$ and $-\tfrac23$; stable ⇒ ROC contains the unit circle ⇒ **ROC $\tfrac23 < \lvert z\rvert < 2$**. Cover-up:
> $$
> A_1 = \left.\frac{1-z^{-1}}{1+\frac23 z^{-1}}\right|_{z=-2} = \frac{3/2}{2/3} = \frac94,\qquad
> A_2 = \left.\frac{1-z^{-1}}{1+2z^{-1}}\right|_{z=-2/3} = \frac{5/2}{-2} = -\frac54 .
> $$
> $H(z) = \dfrac{9/4}{1+2z^{-1}} - \dfrac{5/4}{1+\frac23 z^{-1}}$. The pole $-2$ lies *outside* the ROC (term is left-sided), the pole $-\tfrac23$ *inside* (right-sided):
> $$
> \boldsymbol{h[n] = -\tfrac94(-2)^n u[-n-1] - \tfrac54\left(-\tfrac23\right)^n u[n]}
> $$

> [!success]- Solution 7(b)
> - $H_1(z) = \dfrac{9/4}{1+2z^{-1}}$, ROC $\lvert z\rvert < 2$ (**anti-causal**): $y_1[n] + 2y_1[n-1] = \tfrac94 x[n]$, solved for the *earlier* sample:
>   $$
>   \boldsymbol{y_1[n-1] = -\tfrac12\,y_1[n] + \tfrac98\,x[n]}\quad\text{computed backward in time.}
>   $$
> - $H_2(z) = \dfrac{-5/4}{1+\frac23 z^{-1}}$, ROC $\lvert z\rvert > \tfrac23$ (**causal**):
>   $$
>   \boldsymbol{y_2[n] = -\tfrac23\,y_2[n-1] - \tfrac54\,x[n]}\quad\text{computed forward in time.}
>   $$
> - Output $y[n] = y_1[n] + y_2[n]$. Both recursions have feedback gain of magnitude $< 1$ in their own direction ($\tfrac12$ backward, $\tfrac23$ forward), which is why each is stable.

> [!trap] Where points go
> - Forgetting the **minus sign** of the left-sided pair: $\frac{A}{1-pz^{-1}}$ with $\lvert z\rvert<\lvert p\rvert$ ↔ $-A\,p^n u[-n-1]$.
> - Running $H_1$ forward ($y_1[n] = -2y_1[n-1] + \tfrac94 x[n]$) is the *causal, unstable* system — the direction must match the ROC ([[problems/two-sided-systems-as-recursions|two-sided systems as recursions]]).

```python
import numpy as np
from scipy.signal import residuez
b = [1, -1]
a = np.convolve([1, 2], [1, 2/3])        # (1 + 2z^-1)(1 + (2/3)z^-1)
r, p, k = residuez(b, a)
print("residues A_k:", np.round(r.real, 4))
print("poles    p_k:", np.round(p.real, 4))
print("direct terms:", k)
```

```text
residues A_k: [-1.25  2.25]
poles    p_k: [-0.6667 -2.    ]
direct terms: []
```

`residuez` gives the $A_k$ and $p_k$; *you* still pick the ROC and hence which terms become $u[n]$ and which $-u[-n-1]$.

## Problem 8 · Bounded or unbounded output? (10 pts)

> [!question] Problem 8 (the two parts are unrelated)
> **(a)** An LTI system has
> $$
> H(z) = \frac{1 - \frac34 z^{-1}}{1 + 3z^{-1}},\quad \lvert z\rvert > 3 .
> $$
> Mark True or False: which inputs produce a **bounded output**?
>
> - i. $\left(\tfrac34\right)^n u[n]$
> - ii. $\tfrac13\left(\tfrac12\right)^n u[n] + \left(\tfrac12\right)^{n-1}u[n-1]$
> - iii. $(-3)^n u[n]$
> - iv. $\delta[n] - \tfrac34\delta[n-1]$
> - v. $\delta[n] + 3\delta[n-1]$
>
> **(b)** A causal LTI system has
> $$
> H(z) = \frac{1}{(1 - \frac34 z^{-1})(1 - jz^{-1})(1 - e^{j\frac23}z^{-1})},\quad \lvert z\rvert > 1 .
> $$
> Mark True or False: which inputs produce an **unbounded output**?
>
> - i. $\delta[n] - \tfrac34\delta[n-1]$
> - ii. $\left(\tfrac23\right)^n u[n]$
> - iii. $j^n u[n]$
> - iv. $\sin\!\left(\tfrac{\pi}{2}n\right)u[n]$
> - v. $\cos\!\left(\tfrac{2\pi}{3}n\right)u[n]$

> [!success]- Solution 8(a) — bounded output?
> Causal with an unstable pole at $z = -3$. The output is bounded only if $X(z)$ has a **zero at $-3$** that cancels it.
> - i. **False.** $X = \frac{1}{1-\frac34 z^{-1}}$ cancels the *zero* at $\tfrac34$, not the pole: $Y = \frac{1}{1+3z^{-1}}$, $y = (-3)^n u[n]$.
> - ii. **True.** $X = \frac{\frac13 + z^{-1}}{1-\frac12 z^{-1}} = \frac{\frac13(1+3z^{-1})}{1-\frac12 z^{-1}}$: the zero at $-3$ cancels the pole; $Y = \frac{\frac13(1-\frac34 z^{-1})}{1-\frac12 z^{-1}}$ decays.
> - iii. **False.** Adds a second pole at $-3$ (double pole ⇒ $n(-3)^n$ growth).
> - iv. **False.** $X = 1 - \tfrac34 z^{-1}$ leaves the pole at $-3$ untouched.
> - v. **True.** $X = 1 + 3z^{-1}$ cancels it: $Y = 1 - \tfrac34 z^{-1}$, $y = \delta[n] - \tfrac34\delta[n-1]$.

> [!success]- Solution 8(b) — unbounded output?
> Poles $\tfrac34$ (inside), $j$ and $e^{j2/3}$ (simple, **on** the unit circle): marginally stable. A bounded input gives an unbounded output exactly when it puts a *second* pole on top of $j$ or $e^{j2/3}$ (double pole on $\lvert z\rvert = 1$ ⇒ $n\,p^n$ growth).
> - i. **False.** Cancels $\tfrac34$; the two simple unit-circle poles give bounded $j^n$ and $e^{j2n/3}$ terms.
> - ii. **False.** New pole at $\tfrac23$, inside the circle.
> - iii. **True.** Pole at $j$ ⇒ double pole at $j$.
> - iv. **True.** $\sin(\tfrac{\pi}{2}n) = \frac{j^n - (-j)^n}{2j}$ has poles $\pm j$ ⇒ double pole at $j$.
> - v. **False.** Poles $e^{\pm j2\pi/3}$ (angle $120^\circ$) — **not** $e^{j2/3}$ (angle $\tfrac23$ rad $\approx 38^\circ$). Nothing doubles.

> [!trap] The $e^{j2/3}$ vs $e^{j2\pi/3}$ trap
> The system pole in (b) is $e^{j\frac23}$ — angle $\tfrac23$ **radian**, no $\pi$. The input in v. is $\cos(\tfrac{2\pi}{3}n)$, with poles at angle $\tfrac{2\pi}{3}$. Read on autopilot, they "match" and you answer True; they don't, and the answer is **False**. Always compare pole *locations* digit by digit.
>
> Also: (a) asks which outputs are **bounded**, (b) which are **unbounded** — the same reasoning gives opposite T/F letters. Recipe and more drills: [[problems/unbounded-outputs-and-pole-matching|unbounded outputs and pole matching]], [[concepts/marginal-stability|marginal stability]].
