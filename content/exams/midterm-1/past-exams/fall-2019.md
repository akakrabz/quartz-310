## Problem 1 · True/False (20 pts)

> [!question] Problem 1 (20 pts)
> Mark "True" or "False" for the following statements.
> - **(a)** An LSI system specified by the following difference equation: $y[n] - \tfrac12 y[n-1] = x[n]$ can be causal or anti-causal.
> - **(b)** The input and output relationship of an arbitrary system is completely determined by the system's unit pulse response.
> - **(c)** If an LSI system is BIBO unstable, its unit pulse response $h[n]$ must be unbounded.
> - **(d)** Let $h[n] = h_1[n] * h_2[n]$ be the unit pulse response (UPR) of two serial subsystems with UPR $h_1[n]$ and $h_2[n]$. If $h_1$ or $h_2$ is BIBO *unstable*, $h$ must be BIBO *unstable*.
> - **(e)** An LSI system with a finite-length impulse response can be BIBO stable or unstable.
> - **(f)** A time-varying system cannot be causal.
> - **(g)** Let $\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} x[n]\,\delta[2^n u[n] - 8] = 4$. Then, $x[3] = 2$.
> - **(h)** Suppose that the step response $g[n]$ of an LSI system, i.e., the output of the system to the input $x[n] = u[n]$, has a z-transform with a pole at $z = 1/2$. Then, $H(z)$ has also pole at $z = 1/2$.
> - **(i)** If the response $y[n]$ of an LSI system to the input $x[n] = 3^n u[n]$ is unbounded, the system must be BIBO unstable.
> - **(j)** The response $y[n]$ of a BIBO *unstable* LSI system to any non-zero input $x[n]$ is always unbounded.

> [!success]- Solution
> - **(a) True.** $H(z) = \frac{1}{1-\frac12 z^{-1}}$ without an ROC: $(\frac12)^n u[n]$ or $-(\frac12)^n u[-n-1]$.
> - **(b) False.** $y = x^2[n]$ and $y = x[n]$ share $h = \delta[n]$.
> - **(c) False.** $h = u[n]$ is bounded and unstable.
> - **(d) False.** $u[n] * (\delta[n]-\delta[n-1]) = \delta[n]$.
> - **(e) False.** FIR ⇒ always stable.
> - **(f) False.** $y[n] = n\,x[n]$ is time-varying and causal.
> - **(g) False.** $2^n u[n] - 8 = 0$ only at $n = 3$: $x[3] = 4$.
> - **(h) True.** $G = H\cdot\frac{1}{1-z^{-1}}$; the step's factor is finite, nonzero at $\frac12$.
> - **(i) False.** $h = \delta[n]$ is stable and returns $3^n u[n]$.
> - **(j) False.** $h = u[n]$, $x = \delta[n] - \delta[n-1]$ gives $y = \delta[n]$.
>
> Answers: **T F F F F F F T F F** ([[exams/midterm-1/true-false-bank|T/F bank]] Q40, Q3, Q13, Q26, Q41, Q10, Q43, Q32, Q18, Q19).

> [!trap]
> Counterexample kit: $u[n]$, $\delta[n]$, $\delta[n]-\delta[n-1]$, $y = n\,x[n]$.

## Problem 2 · Classify two systems (12 pts)

> [!question] Problem 2 (12 pts)
> Determine whether the following systems are linear or nonlinear, shift-invariant or shift-varying, and causal or noncausal. *Grading:* Correct answer = +2 pts.; Incorrect answer = −1 pts. No answer = 0 pts.
> - **(a)** $y[n] = x[\lvert n\rvert + n]$
> - **(b)** $y[n] = (0.2)^{\lvert n\rvert}\log[x[n]]$

> [!success]- Solution
> **(a) L, SV, NC.** Index $\lvert n\rvert + n$ is $2n$ for $n \ge 0$, $0$ for $n < 0$. Input shift gives $x[\lvert n\rvert + n - n_0]$, output shift $x[\lvert n-n_0\rvert + n - n_0]$; $y[1] = x[2]$.
>
> **(b) NL, SV, C.** $\log(2x) \ne 2\log x$; $(0.2)^{\lvert n\rvert}$ depends on $n$; memoryless.
>
> (Not asked: (a) stable, (b) not, $\log 0 = -\infty$.)

> [!trap]
> Test causality of $x[f(n)]$ at positive $n$: $y[1] = x[2]$. More: [[exams/midterm-1/system-property-bank|system-property bank]].

> [!success]- Solution
> **(a)** $x[n] = 2\delta[n-2]$, $y[n] = 2h[n-2]$, $h[n] = \tfrac12\,y[n+2]$:
> $$
> \boxed{h[n] = \tfrac12\delta[n+1] + \delta[n] + \tfrac12\delta[n-1] = \{\tfrac12,\ \underset{\uparrow}{1},\ \tfrac12\}}
> $$
> (z: $H = \dfrac{Y}{X} = \dfrac{z^{-1}+2z^{-2}+z^{-3}}{2z^{-2}} = \tfrac12 z + 1 + \tfrac12 z^{-1}$.)
>
> **(b)** $s[n] = \tfrac12 u[n+1] + u[n] + \tfrac12 u[n-1]$:
> $$
> s[-1] = \tfrac12,\quad s[0] = \tfrac32,\quad s[1] = 2,\quad s[2] = 2.
> $$
>
> **(c)** **Not causal:** $h[-1] = \tfrac12 \ne 0$.

> [!trap]
> $h = \frac12 y[n]$ forgets the shift; move $y$ left by 2.

## Problem 4 · Finite convolution (5 pts)

> [!question] Problem 4 (5 pts)
> Calculate the result of the following convolution $\{\underset{\uparrow}{1}, -4, 2, -1, 3, 1\} * \{\underset{\uparrow}{-1}, 1, -1\}$.

> [!success]- Solution
> Both start at $n = 0$; $6 + 3 - 1 = 8$ samples (key: matrix form $y = \mathbf{H}x$):
> $$
> \boxed{y[n] = \{\underset{\uparrow}{-1},\ 5,\ -7,\ 7,\ -6,\ 3,\ -2,\ -1\}}\quad(n = 0,\dots,7).
> $$
> Check: $\sum y = -2 = (\sum x)(\sum h) = 2\cdot(-1)$.

> [!trap]
> Read the arrows first; the sum check misses index slips.

## Problem 5 · z-transforms of finite-length signals (10 pts)

> [!question] Problem 5 (10 pts)
> Calculate the z-transform and corresponding ROC of the following functions:
> - **(a)** $x[n] = 3^{-n}\left(u[n-5] - u[n-100]\right)$
> - **(b)** $x[n] = e^{-n^2}\,u[n-8]\,u[-n+10]$

> [!success]- Solution
> **(a)** 1 for $5 \le n \le 99$ (95 samples):
> $$
> X(z) = \sum_{n=5}^{99}\left(\tfrac13 z^{-1}\right)^n = \boxed{\frac{(\frac13)^5 z^{-5}\left(1 - (\frac13)^{95}z^{-95}\right)}{1 - \frac13 z^{-1}},\qquad z \ne 0}
> $$
> Key stops at $\sum_{k=5}^{99}(\frac13)^k z^{-k}$; "calculate" wants the closed form. The pole at $\frac13$ cancels.
>
> **(b)** $n = 8, 9, 10$:
> $$
> \boxed{X(z) = e^{-64}z^{-8} + e^{-81}z^{-9} + e^{-100}z^{-10},\qquad z \ne 0}
> $$

> [!warning] Answer-key erratum (#5b)
> The key writes $e^{-91}z^{-9}$; $9^2 = 81$. It also labels $X(z)$ as $X_d(\omega)$. See [[0-toolkit/05-errata|Errata]].

> [!trap]
> - Last sample is $n = 99$. Finite length ⇒ ROC all $z$ except $0$/$\infty$, not $\lvert z\rvert > \frac13$.

## Problem 6 · Inverse z-transform (6 pts)

> [!question] Problem 6 (6 pts)
> Calculate the inverse z-transform for
> $$
> Y(z) = 1 + z^{-100} + \frac{1}{1-5z^{-1}},\qquad \text{ROC: } \lvert z\rvert > 5.
> $$

> [!success]- Solution
> $$
> \boxed{y[n] = \delta[n] + \delta[n-100] + 5^n u[n]}
> $$

> [!trap]
> $z^{-100}$ is a delay, $\delta[n-100]$.

## Problem 7 · All valid ROCs (15 pts)

> [!question] Problem 7 (15 pts)
> The z-transform of $x[n]$ is given below:
> $$
> X(z) = \frac{z}{z - e^{j\frac{\pi}{3}}} + \frac{z}{z - 0.5}.
> $$
> Determine all the valid ROCs of $X(z)$ and for each case, calculate the inverse z-transform.

> [!success]- Solution
> $X(z) = \dfrac{1}{1-e^{j\pi/3}z^{-1}} + \dfrac{1}{1-\frac12 z^{-1}}$; pole magnitudes $1$, $\tfrac12$:
>
> | ROC | $x[n]$ |
> |---|---|
> | $\lvert z\rvert > 1$ | $e^{j\pi n/3}u[n] + (\tfrac12)^n u[n]$ (both right-sided) |
> | $\tfrac12 < \lvert z\rvert < 1$ | $-e^{j\pi n/3}u[-n-1] + (\tfrac12)^n u[n]$ |
> | $\lvert z\rvert < \tfrac12$ | $-e^{j\pi n/3}u[-n-1] - (\tfrac12)^n u[-n-1]$ (both left-sided) |
>
> The fourth combination needs $\lvert z\rvert > 1$ and $\lvert z\rvert < \frac12$: empty (key's "Case 4").

> [!trap]
> - Boundaries are pole magnitudes; $N$ distinct magnitudes give $N+1$ ROCs, not $2^N$. Left-sided pairs carry a minus.

## Problem 10 · Causal $H(z)$, stability, LCCDE, a cancelling cascade (12 pts)

> [!question] Problem 10 (12 pts)
> Consider a *causal* LSI system with transfer function
> $$
> H(z) = \frac{1-2z^{-1}}{(1-\frac12 z^{-1})(1-\frac14 z^{-1})}.
> $$
> - **(a)** What is the ROC of $H(z)$?
> - **(b)** Is the system BIBO stable? Justify your answer.
> - **(c)** Find the difference equation (or LCCDE) for this system.
> - **(d)** **True or False:** If the previous system is serially connected to an unstable LSI system with impulse response $\tilde h[n] = 2^n u[n]$, then the overall system is BIBO unstable. Justify your answer.

> [!success]- Solution
> **(a)** **ROC $\lvert z\rvert > \tfrac12$**.
>
> **(b)** **Yes**: ROC contains $\lvert z\rvert = 1$ (the zero at 2 doesn't matter).
>
> **(c)** $Y(z)\left(1 - \frac34 z^{-1} + \frac18 z^{-2}\right) = X(z)\left(1 - 2z^{-1}\right)$:
> $$
> \boxed{y[n] = \tfrac34\,y[n-1] - \tfrac18\,y[n-2] + x[n] - 2x[n-1]}
> $$
>
> **(d)** **False.** $\tilde H(z) = \dfrac{1}{1-2z^{-1}}$; its pole cancels the zero of $H$:
> $$
> H(z)\tilde H(z) = \frac{1}{(1-\frac12 z^{-1})(1-\frac14 z^{-1})},\qquad \lvert z\rvert > \tfrac12,
> $$
> stable.
>
> (Same $H(z)$ as [[exams/midterm-1/past-exams/spring-2023|SP2023 #6]]: $h[n] = -6(\frac12)^n u[n] + 7(\frac14)^n u[n]$.)

> [!trap]
> - (d): multiply and look for cancellation before calling it unstable ([[problems/unbounded-outputs-and-pole-matching|pole matching]]).
