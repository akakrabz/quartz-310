## Problem 1 · True/False (12 pts)

> [!question] Problem 1
> Select **True** or **False** for each statement.
>
> (a) Any causal discrete-time system must also be time-invariant.
>
> (b) The ROC of a given z-transform cannot contain any poles or zeros.
>
> (c) Two BIBO stable systems connected in parallel always form a BIBO stable system.
>
> (d) For a discrete-time system with impulse response $h[n]$, the system output $y[n]$ to any input signal $x[n]$ is always given by $y[n] = x[n]*h[n]$.
>
> (e) Two LTI systems given by impulse responses $h_1[n]$ and $h_2[n]$ are connected in series in some order, i.e. $h_1[n]$ or $h_2[n]$ may come first. If we pass an input signal $x[n]$ to this system, we will receive the same output signal $y[n]$ for either ordering of $h_1[n]$ and $h_2[n]$.
>
> (f) A BIBO stable LTI system with transfer function $H(z) = \dfrac{1}{1 - 3z^{-1}}$ must be non-causal.

> [!success]- Solution 1
> - **(a) False.** $y[n] = n\,x[n]$: causal, time-varying.
> - **(b) False.** No poles, but zeros allowed: $X(z) = 1 - z^{-1}$, ROC $z \ne 0$, contains $z = 1$.
> - **(c) True.** $\lvert y_1 + y_2\rvert \le B_1 + B_2$, even for non-LTI systems.
> - **(d) False.** LTI only.
> - **(e) True.** $x * h_1 * h_2 = x * h_2 * h_1$.
> - **(f) True.** Stability forces ROC $\lvert z\rvert < 3$: $h = -3^n u[-n-1]$.

> [!trap] Where points go
> - (b): "or zeros" makes it False. (e): order can matter for non-LTI blocks.

## Problem 2 · System properties (12 pts)

> [!question] Problem 2
> For each system, indicate "yes" or "no" for Linear, Time-invariant, Causal, Stable (only the answers in the boxes are graded).
>
> 1. $y[n] = \log(\lvert n\rvert + 1)\,x[n]$
> 2. $y[n] = x[n] * u[n+1]$
> 3. $y[n] = x[n] + 3$

> [!success]- Solution 2
>
> | system | Linear | Time-inv. | Causal | Stable |
> |---|---|---|---|---|
> | $\log(\lvert n\rvert+1)\,x[n]$ | **Yes** | **No** | **Yes** | **No** |
> | $x[n]*u[n+1]$ | **Yes** | **Yes** | **No** | **No** |
> | $x[n]+3$ | **No** | **Yes** | **Yes** | **Yes** |
>
> - Row 1: time-varying gain; $x = 1$ gives $y = \log(\lvert n\rvert + 1) \to \infty$.
> - Row 2: $h = u[n+1]$, $h[-1] = 1$; $\sum\lvert h\rvert = \infty$.
> - Row 3: $x = 0$ gives $y = 3$; $\lvert y\rvert \le B + 3$.

> [!trap] Where points go
> - Row 1: logarithmic growth is still unbounded. More: [[exams/midterm-1/system-property-bank|system-property bank]].

## Problem 3 · Convolution (12 pts)

> [!question] Problem 3
> Calculate $y[n] = x[n]*h[n]$.
>
> (a) $\{x[n]\}_{n=0}^{4} = \{\underset{\uparrow}{1},\ 2,\ 3,\ 2,\ 1\}$, $\quad \{h[n]\}_{n=0}^{1} = \{\underset{\uparrow}{-1},\ 1\}$
>
> (b) $x[n] = \left(\tfrac34\right)^n u[n]$, $\quad h[n] = 2u[n] - u[n-1] - u[n-2]$

> [!success]- Solution 3
> **(a)** $h = -\delta[n] + \delta[n-1]$, $y[n] = x[n-1] - x[n]$, $n = 0,\dots,5$:
> $$
> \boldsymbol{y[n] = \{\underset{\uparrow}{-1},\ -1,\ -1,\ 1,\ 1,\ 1\}}
> $$
> **(b)** $h = 2$ at $n = 0$, $2 - 1 = 1$ at $n = 1$, $2 - 1 - 1 = 0$ for $n \ge 2$: $h = 2\delta[n] + \delta[n-1]$:
> $$
> \boldsymbol{y[n] = 2\left(\tfrac34\right)^n u[n] + \left(\tfrac34\right)^{n-1}u[n-1]}
> $$

> [!trap] Where points go
> - (b): evaluate a sum of steps at a few $n$ first; it is a 2-tap FIR.

## Problem 4 · Which $h$? (5 pts)

> [!question] Problem 4
> An LTI system with impulse response $h[n]$ responds to $x[n] = u[n]$ with $y[n] = \delta[n] + \delta[n-1]$. Which sequence is $h[n]$? (Circle one)
>
> (a) $\{\underset{\uparrow}{1},\ 0,\ 1\}$ · (b) $\{\underset{\uparrow}{1},\ 0,\ -1\}$ · (c) $\{\underset{\uparrow}{1},\ 1\}$ · (d) $\{\underset{\uparrow}{1},\ -1\}$

> [!success]- Solution 4
> $h[n] = s[n] - s[n-1] = \delta[n] + \delta[n-1] - \delta[n-1] - \delta[n-2] = \delta[n] - \delta[n-2]$.
>
> **Answer (b): $h[n] = \{\underset{\uparrow}{1},\ 0,\ -1\}$.** (Check: $u[n] - u[n-2] = \delta[n] + \delta[n-1]$ ✓.)

> [!trap] Where points go
> - (c) is the step response, not $h$ ([[problems/finding-h-from-input-output-pairs|finding h]]).

## Problem 5 · z-transform of $n\,u[n+1]$ (9 pts)

> [!question] Problem 5
> Calculate the z-transform $X(z)$ of $x[n] = n\,u[n+1]$.

> [!success]- Solution 5
> $x[-1] = -1$, $x[0] = 0$: $x[n] = -\delta[n+1] + n\,u[n]$:
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> \boldsymbol{X(z)} &\boldsymbol{= -z + \frac{z^{-1}}{(1-z^{-1})^2} = \frac{1}{(1-z^{-1})^2} - \frac{z}{1-z^{-1}}}\\[4pt]
> &\boldsymbol{= \frac{-z(1-2z^{-1})}{(1-z^{-1})^2},\qquad 1 < \lvert z\rvert < \infty}
> \end{aligned}
> $$
> (Key's routes: $(n+1)u[n+1] - u[n+1]$, or $X = -z\,\frac{d}{dz}\frac{z}{1-z^{-1}}$.)

> [!trap] Where points go
> - The $z^{+1}$ term excludes $z = \infty$. $n\,u[n+1]$ is not $n\,u[n]$ advanced.

## Problem 6 · All possible ROCs (20 pts)

> [!question] Problem 6
> An LTI system has transfer function
> $$
> H(z) = \frac{1 - z^{-1}}{\left(1 - \frac12 z^{-1}\right)\left(1 - 2z^{-1}\right)} .
> $$
> (a) Determine all possible ROCs and the corresponding impulse response $h[n]$ for each ROC.
>
> (b) Determine the corresponding difference equation.

> [!success]- Solution 6(a)
> $A_1 = \left.\dfrac{1-z^{-1}}{1-2z^{-1}}\right|_{z=1/2} = \dfrac{-1}{-3} = \tfrac13$, $\;A_2 = \left.\dfrac{1-z^{-1}}{1-\frac12 z^{-1}}\right|_{z=2} = \dfrac{1/2}{3/4} = \tfrac23$; $H = \dfrac{1/3}{1-\frac12 z^{-1}} + \dfrac{2/3}{1-2z^{-1}}$.
>
> | ROC | pole $\tfrac12$ term | pole $2$ term | $h[n]$ | system |
> |---|---|---|---|---|
> | $\lvert z\rvert > 2$ | right | right | $\tfrac13(\tfrac12)^n u[n] + \tfrac23\,2^n u[n]$ | causal, unstable |
> | $\lvert z\rvert < \tfrac12$ | left | left | $-\tfrac13(\tfrac12)^n u[-n-1] - \tfrac23\,2^n u[-n-1]$ | anti-causal, unstable |
> | $\tfrac12 < \lvert z\rvert < 2$ | right | left | $\tfrac13(\tfrac12)^n u[n] - \tfrac23\,2^n u[-n-1]$ | two-sided, **stable** |
>
> The fourth combination needs $\lvert z\rvert < \tfrac12$ and $\lvert z\rvert > 2$: empty.

> [!success]- Solution 6(b)
> $\left(1 - \tfrac12 z^{-1}\right)\left(1 - 2z^{-1}\right) = 1 - \tfrac52 z^{-1} + z^{-2}$:
> $$
> \boldsymbol{y[n] - \tfrac52\,y[n-1] + y[n-2] = x[n] - x[n-1]}
> $$
> Same for all three ROCs.

> [!trap] Where points go
> - Two poles give three ROCs; minus on left-sided terms ([[problems/all-possible-rocs|all possible ROCs]]).

## Problem 7 · Causal system with a pole on the unit circle (20 pts)

> [!question] Problem 7
> A **causal** LTI system has
> $$
> H(z) = \frac{1 - 3z^{-1} + 2z^{-2}}{1 + \frac34 z^{-1} - \frac14 z^{-2}} .
> $$
> (a) Determine the poles, zeros, and ROC of the system.
>
> (b) Calculate the system response $y[n]$ to the input $x[n] = u[n]$.
>
> (c) Is the system BIBO stable? If not, give an example of a bounded input that will produce an unbounded output.

> [!success]- Solution 7(a)
> $$
> H(z) = \frac{(1-2z^{-1})(1-z^{-1})}{(1-\frac14 z^{-1})(1+z^{-1})}
> $$
> **Poles $z = \tfrac14,\ -1$; zeros $z = 1,\ 2$; ROC $\lvert z\rvert > 1$.**

> [!success]- Solution 7(b)
> $X(z) = \dfrac{1}{1-z^{-1}}$ cancels the zero at 1:
> $$
> Y(z) = \frac{1 - 2z^{-1}}{(1-\frac14 z^{-1})(1+z^{-1})} = \frac{-7/5}{1-\frac14 z^{-1}} + \frac{12/5}{1+z^{-1}},\qquad \lvert z\rvert > 1
> $$
> ($A_1 = \frac{1-8}{1+4} = -\frac75$; $A_2 = \frac{1+2}{1+\frac14} = \frac{12}{5}$.)
> $$
> \boldsymbol{y[n] = -\tfrac75\left(\tfrac14\right)^n u[n] + \tfrac{12}{5}(-1)^n u[n]}
> $$

> [!success]- Solution 7(c)
> **Not stable**: pole at $-1$ on the unit circle. Bounded input $\boldsymbol{x[n] = (-1)^n u[n] = \cos(\pi n)u[n]}$ makes a double pole at $-1$; $y$ grows like $n$.

> [!trap] Where points go
> - (c): a bounded step response proves nothing; stability is about every bounded input ([[concepts/marginal-stability|marginal stability]]).

## Problem 8 · Stability parameter, causal vs two-sided (10 pts)

> [!question] Problem 8
> An LTI system is described by
> $$
> y[n] - \tfrac23\,y[n-1] - \tfrac89\,y[n-2] = 3x[n] + \alpha\,x[n-1],
> $$
> where $\alpha$ is a finite, real-valued constant and a transfer function $H(z)$ exists for the system.
>
> (a) Assuming the system is **causal**, for what value(s) of $\alpha$ is it BIBO stable?
>
> (b) Assuming the system is **non-causal** and **two-sided**, for what value(s) of $\alpha$ is this non-causal system BIBO stable?

> [!success]- Solution 8
> $1 - \tfrac23 z^{-1} - \tfrac89 z^{-2} = \left(1 - \tfrac43 z^{-1}\right)\left(1 + \tfrac23 z^{-1}\right)$:
> $$
> H(z) = \frac{3 + \alpha z^{-1}}{\left(1 - \frac43 z^{-1}\right)\left(1 + \frac23 z^{-1}\right)},\qquad \text{poles } \tfrac43,\ -\tfrac23;\ \text{zero } z = -\tfrac{\alpha}{3}.
> $$
> **(a)** ROC $\lvert z\rvert > \tfrac43$; cancel the pole: $-\tfrac{\alpha}{3} = \tfrac43$, $\boldsymbol{\alpha = -4}$ (then $H = \dfrac{3}{1+\frac23 z^{-1}}$, $\lvert z\rvert > \tfrac23$).
>
> **(b)** ROC $\tfrac23 < \lvert z\rvert < \tfrac43$ contains the unit circle: stable **for all $\alpha$** (strictly, except $\alpha = -4$ or $2$, which cancel a pole and leave no two-sided version).

> [!trap] Where points go
> - (b): the annulus makes the pole at $\tfrac43$ harmless. Zero is at $-\alpha/3$, not $-\alpha$ ([[problems/parameters-for-stability|parameters for stability]]).
