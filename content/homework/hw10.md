> [!abstract] In one breath
> - A branch weight in an FFT diagram is fixed by three things: the length its stage merges to, the index $k$ inside the group, and which of the four branches of the butterfly it is.
> - An $N$-point circular convolution is the linear one with everything from index $N$ on folded back onto the start.
> - To convolve with DFTs, pad both sequences to the same length, at least (length of $x$) + (length of $h$) − 1; a radix-2 FFT needs the next power of 2.

> [!success]- Solution
> A butterfly of a stage that merges to length $N$ joins rows $k$ and $k+\frac N2$ of its group:
> $$
> X[k]=E[k]+W_N^kO[k],\qquad X\!\left[k+\tfrac N2\right]=E[k]-W_N^kO[k],\qquad W_N^k=e^{-j\frac{2\pi k}{N}}.
> $$
> Four branch types: upper → upper $=1$, upper → lower $=1$, lower → upper $=W_N^k$, lower → lower $=-W_N^k$ ([[concepts/butterfly-diagram|butterfly diagram]]). For each weight find the stage (it fixes $N$), the pair of rows (it fixes $k$), then the branch type. Rows are numbered 0–15 from the top.
>
> **Stages.** The right-hand butterflies join rows 8 apart: the last stage, $N=16$. The left-hand butterflies join rows 4 apart: the stage before it, $N=8$, working inside the groups of rows 0–7 and 8–15.
>
> - **$a$:** middle row 8 → right row 8, last stage. Row 8 belongs to the pair $(0,8)$, so $k=0$, and it is the lower row at both ends: lower → lower.
> $$
> a=-W_{16}^{0}=-1.
> $$
> - **$b$:** middle row 14 → right row 6, last stage. Pair $(6,14)$, $k=6$; from the lower row to the upper row.
> $$
> b=W_{16}^{6}=e^{-j\frac{2\pi\cdot6}{16}}=e^{-j\frac{3\pi}{4}}=-\frac{\sqrt2}{2}-j\frac{\sqrt2}{2}.
> $$
> - **$c$:** left row 14 → middle row 10, the $N=8$ stage. Inside the group of rows 8–15 the upper half is rows 8–11 and the lower half rows 12–15; row 14 pairs with row 10, the third pair, so $k=2$; lower → upper.
> $$
> c=W_8^{2}=e^{-j\frac{2\pi\cdot2}{8}}=e^{-j\frac{\pi}{2}}=-j.
> $$

## Problem 2 — linear and circular convolution

> [!question] Problem 2
> Consider the two finite-length sequences $x=\{\underset{\uparrow}{1},\ 2,\ 3,\ 4,\ 5\}$ and $h=\{\underset{\uparrow}{1},\ 1\}$.
>
> (a) Compute the linear convolution $x*h$.
>
> (b) Compute the circular convolution $x\circledast_5h$.
>
> (c) What is the smallest value of $N$ so that $N$-point circular convolution is equal to the linear convolution?

> [!success]- Solution (a)
> $h=\{1,1\}$ means $y[n]=x[n]+x[n-1]$: each output is a sample plus the one before it. Length $5+2-1=6$.
> $$
> y[0]=1,\quad y[1]=2+1=3,\quad y[2]=3+2=5,\quad y[3]=4+3=7,\quad y[4]=5+4=9,\quad y[5]=5.
> $$
> $$
> x*h=\{\underset{\uparrow}{1},\ 3,\ 5,\ 7,\ 9,\ 5\}
> $$

> [!success]- Solution (b)
> Pad $h$ to length 5, $h=\{1,1,0,0,0\}$, and use $y_c[n]=\sum_kx[k]\,h[\langle n-k\rangle_5]$. Easier: the 5-point circular convolution is the linear result with index 5 wrapped onto index 0 (since $\langle5\rangle_5=0$):
> $$
> y_c[0]=y[0]+y[5]=1+5=6,\qquad y_c[n]=y[n]\ \text{for }n=1,2,3,4.
> $$
> $$
> x\circledast_5h=\{\underset{\uparrow}{6},\ 3,\ 5,\ 7,\ 9\}
> $$
> Directly: $y_c[0]=x[0]h[0]+x[4]h[1]=1+5=6$, because $h[\langle0-4\rangle_5]=h[1]$.

> [!success]- Solution (c)
> Nothing wraps once $N$ is at least the length of the linear convolution:
> $$
> N\ge5+2-1=6\quad\Rightarrow\quad N=6.
> $$
> With $N=5$ exactly one sample wraps, which is part (b).

## Problem 3 — zero padding for the DFT method

> [!question] Problem 3
> The following linear convolution $\{x_n\}_{n=0}^{46}*\{h_n\}_{n=0}^{32}$ is to be evaluated using the DFT method. Namely,
> $$
> \{x_n\}_{n=0}^{46}*\{h_n\}_{n=0}^{32}=\mathrm{DFT}^{-1}\{\mathrm{DFT}\{x_n\}\cdot\mathrm{DFT}\{h_n\}\}.
> $$
> (a) Determine the minimum number of zeros that should be padded to $\{x_n\}$ and $\{h_n\}$, respectively, before the DFTs are applied.
>
> (b) If the DFTs are to be calculated with a radix-2 FFT algorithm, how many zeros should now be padded to $\{x_n\}$ and $\{h_n\}$, respectively?
>
> (c) In (a), can the zeros be padded at the beginning (instead of the end) of the sequences? If so, how do you obtain $\{x_n\}_{n=0}^{46}*\{h_n\}_{n=0}^{32}$ from $\mathrm{DFT}^{-1}\{\mathrm{DFT}\{x_n\}\cdot\mathrm{DFT}\{h_n\}\}$?

> [!success]- Solution (a)
> Indices 0–46 are 47 samples; indices 0–32 are 33 samples. The convolution has $47+33-1=79$ samples, so both must be padded to $N=79$:
> $$
> x:\ 79-47=32\ \text{zeros},\qquad h:\ 79-33=46\ \text{zeros}.
> $$

> [!success]- Solution (b)
> $N$ must also be a power of 2: $2^6=64\lt79\le2^7=128$, so $N=128$:
> $$
> x:\ 128-47=81\ \text{zeros},\qquad h:\ 128-33=95\ \text{zeros}.
> $$

> [!success]- Solution (c)
> **Yes, but the result comes out rotated.** Let $x_e$, $h_e$ be the sequences padded at the end, as in (a), and $y=x*h$. Putting $p$ zeros in front of $x$ instead is the same as circularly shifting $x_e$ to the right by $p$ (the zeros that were at the end reappear at the front): $x'[n]=x_e[\langle n-p\rangle_N]$. Likewise $h'[n]=h_e[\langle n-q\rangle_N]$. A circular shift multiplies the DFT by a phase ramp ([[concepts/dft-properties|DFT properties]]), and the two ramps multiply:
> $$
> \mathrm{DFT}\{x'\}\cdot\mathrm{DFT}\{h'\}=e^{-j\frac{2\pi k(p+q)}{N}}X_e[k]H_e[k]\quad\Rightarrow\quad r[n]=\mathrm{IDFT}\{\dots\}=y[\langle n-(p+q)\rangle_N].
> $$
> Here $p=32$, $q=46$, $N=79$: the total shift is $p+q=78$, and a shift by 78 is a shift by $-1$ modulo 79. So $r[n]=y[\langle n+1\rangle_{79}]$, the correct convolution rotated left by one sample, and
> $$
> y[n]=r[\langle n-1\rangle_{79}]:\qquad y[0]=r[78],\qquad y[n]=r[n-1]\ \text{for }n=1,\dots,78.
> $$
> Circularly shift the inverse DFT to the right by one sample to recover $x*h$.

**On the exam:** [[exams/midterm-2/past-exams/fall-2019|FA2019 MT2 #11]] is parts (a) and (b) with lengths 15 and 8 (answers 7 and 14; 17 and 24).

> [!trap] Traps
> - **Count samples, not the last index.** $\{x_n\}_{n=0}^{46}$ has 47 samples.
> - **Same $N$ for both**, hence different numbers of zeros. Padding only the shorter sequence up to the longer one is not enough.
> - **Rows versus $k$** in Problem 1: row 14 is $k=6$ in the last stage and $k=2$ in the stage before it.
> - In 3(c) the shift is the **sum** of the two front paddings, taken modulo $N$.
> - $x\circledast_5h$ pads $h$ to 5 first; the result has 5 samples, not 6.
