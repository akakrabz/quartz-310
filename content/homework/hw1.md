## Problem 1 · Plotting signals built from impulses and steps

> [!question] Problem 1 (18 pts)
> Plot each of the following discrete-time signals for $-5 \le n \le 5$. Recall that $\delta[n]$ is the unit impulse (Kronecker delta) and $u[n]$ is the unit step.
>
> - (a) $\delta[n+4] - \delta[n+1] + 3\delta[n] + 2\delta[n-2]$
> - (b) $\left(\tfrac{2}{3}\right)^{n}\left(u[n+2] - u[n-3]\right)$
> - (c) $n\,u[n]\,u[n-4]$

> [!success]- Solution
> Read each building block as a statement about *where* the samples are:
>
> - $\delta[n-k]$ is one sample at $n = k$. The sign inside the bracket is flipped: $\delta[n+4]$ sits at $n = -4$.
> - $u[n-k]$ switches on at $n = k$, so $u[n+2]-u[n-3]$ is a **window**: on for $n = -2,\dots,2$ (on at $-2$, off from $3$).
> - A **product** of steps is on only where both are on: $u[n]\,u[n-4] = u[n-4]$.
>
> **(a)** Four samples: $x[-4] = 1$, $x[-1] = -1$, $x[0] = 3$, $x[2] = 2$, and zero elsewhere.
>
> **(b)** $(2/3)^n$ on $n = -2,\dots,2$: $\ \tfrac94,\ \tfrac32,\ \underset{\uparrow}{1},\ \tfrac23,\ \tfrac49$, and zero elsewhere. The samples at negative $n$ exceed 1, because $(2/3)^{-2} = (3/2)^2$.
>
> **(c)** $n\,u[n-4]$ is zero for $n \le 3$, then $4$ at $n = 4$ and $5$ at $n = 5$ (it keeps growing, $6, 7, \dots$, past the plotted range).

## Problem 2 · Time reversal, shift, and decimation

> [!question] Problem 2 (14 pts)
> Let $x[n]$ be the length-8 discrete-time signal
>
> $$
> x[n] = \{2,\ 4,\ \underset{\uparrow}{1},\ 0,\ -2,\ 5,\ 7,\ 3\}
> $$
>
> (the arrow marks $n = 0$, so $x$ occupies $-2 \le n \le 5$).
>
> - (a) Sketch the signal $y[n] = x[-n+3]$.
> - (b) Sketch the signal $z[n] = x[2n+1]$.

> [!success]- Solution
> The error-proof method is a table: for each output index $n$, compute the argument, then look $x$ up at that argument. Here $x[-2], \dots, x[5] = 2, 4, 1, 0, -2, 5, 7, 3$.
>
> | $n$ | $-2$ | $-1$ | $0$ | $1$ | $2$ | $3$ | $4$ | $5$ |
> |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
> | $-n+3$ | $5$ | $4$ | $3$ | $2$ | $1$ | $0$ | $-1$ | $-2$ |
> | $y[n] = x[-n+3]$ | $3$ | $7$ | $5$ | $-2$ | $0$ | $1$ | $4$ | $2$ |
>
> **(a)** $\ y[n] = \{3,\ 7,\ \underset{\uparrow}{5},\ -2,\ 0,\ 1,\ 4,\ 2\}$, nonzero only on $-2 \le n \le 5$.
>
> Cross-check with operations: $x[-n+3] = x[-(n-3)]$ means "flip, then delay by 3". Flipping keeps $x[0] = 1$ at $n = 0$ and reverses the order; the delay moves it to $n = 3$. The one-line sanity check: $x[0]$ lands where the argument is zero, $-n + 3 = 0 \Rightarrow n = 3$, and indeed $y[3] = 1$.
>
> | $n$ | $-1$ | $0$ | $1$ | $2$ |
> |---|---|---|---|---|
> | $2n+1$ | $-1$ | $1$ | $3$ | $5$ |
> | $z[n] = x[2n+1]$ | $4$ | $0$ | $5$ | $3$ |
>
> **(b)** $\ z[n] = \{4,\ \underset{\uparrow}{0},\ 5,\ 3\}$, nonzero only on $-1 \le n \le 2$.
>
> Since $2n+1$ is always odd, $z$ keeps the odd-indexed samples $x[-1], x[1], x[3], x[5]$ and discards $x[-2], x[0], x[2], x[4]$ for good: decimation loses information. For $n \le -2$ or $n \ge 3$ the argument leaves $-2 \le 2n+1 \le 5$, so $z[n] = 0$ there.
>
> *Rubric (7 pts per part):* a minor slip (axis labels, a small numerical error) costs 1 point; a major mistake costs 4. The plot must show the values and where $n = 0$ is.

> [!trap] "+3 means shift left"
> In $x[-n+3]$ the $+3$ alone does not tell you the direction. "Advance by 3 ($x[n+3]$), then flip" and "flip, then *delay* by 3" are both correct readings; mixing the two is the classic error. The table (or the "where does $x[0]$ land" check) never fails. In (b), $z[0] = x[1] = 0$, not $x[0] = 1$.

The same bookkeeping in Python: store $x$ as a dictionary keyed by $n$, so the index origin cannot get lost.

```python
x = dict(zip(range(-2, 6), [2, 4, 1, 0, -2, 5, 7, 3]))   # x[-2..5]; x[0] = 1
y = lambda n: x.get(-n + 3, 0)                           # y[n] = x[-n+3]
z = lambda n: x.get(2 * n + 1, 0)                        # z[n] = x[2n+1]
print("y, n = -3..6:", [y(n) for n in range(-3, 7)])
print("z, n = -2..3:", [z(n) for n in range(-2, 4)])
```

```text
y, n = -3..6: [0, 3, 7, 5, -2, 0, 1, 4, 2, 0]
z, n = -2..3: [0, 4, 0, 5, 3, 0]
```

**On the exam:** flip-and-shift is exactly the $h[n-k]$ step inside every convolution sum ([[problems/finite-length-convolution|finite-length convolution]], on 7/7 past exams), and index maps such as $x[\lvert n\rvert + n]$ ([[0-midterm-1/past-exams/fall-2019|FA2019 #2]]) and $2x[\lvert n\rvert] + 10$ ([[0-midterm-1/past-exams/spring-2025|SP2025 #2]]) are where "time-varying" and "non-causal" answers come from in the property table.

>
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> \text{(i)}\quad x[n] &= 3\big(\delta[n+3] + \delta[n+2] + \delta[n+1] + \delta[n] + \delta[n-1]\big)\\
> \text{(ii)}\quad x[n] &= 3\big(u[n+3] - u[n-2]\big)
> \end{aligned}
> $$
>
> The "off" step sits at the first zero *after* the pulse ($n = 2$), not at the last nonzero sample.
>
> **(b)** $y[n] = 2$ for $n = -3, \dots, 0$ and $y[n] = -1$ for $n = 1, \dots, 4$:
>
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> \text{(i)}\quad y[n] &= 2\big(\delta[n+3] + \delta[n+2] + \delta[n+1] + \delta[n]\big)\\
> &\quad - \big(\delta[n-1] + \delta[n-2] + \delta[n-3] + \delta[n-4]\big)\\
> \text{(ii)}\quad y[n] &= 2u[n+3] - 3u[n-1] + u[n-5]\\
> &= 2\big(u[n+3] - u[n-1]\big) - \big(u[n-1] - u[n-5]\big)
> \end{aligned}
> $$
>
> Read (ii) off the jumps from left to right: $+2$ at $n = -3$ (0 to 2), $-3$ at $n = 1$ (2 to $-1$), $+1$ at $n = 5$ ($-1$ back to 0).
>
> *Rubric:* 4 points for each of the four expressions; a minor slip costs 1, a major one 2.

> [!recipe] Steps from jumps
> Scan left to right. Every time the level changes by $J$ at index $n_0$, add $J\,u[n-n_0]$. For a finite-length signal the step coefficients must add up to zero, because the signal returns to 0: $3 - 3 = 0$ in (a), $2 - 3 + 1 = 0$ in (b). If they don't, a jump is missing.

> [!trap] Off-by-one on the "off" step
> $3(u[n+3] - u[n-1])$ covers only $n = -3, \dots, 0$ and silently drops the sample at $n = 1$. The second step starts at the first zero after the pulse.

**On the exam:** step forms are what you feed a z-transform: [[0-midterm-1/past-exams/fall-2025|FA2025 #5(b)]] $u[n] - u[n-8] \to \sum_{k=0}^{7} z^{-k}$, [[0-midterm-1/past-exams/spring-2025|SP2025 #5(a)]] $x[n] = \sum_{k=0}^{2} (1/2)^k u[n-k]$, [[0-midterm-1/past-exams/fall-2019|FA2019 #5(a)]] $3^{-n}(u[n-5] - u[n-100])$. ## Problem 4 · The roots of z⁴ = 1 and z⁴ = −1

> [!question] Problem 4 (16 pts)
> For each of the following parts, (i) determine the value(s) of $z$ that satisfy the equation and (ii) plot these values of $z$ on the complex plane. *Hint:* complex numbers in polar form may be written as $Re^{j\theta} = Re^{j(\theta_p + 2\pi k)}$, where $\theta_p$ is the principal angle and $k$ is some integer.
>
> - (a) $z^4 - 1 = 0$
> - (b) $z^4 + 1 = 0$

> [!success]- Solution
> Write the right-hand side with *all* of its angles, take the 4th root of the magnitude (here 1) and divide every angle by 4. Keep $k = 0, 1, 2, 3$: $k = 4$ adds $2\pi$ to the angle and repeats $k = 0$. A degree-4 equation has exactly four roots.
>
> **(a)** $z^4 = 1 = e^{j2\pi k} \;\Rightarrow\; z = e^{j\pi k/2}$:
>
> $$
> z \in \{1,\ j,\ -1,\ -j\}.
> $$
>
> **(b)** $z^4 = -1 = e^{j(\pi + 2\pi k)} \;\Rightarrow\; z = e^{j(\pi/4 + \pi k/2)}$:
>
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> z &\in \left\{e^{j\pi/4},\ e^{j3\pi/4},\ e^{-j3\pi/4},\ e^{-j\pi/4}\right\}\\
> &= \left\{\tfrac{1+j}{\sqrt2},\ \tfrac{-1+j}{\sqrt2},\ \tfrac{-1-j}{\sqrt2},\ \tfrac{1-j}{\sqrt2}\right\}.
> \end{aligned}
> $$
>

## Problem 6 · The windowing system

> [!question] Problem 6 (18 pts)
> Another common operation in digital systems is *windowing*: extracting $N$ samples in a given range of indices and discarding the samples outside this range. For an input $x[n]$ and output $y[n]$, the windowing system is
>
> $$
> y[n] = \begin{cases} x[n], & 0 \le n \le N-1 \\ 0, & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}
> $$

> - (a) Let $x[n] = \{-2,\ -1,\ \underset{\uparrow}{0},\ 1,\ 2,\ 3,\ 4,\ 5,\ 6\}$. Sketch $y[n]$ for $N = 4$.

> **(b) Linear.** Write the system as multiplication by a fixed 0/1 sequence, $y[n] = w[n]\,x[n]$ with $w[n] = u[n] - u[n-N]$. For any inputs $x_1, x_2$ and constants $\alpha, \beta$:
>
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> T\{\alpha x_1 + \beta x_2\}[n] &= w[n]\big(\alpha x_1[n] + \beta x_2[n]\big) = \alpha\,w[n]\,x_1[n] + \beta\,w[n]\,x_2[n]\\
> &= \alpha\,T\{x_1\}[n] + \beta\,T\{x_2\}[n].
> \end{aligned}
> $$
>
> **(c) Time-varying.** The window is nailed to $0 \le n \le N-1$; it does not move with the input. Counterexample: $x[n] = \delta[n]$ gives $y[n] = \delta[n]$. Delay the input by $N$: $\delta[n-N]$ falls outside the window, so $T\{\delta[n-N]\} = 0$, whereas time invariance would require the output $y[n-N] = \delta[n-N] \ne 0$.
>
> With the part-(a) input delayed by $n_0 = 1$ (the official choice): $T\{x[n-1]\} = \{\underset{\uparrow}{-1},\ 0,\ 1,\ 2\}$, while $y[n-1] = \{\underset{\uparrow}{0},\ 0,\ 1,\ 2,\ 3\}$. They differ at $n = 0$ and at $n = 4$.
>
> **Exam-table row:** linear **Y** · time-invariant **N** · causal **Y** (memoryless) · stable **Y** ($\lvert y[n]\rvert \le \lvert x[n]\rvert$).
>
> *Rubric:* (a) 4 pts; (b) and (c) 7 pts each, scored as in problem 5.

> [!warning] A slip in the official comparison for 6(c)
> The key's last line reads $\{\underset{\uparrow}{0}, 1, 2, 3\} \ne \{\underset{\uparrow}{-1}, 0, 1, 2\}$: it compares the *unshifted* output $y[n]$ with $T\{x[n-1]\}$. The time-invariance test compares $y[n-1] = \{\underset{\uparrow}{0}, 0, 1, 2, 3\}$ with $T\{x[n-1]\} = \{\underset{\uparrow}{-1}, 0, 1, 2\}$. These differ too, so the verdict (time-varying) stands; just write the right pair on an exam.

> [!intuition] Two fast tests for the property table
> - $n$ appears **outside** the brackets of $x$ (a gain that depends on $n$, a window, a fixed time reference): suspect **time-varying**, then confirm with a shifted $\delta$.
> - $x$ sits **inside** a nonlinear operation (clipping, $\lvert\cdot\rvert$, $e^{(\cdot)}$, adding a constant): suspect **non-linear**, then confirm with a scaled input.
>
> Clipping is the second kind, windowing the first.

**On the exam:** gains that depend on $n$ are the classic "linear yes, time-invariant no" rows: $\lvert n\rvert\, x[n]$ ([[0-midterm-1/past-exams/fall-2025|FA2025 #2]], Y N Y N), $x[n]/(\lvert n\rvert + 1)$ ([[0-midterm-1/past-exams/fall-2024|FA2024 #2]], Y N Y Y), $x[n]\cos(\pi(n-2)/3)$ ([[0-midterm-1/past-exams/spring-2021|SP2021 #3]], Y N Y Y). The T/F version: [[0-midterm-1/past-exams/fall-2023|FA2023 1(a)]], "any causal system must also be time-invariant", is False (take $y[n] = n\,x[n]$). More in [[problems/classifying-system-properties|classifying system properties]] and the [[0-midterm-1/true-false-bank|T/F bank]].
