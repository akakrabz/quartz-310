## Problem 1 · Plotting signals built from impulses and steps

> [!question] Problem 1 (18 pts)
> Plot each of the following discrete-time signals for $-5 \le n \le 5$. Recall that $\delta[n]$ is the unit impulse (Kronecker delta) and $u[n]$ is the unit step.
>
> - (a) $\delta[n+4] - \delta[n+1] + 3\delta[n] + 2\delta[n-2]$
> - (b) $\left(\tfrac{2}{3}\right)^{n}\left(u[n+2] - u[n-3]\right)$
> - (c) $n\,u[n]\,u[n-4]$

> [!success]- Solution
> - $\delta[n-k]$: one sample at $n = k$ ($\delta[n+4]$ sits at $n = -4$).
> - $u[n+2]-u[n-3]$: window on $n = -2,\dots,2$.
> - $u[n]\,u[n-4] = u[n-4]$ (product: on where both are on).
>
> **(a)** $x[-4] = 1$, $x[-1] = -1$, $x[0] = 3$, $x[2] = 2$, zero elsewhere.
>
> **(b)** $\tfrac94,\ \tfrac32,\ \underset{\uparrow}{1},\ \tfrac23,\ \tfrac49$ on $n = -2,\dots,2$, zero elsewhere.
>
> **(c)** Zero for $n \le 3$; $4$ at $n = 4$, $5$ at $n = 5$ (continues $6, 7, \dots$).

## Problem 2 · Time reversal, shift, and decimation

> [!question] Problem 2 (14 pts)
> Let $x[n]$ be the length-8 discrete-time signal
>
> $$
> x[n] = \{2,\ 4,\ \underset{\uparrow}{1},\ 0,\ -2,\ 5,\ 7,\ 3\}
> $$
>
> (the arrow marks $n = 0$, so $x$ occupies $-2 \le n \le 5$).
>
> - (a) Sketch the signal $y[n] = x[-n+3]$.
> - (b) Sketch the signal $z[n] = x[2n+1]$.

> [!success]- Solution
> Tabulate the argument for each $n$, then look up $x$.
>
> | $n$ | $-2$ | $-1$ | $0$ | $1$ | $2$ | $3$ | $4$ | $5$ |
> |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
> | $-n+3$ | $5$ | $4$ | $3$ | $2$ | $1$ | $0$ | $-1$ | $-2$ |

>

> \begin{aligned}
> \text{(i)}\quad x[n] &= 3\big(\delta[n+3] + \delta[n+2] + \delta[n+1] + \delta[n] + \delta[n-1]\big)\\
> \text{(ii)}\quad x[n] &= 3\big(u[n+3] - u[n-2]\big)
> \end{aligned}
> $$
>
> **(b)** $y[n] = 2$ for $n = -3, \dots, 0$ and $y[n] = -1$ for $n = 1, \dots, 4$:
>
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> \text{(i)}\quad y[n] &= 2\big(\delta[n+3] + \delta[n+2] + \delta[n+1] + \delta[n]\big)\\
> &\quad - \big(\delta[n-1] + \delta[n-2] + \delta[n-3] + \delta[n-4]\big)\\
> \text{(ii)}\quad y[n] &= 2u[n+3] - 3u[n-1] + u[n-5]\\
> &= 2\big(u[n+3] - u[n-1]\big) - \big(u[n-1] - u[n-5]\big)
> \end{aligned}
> $$
>
> Steps from jumps: add $J\,u[n-n_0]$ for each level change $J$ at $n_0$; "off" step at the first zero after the pulse. Coefficients of a finite-length signal sum to zero: $3 - 3 = 0$, $2 - 3 + 1 = 0$.

window keeps x[0], …, x[3] and zeroes the rest.</strong> It stays at n = 0,…,3 wherever the input sits: time-varying.</figcaption></figure>
>
> **(b) Linear.** $y[n] = w[n]\,x[n]$ with $w[n] = u[n] - u[n-N]$:
>
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> T\{\alpha x_1 + \beta x_2\}[n] &= w[n]\big(\alpha x_1[n] + \beta x_2[n]\big) = \alpha\,w[n]\,x_1[n] + \beta\,w[n]\,x_2[n]\\
> &= \alpha\,T\{x_1\}[n] + \beta\,T\{x_2\}[n].
> \end{aligned}
> $$
>
> **(c) Time-varying.** $x[n] = \delta[n] \to y[n] = \delta[n]$, but $T\{\delta[n-N]\} = 0 \ne y[n-N] = \delta[n-N]$.
>
> **Table row:** linear **Y** · time-invariant **N** · causal **Y** (memoryless) · stable **Y** ($\lvert y[n]\rvert \le \lvert x[n]\rvert$).

> [!warning] A slip in the official comparison for 6(c)
> Key compares $y[n] = \{\underset{\uparrow}{0}, 1, 2, 3\}$ with $T\{x[n-1]\} = \{\underset{\uparrow}{-1}, 0, 1, 2\}$. The correct pair: $y[n-1] = \{\underset{\uparrow}{0}, 0, 1, 2, 3\}$ vs. $T\{x[n-1]\} = \{\underset{\uparrow}{-1}, 0, 1, 2\}$. Still differ; verdict stands.

> [!trap] Traps
> - $u[n]\,u[n-4] = u[n-4]$; $u[n]-u[n-4]$ is the window $n = 0,\dots,3$.
> - $3(u[n+3] - u[n-1])$ drops the sample at $n = 1$.
> - $z^4 = 1$ has four roots, not just $\pm 1$.
> - Linearity tests with inputs that never clip prove nothing.
