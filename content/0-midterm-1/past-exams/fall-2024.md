## Problem 1 · True/False (12 pts)

> [!question] Problem 1
> Select **True** or **False** for each statement.
>
> (a) For a discrete-time system with impulse response $h[n]$, the system output $y[n]$ to any input signal $x[n]$ is always determined using $h[n]$.
>
> (b) The impulse response $h[n]$ of an unstable LTI system will always be an unbounded sequence.
>
> (c) Two BIBO stable LTI systems connected in parallel will always form a BIBO stable LTI system.
>
> (d) An LCCDE system can only have a finite number of poles.
>
> (e) An LTI system given by $y[n] = y[n-3] + x[n]$ will have three distinct poles in its transfer function.
>
> (f) An LTI system with transfer function $H(z) = \dfrac{1}{1 - 0.5z^{-1}} + \dfrac{1}{1 - 2z^{-1}}$ must **not** be BIBO stable.

> [!success]- Solution 1
> - **(a) False.** Only for LTI systems. $y = x^2[n]$ has $h = \delta^2[n] = \delta[n]$, yet $x = 2\delta[n]$ gives $4\delta[n]$, not $x*h = 2\delta[n]$.
> - **(b) False.** $h = u[n]$ is bounded by 1 but $\sum\lvert h\rvert = \infty$ (unstable).
> - **(c) True.** $h = h_1 + h_2$ and $\sum\lvert h_1+h_2\rvert \le \sum\lvert h_1\rvert + \sum\lvert h_2\rvert < \infty$.
> - **(d) True.** $H(z)$ is a ratio of two polynomials of finite degree, so it has finitely many poles.
> - **(e) True.** $H(z) = \dfrac{1}{1 - z^{-3}} = \dfrac{z^3}{z^3 - 1}$: poles at the three cube roots of unity $1, e^{\pm j2\pi/3}$, all distinct.
> - **(f) False.** No causality is stated: with ROC $\tfrac12 < \lvert z\rvert < 2$, $h = (\tfrac12)^n u[n] - 2^n u[-n-1]$ is absolutely summable.

> [!trap] Where points go
> - **(f)** "A pole outside the unit circle ⇒ unstable" is true only for **causal** systems. Without "causal", you may pick the ROC that contains $\lvert z\rvert = 1$.
> - **(a)** $h[n]$ characterises a system only when it is LTI — the same idea as SP2025 #1(b).
> - **(e)** Poles of $1/(1-z^{-3})$ are the roots of $z^3 = 1$ (three of them), not "a triple pole at 1".

## Problem 2 · System properties (12 pts)

> [!question] Problem 2
> For each system, indicate "yes" or "no" for Linear, Shift-Invariant, Causal, Stable.
>
> 1. $y[n] = x[n]\,x[n+1]$
> 2. $y[n] = \dfrac{1}{\lvert n\rvert + 1}\,x[n]$
> 3. $y[n] = \sin(x[n]) + x[0]$

> [!success]- Solution 2
>
> | system | Linear | Shift-inv. | Causal | Stable |
> |---|---|---|---|---|
> | $x[n]\,x[n+1]$ | **No** | **Yes** | **No** | **Yes** |
> | $x[n]/(\lvert n\rvert+1)$ | **Yes** | **No** | **Yes** | **Yes** |
> | $\sin(x[n]) + x[0]$ | **No** | **No** | **No** | **Yes** |
>
> - Row 1: $2x \mapsto 4y$ ⇒ nonlinear; no explicit $n$ ⇒ SI; needs $x[n+1]$ ⇒ non-causal; $\lvert y\rvert \le B^2$ ⇒ stable.
> - Row 2: a time-varying gain is linear but not SI; memoryless ⇒ causal; the gain is at most 1 ⇒ $\lvert y\rvert \le B$.
> - Row 3: $\sin$ is nonlinear; $x[0]$ pins a fixed instant ⇒ not SI; $y[-5]$ needs $x[0]$, a future sample ⇒ non-causal; $\lvert y\rvert \le 1 + B$.

> [!trap] Where points go
> - **Row 3 causality:** $x[0]$ is in the *past* for $n > 0$ but in the *future* for $n < 0$ — one such $n$ is enough for "No".
> - **Row 2 stability:** a gain that *shrinks* with $\lvert n\rvert$ is harmless; compare FA2025's $\lvert n\rvert x[n]$, which grows. More rows: [[0-midterm-1/system-property-bank|system-property bank]].

## Problem 3 · Series connection: find $h_1$ (8 pts)

> [!question] Problem 3
> Two LTI systems are connected in series: $x[n] \to h_1[n] \to h_2[n] \to y[n]$. The input $x[n] = \{\underset{\uparrow}{1},\ -1\}$ produces the output $y[n] = \{4,\ \underset{\uparrow}{-2},\ -2\}$. We also know that $h_2[n] = \{\underset{\uparrow}{2},\ 1\}$.
>
> (a) Determine $h_1[n]$.
>
> (b) Determine the overall system's impulse response $h[n]$ such that $y[n] = x[n]*h[n]$. Is this overall system causal? Justify your reasoning.

> [!success]- Solution 3
> **(a)** Convolution commutes, so $y = (x*h_2)*h_1$. With $x = \delta[n] - \delta[n-1]$: $x*h_2 = h_2[n] - h_2[n-1] = \{\underset{\uparrow}{2},\ -1,\ -1\}$. The output $\{4,\ \underset{\uparrow}{-2},\ -2\}$ is that sequence **doubled and advanced by one**:
> $$
> \boldsymbol{h_1[n] = 2\delta[n+1]}
> $$
> (z-domain: $Y = 4z - 2 - 2z^{-1} = 2z(1-z^{-1})(2+z^{-1}) = 2z\,X(z)H_2(z)$, so $H_1(z) = 2z$.)
>
> **(b)** $h = h_1 * h_2 = 2h_2[n+1] = \boldsymbol{\{4,\ \underset{\uparrow}{2}\}}$. **Not causal**: $h[-1] = 4 \ne 0$.

> [!trap] Where points go
> - **Series = convolve** the impulse responses (parallel = add). Answering (b) with $h_1 + h_2$ loses the part.
> - The arrow of $y$ is on the **middle** entry, so $y$ starts one sample *before* $x*h_2$ — that is the $\delta[n+1]$.

## Problem 4 · Convolution (10 pts)

> [!question] Problem 4
> Compute $x[n]*h[n]$.
>
> (a) $x[n] = \{1,\ -3,\ \underset{\uparrow}{2},\ -1,\ 4\}$, $\quad h[n] = \{2,\ \underset{\uparrow}{0},\ -1\}$
>
> (b) $x[n] = e^{-n}u[n]$, $\quad h[n] = (n+1)\big(u[n] - u[n-3]\big)$

> [!success]- Solution 4(a)
> $x$ starts at $n = -2$, $h$ at $n = -1$ ⇒ $y$ starts at $n = -3$, length $5 + 3 - 1 = 7$:
> $$
> \boldsymbol{y[n] = \{2,\ -6,\ 3,\ \underset{\uparrow}{1},\ 6,\ 1,\ -4\}}
> $$
> Sum check: $\sum y = 3 = \big(\sum x\big)\big(\sum h\big) = 3 \cdot 1$ ✓.

> [!success]- Solution 4(b)
> $h[n] = (n+1)$ on $n = 0, 1, 2$: $h = \{\underset{\uparrow}{1},\ 2,\ 3\} = \delta[n] + 2\delta[n-1] + 3\delta[n-2]$, so $y = x[n] + 2x[n-1] + 3x[n-2]$:
> $$
> \boldsymbol{y[n] = e^{-n}u[n] + 2e^{-(n-1)}u[n-1] + 3e^{-(n-2)}u[n-2]}
> $$

> [!warning] Answer-key erratum — #4(b)
> The key's boxed line reads "$x[n] = e^{-n}u[n] + 2e^{-(n-1)}u[n-1] + \dots$". The right-hand side is correct, but it is the **output** $y[n] = (x*h)[n]$, not $x[n]$.

> [!trap] Where points go
> - **(a)** The output's start index is $n_x + n_h = -3$; putting the arrow under the first entry of the matrix product is the classic slip.
> - **(b)** $h$ is $1, 2, 3$ (not $0, 1, 2$), and each shifted copy keeps **its own** step: $u[n-1]$, $u[n-2]$. Writing everything with $u[n]$ is wrong at $n = 0, 1$.

## Problem 5 · z-transforms (15 pts)

> [!question] Problem 5
> Find the z-transform and region of convergence.
>
> (a) $x[n] = (n+1)\,u[n-1]$
>
> (b) $x[n] = u[n-1]\,u[3-n]$
>
> (c) $x[n] = 3^{-n}u[n] + 3^{n}u[-n]$

> [!success]- Solution 5(a)
> Split so that the delay property applies cleanly: $x[n] = (n-1)u[n-1] + 2u[n-1]$. With $n\,u[n] \leftrightarrow \dfrac{z^{-1}}{(1-z^{-1})^2}$ and a delay of 1:
> $$
> \boldsymbol{X(z) = \frac{z^{-2}}{(1-z^{-1})^2} + \frac{2z^{-1}}{1-z^{-1}} = \frac{2z^{-1} - z^{-2}}{(1-z^{-1})^2},\qquad \lvert z\rvert > 1}
> $$
> (Method 2: $x = n\,u[n-1] + u[n-1] = n\,u[n] + u[n-1]$ gives $\dfrac{z^{-1}}{(1-z^{-1})^2} + \dfrac{z^{-1}}{1-z^{-1}}$ — the same function.)

> [!success]- Solution 5(b)
> $u[n-1]u[3-n] = 1$ for $1 \le n \le 3$: $x = \delta[n-1] + \delta[n-2] + \delta[n-3]$,
> $$
> \boldsymbol{X(z) = z^{-1} + z^{-2} + z^{-3},\qquad z \ne 0}
> $$

> [!success]- Solution 5(c)
> Right part: $(\tfrac13)^n u[n] \leftrightarrow \dfrac{1}{1-\frac13 z^{-1}}$, $\lvert z\rvert > \tfrac13$. Left part: $3^n u[-n] = \sum_{n\le0}3^n z^{-n} = \sum_{m\ge0}\left(\tfrac z3\right)^m = \dfrac{1}{1 - z/3}$, $\lvert z\rvert < 3$ (equivalently $3\cdot 3^{n-1}u[-(n-1)-1] \leftrightarrow -\dfrac{3z^{-1}}{1-3z^{-1}}$):
> $$
> \boldsymbol{X(z) = \frac{1}{1-\frac13 z^{-1}} - \frac{3z^{-1}}{1-3z^{-1}},\qquad \tfrac13 < \lvert z\rvert < 3}
> $$

> [!trap] Where points go
> - **(a)** Using $n\,u[n]$'s pair on $(n+1)u[n-1]$ directly. Rewrite the sequence first so the multiplier matches the shift.
> - **(c)** $3^n u[-n]$ is **not** the table pair $-a^n u[-n-1]$: it includes $n = 0$ and has no minus sign. Both terms contain $n = 0$ ($x[0] = 2$) — easy to double-drop or double-count.

## Problem 6 · A causal system from one input–output pair (15 pts)

> [!question] Problem 6
> A causal LTI system produces the output $y[n] = \{1,\ \tfrac12,\ 0,\ 0,\ 0,\ \dots\}_{n=0}^{\infty}$ when excited by the input $x[n] = \{1,\ \tfrac13,\ 0,\ 0,\ 0,\ \dots\}_{n=0}^{\infty}$.
>
> (a) Determine the transfer function of the system.
>
> (b) Determine the impulse response of the system.
>
> (c) Express the system in the form of an LCCDE.

> [!success]- Solution 6
> **(a)** $Y(z) = 1 + \tfrac12 z^{-1}$, $X(z) = 1 + \tfrac13 z^{-1}$:
> $$
> \boldsymbol{H(z) = \frac{Y(z)}{X(z)} = \frac{1 + \frac12 z^{-1}}{1 + \frac13 z^{-1}},\qquad \lvert z\rvert > \tfrac13\ \text{(causal)}}
> $$
> **(b)** Any of the three equivalent forms:
> $$
> \boldsymbol{h[n] = \left(-\tfrac13\right)^n u[n] + \tfrac12\left(-\tfrac13\right)^{n-1}u[n-1] = \delta[n] + \tfrac16\left(-\tfrac13\right)^{n-1}u[n-1] = \tfrac32\delta[n] - \tfrac12\left(-\tfrac13\right)^n u[n]}
> $$
> (The last one is long division: $H = \tfrac32 - \dfrac{1/2}{1+\frac13 z^{-1}}$.)
>
> **(c)** $Y(z)\left(1 + \tfrac13 z^{-1}\right) = X(z)\left(1 + \tfrac12 z^{-1}\right)$:
> $$
> \boldsymbol{y[n] = -\tfrac13\,y[n-1] + x[n] + \tfrac12\,x[n-1]}
> $$

> [!trap] Where points go
> - Two FIR signals, but the system is **IIR**: dividing them creates a pole at $-\tfrac13$.
> - $H$ is not strictly proper (equal degrees in $z^{-1}$): either split the numerator, $\frac{1}{1+\frac13 z^{-1}} + \frac{\frac12 z^{-1}}{1+\frac13 z^{-1}}$, or long-divide first (constant $\tfrac32$ plus a proper part). Writing $H = \frac{A}{1+\frac13 z^{-1}}$ alone and solving for $A$ by cover-up is wrong.

```python
import numpy as np
from scipy.signal import lfilter
b, a = [1, 1/2], [1, 1/3]                  # H(z) = (1 + z^-1/2)/(1 + z^-1/3)
print(lfilter(b, a, [1, 1/3, 0, 0, 0, 0]))     # reproduces the given output?
h = lfilter(b, a, [1, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0])          # impulse response
n = np.arange(6)
print(np.allclose(h, 1.5*(n == 0) - 0.5*(-1/3)**n))
```

```text
[1.  0.5 0.  0.  0.  0. ]
True
```

## Problem 7 · Causal unstable system, cancelling input (18 pts)

> [!question] Problem 7
> A **causal** LTI system has transfer function
> $$
> H(z) = \frac{1}{\left(1 - \frac12 z^{-1}\right)\left(1 - 2z^{-1}\right)} .
> $$
> (a) Sketch the ROC of $H(z)$ and clearly indicate the location of the poles.
>
> (b) Find the impulse response $h[n]$.
>
> (c) Suppose the input is **right-sided** with z-transform $X(z) = \dfrac{z^{-1} - 2z^{-2}}{1 - \frac12 z^{-1}}$. Find the z-transform $Y(z)$ of the output and its ROC. Check for any pole–zero cancellations.
>
> (d) Determine the output signal $y[n]$ corresponding to $Y(z)$ in part (c). Is $y[n]$ bounded?

> [!success]- Solution 7(a)–(b)
> **(a)** Poles at $z = \tfrac12$ and $z = 2$ (on the real axis). Causal ⇒ **ROC $\lvert z\rvert > 2$**: shade everything outside the circle of radius 2. The unit circle is not in the ROC, so the system is unstable.
>
> **(b)** Cover-up: $A = \left.\dfrac{1}{1-2z^{-1}}\right|_{z=1/2} = \dfrac{1}{1-4} = -\tfrac13$, $\;B = \left.\dfrac{1}{1-\frac12 z^{-1}}\right|_{z=2} = \dfrac{1}{3/4} = \tfrac43$. Causal ⇒ both right-sided:
> $$
> \boldsymbol{h[n] = -\tfrac13\left(\tfrac12\right)^n u[n] + \tfrac43\,(2)^n u[n]}
> $$

> [!success]- Solution 7(c)–(d)
> **(c)** $X(z) = \dfrac{z^{-1}(1 - 2z^{-1})}{1 - \frac12 z^{-1}}$ has a zero at $z = 2$ that cancels the unstable pole:
> $$
> \boldsymbol{Y(z) = \frac{z^{-1}}{\left(1 - \frac12 z^{-1}\right)^2},\qquad \lvert z\rvert > \tfrac12}
> $$
> (The ROC grows from $\lvert z\rvert > 2$ to $\lvert z\rvert > \tfrac12$ because the pole at 2 is gone.)
>
> **(d)** From $n\,a^n u[n] \leftrightarrow \dfrac{az^{-1}}{(1-az^{-1})^2}$ with $a = \tfrac12$:
> $$
> \boldsymbol{y[n] = 2n\left(\tfrac12\right)^n u[n]},\quad\text{bounded (its maximum is 1, at } n = 1, 2\text{).}
> $$

> [!trap] Where points go
> - **(c)** Keeping ROC $\lvert z\rvert > 2$ after the cancellation. The ROC of $Y$ is set by the poles $Y$ actually has.
> - **(d)** "A double pole ⇒ unbounded" is only true **on** the unit circle. At $\tfrac12$ the factor $n$ loses to $(\tfrac12)^n$. See [[problems/unbounded-outputs-and-pole-matching|pole matching]].

## Problem 8 · Parameters for stability (10 pts)

> [!question] Problem 8
> (a) A **non-causal** LTI system is described by $y[n] + \alpha\,y[n-1] = x[n] + 3x[n-1]$, where $\alpha$ is a finite, real-valued constant. For what value(s) of $\alpha$ is this system BIBO stable?
>
> (b) A **causal** LTI system is described by $y[n] - \tfrac52\,y[n-1] + y[n-2] = x[n] + \beta\,x[n-1]$, where $\beta$ is a finite, real-valued constant. For what value(s) of $\beta$ is this system BIBO stable?

> [!success]- Solution 8(a)
> $H(z) = \dfrac{1 + 3z^{-1}}{1 + \alpha z^{-1}}$, one pole at $z = -\alpha$. Non-causal ⇒ the ROC is the **inside** of the pole: $\lvert z\rvert < \lvert\alpha\rvert$ (a left-sided $h$). Stable ⇔ that disk contains the unit circle:
> $$
> \boldsymbol{\lvert\alpha\rvert > 1}
> $$
> (the opposite of the causal condition $\lvert\alpha\rvert < 1$). At $\alpha = 3$ the pole cancels against the zero and $H = 1$ — also stable, and inside the answer.

> [!warning] Answer-key erratum — #8(a)
> The typed key's box reads "$\lvert a\rvert > 1$"; the parameter is $\alpha$, so it means $\lvert\alpha\rvert > 1$.

> [!success]- Solution 8(b)
> $1 - \tfrac52 z^{-1} + z^{-2} = (1 - 2z^{-1})(1 - \tfrac12 z^{-1})$, so
> $$
> H(z) = \frac{1 + \beta z^{-1}}{(1 - 2z^{-1})(1 - \frac12 z^{-1})},\qquad \lvert z\rvert > 2\ \text{(causal)} .
> $$
> Unstable unless the zero $z = -\beta$ sits on the pole at $2$: $\boldsymbol{\beta = -2}$. Then $H = \dfrac{1}{1-\frac12 z^{-1}}$, $h = (\tfrac12)^n u[n]$.

> [!trap] Where points go
> - **(a)** Reflexively answering $\lvert\alpha\rvert < 1$. Non-causal flips the ROC to the inside of the pole, so the pole must be *outside* the unit circle.
> - **(b)** $\beta = -\tfrac12$ cancels the *harmless* pole; only the pole at 2 matters. Recipes: [[problems/parameters-for-stability|parameters for stability]], [[problems/two-sided-systems-as-recursions|two-sided systems as recursions]].
