## What it looks like on the exam

Ideal A/D → $H_d(\omega)$ → ideal D/A with one $T$, a triangle or band $X_a$, an ideal filter. Two shapes:

- **A · Sketch** $X_d$, $Y_d$, $Y_a$ at one or two $T$, one often at or below Nyquist.
- **B · Design** the largest $T$ and $\omega_c$ or $H_d$ for a wanted analog filter, often "some aliasing is allowable".

Opening parts ($T_N$, $X_d$) belong to the [[problems/sampling-and-the-nyquist-rate|sampling family]]. C/D and D/C on the exams are the ideal A/D and D/A.

## The recipe

> [!recipe] A/D → $H_d$ → D/A
> $$
> \begin{gathered}
> X_d(\omega)=\frac1T\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}X_a\!\left(\frac{\omega-2\pi k}{T}\right),\qquad Y_d(\omega)=H_d(\omega)X_d(\omega),\\
> Y_a(\Omega)=\begin{cases}T\,Y_d(\Omega T),&\lvert\Omega\rvert\lt\frac{\pi}{T}\\0,&\lvert\Omega\rvert\gt\frac{\pi}{T}\end{cases}
> \end{gathered}
> $$
> 1. **$X_d$:** relabel $\omega=\Omega T$, height $\frac1T$, copy every $2\pi$, add overlaps.
> 2. **$Y_d$:** multiply point by point; still $2\pi$-periodic; signs and zeros of $H_d$ pass through.
> 3. **$Y_a$:** keep $\lvert\omega\rvert\lt\pi$, relabel $\Omega=\frac{\omega}{T}$, times $T$. Cutoff $\omega_c$ lands at $\frac{\omega_c}{T}$ rad/s ($\frac{\omega_c}{2\pi T}$ Hz); nothing beyond $\frac{\pi}{T}$.
> 4. **One analog filter.** If no other copy reaches where $H_d\ne0$ (always when $f_s\gt2B$): $H_a(\Omega)=H_d(\Omega T)$ on $\lvert\Omega\rvert\lt\frac{\pi}{T}$. Backwards: $H_d(\omega)=H_a(\frac{\omega}{T})$.
> 5. **Largest $T$.** Aliasing only spoils the overlap near $\pm\pi$; keep the passband below the next copy's start $2\pi-\Omega_{\max}T$. Low-pass to $f_c$: $f_s\ge f_c+B$, $\omega_c=2\pi f_cT$. Fixed $\omega_c$: $T\le\frac{2\pi-\omega_c}{\Omega_{\max}}$. High-pass (band reaches $\pi$): $f_s\ge2B$. Band-pass to $f_{hi}$: $f_s\ge f_{hi}+B$.
> 6. **Check:** $\frac1T$ in $X_d$, $Y_d$; $X_a$'s height back in $Y_a$; $Y_a=0$ beyond $\frac{\pi}{T}$. Keys box $T\lt\dots$ but quote the bound.

> [!trap] Where the points go
> - **"Below Nyquist, so not LTI."** Compare the passband edge with $2\pi-\Omega_{\max}T$, not $\Omega_{\max}T$ with $\pi$ (FA2019 #9(c), HW7 #4(c)).
> - **Wrong rule.** A low-pass "largest $T$" is not the Nyquist period (FA2023 #5(a), FA2024 #5(c), SP2025 #5(b)); a high-pass's is (FA2023 #5(b)).
> - **Units.** $\omega_c$ is rad/sample: FA2019 #9(b) wants $\frac{\pi}{3T}$, not $\frac{\pi}{3}$.
> - **Heights.** $\frac1T$ in $X_d$, $Y_d$; the D/A's gain $T$ restores it.
> - **Signs.** $H_d=-1$ flips a band (SP2023 #7(b)); an odd spectrum can cancel (SP2023 #7(c)).

## Practice problems

> [!question] Practice 1 — one filter, three sampling periods
> $X_a(\Omega)=1$ for $\lvert\Omega\rvert\le40\pi$, falls linearly to 0 at $\lvert\Omega\rvert=80\pi$. Ideal low-pass $\omega_c=\frac{\pi}{2}$. Sketch $X_d$, $Y_d$, $Y_a$ for (a) $T=\frac{1}{100}$ s, (b) $T=\frac{1}{64}$ s, (c) $T=\frac{1}{40}$ s. (d) Largest $T$ with $y_a$ an undistorted low-pass of $x_a$, and its analog cutoff?

> [!success]- Solution
> $B=40$ Hz, $T_N=\frac{1}{80}$ s. Flat top to $40\pi T$, edge $80\pi T$, height $\frac1T$, analog cutoff $\frac{\pi/2}{T}$.
> **(a)** $X_d$: 100 to $0.4\pi$, ramp to 0 at $0.8\pi$. $Y_d$: that on $\lvert\omega\rvert\le\frac{\pi}{2}$ (75 at the edge). $Y_a=X_a$ on $\lvert\Omega\rvert\le50\pi$ (down to $\frac34$), 0 beyond.
> **(b)** Height 64, flat to $\frac{5\pi}{8}$, edge $\frac{5\pi}{4}$; the next copy starts at $\frac{3\pi}{4}$:
> $$
> X_d(\omega)=\begin{cases}64,&\lvert\omega\rvert\le\frac{5\pi}{8}\\[2pt]64\left(2-\frac{8\lvert\omega\rvert}{5\pi}\right),&\frac{5\pi}{8}\le\lvert\omega\rvert\le\frac{3\pi}{4}\\[2pt]51.2,&\frac{3\pi}{4}\le\lvert\omega\rvert\le\pi.\end{cases}
> $$
> Passband inside the flat top: $Y_d=64$, $Y_a=1$ on $\lvert\Omega\rvert\le32\pi$. Aliased, yet a clean low-pass at 16 Hz.
> **(c)** Height 40; the flat top reaches $\pi$ and the next copy adds $40\frac{\lvert\omega\rvert}{\pi}$: $X_d=40\left(1+\frac{\lvert\omega\rvert}{\pi}\right)$. $Y_a=1+\frac{\lvert\Omega\rvert}{40\pi}$ on $\lvert\Omega\rvert\le20\pi$, up to 1.5: 30–40 Hz folded onto 0–10 Hz.
> **(d)** $2\pi-80\pi T\ge\frac{\pi}{2}$: $T\le\frac{3}{160}$ s, cutoff $\frac{80\pi}{3}$ rad/s ($13\tfrac13$ Hz).

> [!question] Practice 2 — how slowly may we sample?
> $x_a$ is bandlimited to 8 kHz, with energy at every frequency up to 8 kHz. (a) Remove everything above 3 kHz: largest $T$, $\omega_c$? (b) At 10 kHz with $\omega_c$ set for 3 kHz, which input frequencies corrupt the output, and where? (c) Keep only above 3 kHz: largest $T$, $H_d$? (d) Keep only 2–3 kHz?

> [!success]- Solution
> **(a)** $f_s\ge3+8=11$ kHz: $T=\frac{1}{11000}$ s, $\omega_c=\frac{6\pi}{11}$. The Nyquist rate, 16 kHz, is not needed.
> **(b)** $\omega_c=0.6\pi$; the next copy starts at $0.4\pi$. Input at 7–8 kHz lands on $[-0.6\pi,-0.4\pi]$ and comes out at $10-f$ kHz, reversed, on top of 2–3 kHz.
> **(c)** Band reaches $\pi$: $f_s\ge16$ kHz, $T=\frac{1}{16000}$ s, $H_d=1$ for $\frac{3\pi}{8}\lt\lvert\omega\rvert\le\pi$.
> **(d)** Same condition as (a): $T=\frac{1}{11000}$ s, $H_d=1$ on $\frac{4\pi}{11}\le\lvert\omega\rvert\le\frac{6\pi}{11}$.

> [!question] Practice 3 — True or False
> (a) If $T$ exceeds the Nyquist period, the chain cannot act on $x_a$ as an LTI system. (b) With $H_d=1$ and $f_s\gt2B$, $y_a=x_a$. (c) Without aliasing, a digital low-pass at $\omega_c$ gives an analog cutoff of $\frac{\omega_c}{2\pi T}$ Hz. (d) Doubling $f_s$ with the same $H_d$ halves the analog cutoff. (e) A signal bandlimited to 10 kHz can be high-passed to 5–10 kHz at $f_s=15$ kHz.

> [!success]- Solution
> **(a) False:** LTI while no alias reaches the passband (FA2019 #9(c), Practice 1(b)). **(b) True:** Lecture 18's perfect reconstruction. **(c) True:** $\Omega_c=\frac{\omega_c}{T}$. **(d) False:** $\Omega_c=\omega_cf_s$ doubles. **(e) False:** the overlap $\frac{2\pi}{3}\le\lvert\omega\rvert\le\pi$ is the wanted band; a high-pass needs 20 kHz.
