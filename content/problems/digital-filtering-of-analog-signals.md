*Problem family · one 15–22-point problem on six of the seven past Midterm 2 exams (every one but SP2021; two problems on FA2021) · uses [[4-sampling-and-reconstruction/17-ideal-analog-to-digital-conversion|Lecture 17]] ($X_d$), [[4-sampling-and-reconstruction/18-ideal-digital-to-analog-conversion|Lecture 18]] (the ideal D/A as a low-pass of height $T$) and [[4-sampling-and-reconstruction/19-aliasing|Lecture 19]] (aliasing), with the frequency response of [[3-fourier-analysis/15-frequency-response|Lecture 15]] · worked in the course: HW7 #4 (on the [[exams/quiz-2|Quiz 2 review]]) and the Lecture 18 example (slides 10–11) · concepts: [[concepts/sampling]], [[concepts/ideal-reconstruction]], [[concepts/aliasing]], [[concepts/nyquist-criterion]] · see it: [[demos/sampling-explorer|sampling explorer]]*

> [!abstract] In one breath
> Three boxes, three spectra. The ideal A/D gives $X_d(\omega)=\frac1T\sum_kX_a\!\left(\frac{\omega-2\pi k}{T}\right)$ (copies of height $\frac1T$ every $2\pi$, $\omega=\Omega T$); the filter multiplies, $Y_d(\omega)=H_d(\omega)X_d(\omega)$, still $2\pi$-periodic; the ideal D/A of Lecture 18 is a low-pass of height $T$ and edge $\frac{\pi}{T}$, so it keeps one period and undoes the relabelling: $Y_a(\Omega)=T\,Y_d(\Omega T)$ for $\lvert\Omega\rvert\lt\frac{\pi}{T}$ and $0$ outside. If no copy reaches the filter's passband, the whole chain is one LTI analog filter, $H_a(\Omega)=H_d(\Omega T)$ on $\lvert\Omega\rvert\lt\frac{\pi}{T}$: a digital cutoff $\omega_c$ is the analog cutoff $\frac{\omega_c}{T}$ rad/s. That condition is weaker than the Nyquist criterion: Lecture 19's aliasing does no harm if the filter throws the aliased band away, which is why a low-pass that keeps $\lvert f\rvert\le f_c$ only needs $f_s\ge f_c+B$, not $2B$. The exams write $x_c(t)$, $X_c(\Omega)$, $T_N$, "C/D" and "D/C" for the same things.

## What it looks like on the exam

The opening parts (the Nyquist rate, the sketch of $X_d$) are the [[problems/sampling-and-the-nyquist-rate|sampling family]]; this page takes over at the filter.

## The recipes

> [!recipe] A · The three sketches
> $$
> X_d(\omega)=\frac1T\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}X_a\!\left(\frac{\omega-2\pi k}{T}\right),\qquad Y_d(\omega)=H_d(\omega)X_d(\omega),\qquad Y_a(\Omega)=\begin{cases}T\,Y_d(\Omega T),&\lvert\Omega\rvert\lt\frac{\pi}{T}\\0,&\lvert\Omega\rvert\gt\frac{\pi}{T}\end{cases}
> $$
> 1. **$X_d$:** relabel every landmark with $\omega=\Omega T$, scale the height by $\frac1T$, copy every $2\pi$, add where copies overlap ([[problems/sampling-and-the-nyquist-rate|sampling family]], recipe B).
> 2. **$Y_d$:** multiply point by point. $H_d$ is $2\pi$-periodic, so $Y_d$ is too; draw it on the interval asked ($[-\pi,\pi]$ or $[-3\pi,3\pi]$). Signs and zeros of $H_d$ pass straight through (SP2023 #7).
> 3. **$Y_a$:** take the period $\lvert\omega\rvert\lt\pi$, relabel $\Omega=\frac{\omega}{T}$, multiply by $T$. A digital cutoff $\omega_c$ lands at $\Omega_c=\frac{\omega_c}{T}$ rad/s ($\frac{\omega_c}{2\pi T}$ Hz); the ends $\pm\pi$ land at $\pm\frac{\pi}{T}$, the D/A's edge, and nothing comes out beyond it.
>
> **Checks:** $X_d$ and $Y_d$ carry the factor $\frac1T$, $Y_a$ is back at the height of $X_a$; $Y_a$ is zero for $\lvert\Omega\rvert\gt\frac{\pi}{T}$; where nothing aliased and $H_d=1$, $Y_a=X_a$.

> [!key] The chain as one analog filter
> If no copy other than the centred one reaches the part of $[-\pi,\pi]$ where $H_d\ne0$ (always true when $f_s\gt2B$), the sum in step 3 keeps only $k=0$ and
> $$
> Y_a(\Omega)=H_d(\Omega T)\,X_a(\Omega),\qquad\lvert\Omega\rvert\lt\frac{\pi}{T}.
> $$
> The chain is then LTI with $H_a(\Omega)=H_d(\Omega T)$ for $\lvert\Omega\rvert\lt\frac{\pi}{T}$ (and 0 beyond). Read it both ways: given $H_d$, stretch its axis by $\frac1T$ (FA2019 #9(b): cutoff $\frac{\pi}{3T}$); given a wanted $H_a$, set $H_d(\omega)=H_a(\frac{\omega}{T})$ on $\lvert\omega\rvert\le\pi$ and repeat it every $2\pi$ (FA2021 #5(b)).

> [!derivation]- Why the $\frac1T$ and the $T$ cancel
> For $\lvert\Omega\rvert\lt\frac{\pi}{T}$, put $\omega=\Omega T$ into the three formulas of recipe A:
> $$
> Y_a(\Omega)=T\,H_d(\Omega T)\,\frac1T\sum_kX_a\!\left(\Omega-\frac{2\pi k}{T}\right)=H_d(\Omega T)\Big[X_a(\Omega)+\sum_{k\ne0}X_a\!\left(\Omega-\tfrac{2\pi k}{T}\right)\Big].
> $$
> The bracket is $X_a(\Omega)$ plus the aliases. Wherever $H_d(\Omega T)=0$ the aliases are multiplied by zero; wherever $H_d(\Omega T)\ne0$ they are zero by assumption. Either way $Y_a(\Omega)=H_d(\Omega T)X_a(\Omega)$. With $f_s\gt2B$ the aliases vanish on all of $\lvert\Omega\rvert\lt\frac{\pi}{T}$, which is Lecture 18's perfect reconstruction when $H_d=1$.

> [!recipe] B · The largest $T$ for a wanted analog filter
> 1. **Write the wanted band in digital frequency:** an analog edge $\Omega_c=2\pi f_c$ lands at $\omega_c=\Omega_cT$; the band edge of $X_a$ lands at $\Omega_{\max}T$.
> 2. **Find where aliasing starts:** the copy centred at $2\pi$ begins at $2\pi-\Omega_{\max}T$ (and its mirror at $-2\pi+\Omega_{\max}T$).
> 3. **Keep the passband clean:**
>    - **low-pass** keeping $\lvert\Omega\rvert\le\Omega_c$: $\Omega_cT\le2\pi-\Omega_{\max}T$, so $T\le\dfrac{2\pi}{\Omega_c+\Omega_{\max}}$, i.e. $f_s\ge f_c+B$; then $\omega_c=\Omega_cT$;
>    - **fixed digital cutoff** $\omega_c$ (FA2019 #9(c)): $\omega_c\le2\pi-\Omega_{\max}T$, so $T\le\dfrac{2\pi-\omega_c}{\Omega_{\max}}$;
>    - **high-pass**, or anything that keeps frequencies up to $B$: the passband reaches $\omega=\pi$, where the overlap starts, so no aliasing at all: $f_s\ge2B$;
>    - **band-pass** $f_{lo}\le\lvert f\rvert\le f_{hi}$ of a signal with energy across the whole band: the same condition as a low-pass at $f_{hi}$, $f_s\ge f_{hi}+B$.
> 4. **Check the D/A** reaches the band: $\frac{\pi}{T}\ge\Omega_c$. In the low-pass case it always does, since $f_c+B\ge2f_c$.
>
> **Strict or not?** The keys box $T\lt\dots$ (FA2019 #9(c), FA2023 #5, FA2024 #5(c), SP2025 #5(b)) and then quote the bound as "the largest $T$". Equality is fine when $X_a$ is zero at its own edge, since the next copy then starts with height 0 at the passband edge.

> [!intuition] Why aliasing can be harmless
> [[4-sampling-and-reconstruction/19-aliasing|Lecture 19]]: once the copies overlap, $x_a(t)$ cannot be recovered from $x[n]$. The chain never tries to recover all of $x_a$: it only delivers the part $H_d$ keeps. Undersampling destroys information *where the copies overlap*, near $\omega=\pm\pi$, so a low-pass, which discards that region anyway, survives it; a high-pass, which keeps it, does not.

> [!trap] Where the points go
> - **"Below the Nyquist rate, so not LTI / the output is ruined."** Compare the passband edge with the start of the next copy, $2\pi-\Omega_{\max}T$, not $\Omega_{\max}T$ with $\pi$ (FA2019 #9(c), HW7 #4(c), Lecture 18 at $T=\frac{1}{50}$).
> - **Answering the Nyquist period for a low-pass "largest $T$"** (FA2023 #5(a), FA2024 #5(c), SP2025 #5(b)): too strict. The other way round, the low-pass rule $f_s\ge f_c+B$ on a high-pass is too lax: there the Nyquist period *is* the answer (FA2023 #5(b)).
> - **Units of the cutoff.** $\omega_c$ is in rad/sample; the analog cutoff is $\frac{\omega_c}{T}$ rad/s or $\frac{\omega_c}{2\pi T}$ Hz. FA2019 #9(b) wants $\frac{\pi}{3T}$, not $\frac{\pi}{3}$; FA2023 #5 needs $\omega_c$ from hertz, $2\pi f_cT$.
> - **Heights.** $\frac1T$ in $X_d$ and $Y_d$ (8000, 4000, 300, 150 on the exams), back to the height of $X_a$ in $Y_a$: the D/A's gain is $T$.
> - **One copy or many.** $Y_d$ is periodic; only $Y_a$ has a single copy, and it stops at $\frac{\pi}{T}$. Content of $X_a$ beyond $\frac{\pi}{T}$ is either lost or returned at a wrong frequency: the inverted image of FA2021 #4 at $T=\frac{1}{4000}$, the rectangle of FA2024 #5(b) at $2T_N$.
> - **Signs.** A filter value of $-1$ flips a band (SP2023 #7(b)); an odd spectrum can cancel itself after sampling (SP2023 #7(c)).
> - **Notation.** C/D and D/C are the ideal A/D and D/A; $x_c$, $X_c$ are $x_a$, $X_a$. The FA2024 key labels its $X_d$ axes "$\Omega$"; a DTFT lives on $\omega$.

## Practice problems

> [!question] Practice 1 — one filter, three sampling periods
> $X_a(\Omega)=1$ for $\lvert\Omega\rvert\le40\pi$, falls linearly to 0 at $\lvert\Omega\rvert=80\pi$, and is 0 beyond. $H_d$ is an ideal low-pass with $\omega_c=\frac{\pi}{2}$. Sketch $X_d$, $Y_d$ (on $[-\pi,\pi]$) and $Y_a$ for (a) $T=\frac{1}{100}$ s, (b) $T=\frac{1}{64}$ s, (c) $T=\frac{1}{40}$ s, with every height and break point. (d) With $\omega_c=\frac{\pi}{2}$ fixed, what is the largest $T$ for which $y_a$ is $x_a$ low-passed without distortion, and what is the analog cutoff then?

> [!success]- Solution
> $B=40$ Hz, so the Nyquist rate is 80 Hz and $T_N=\frac{1}{80}$ s. In $X_d$ the flat top ends at $40\pi T$, the band edge is at $80\pi T$, the height is $\frac1T$; the analog cutoff is $\frac{\pi/2}{T}$.
> **(a)** $T=\frac{1}{100}$ (above Nyquist). $X_d$: 100 out to $0.4\pi$, a ramp to 0 at $0.8\pi$, zero to $\pi$ (the next copy starts at $1.2\pi$). $Y_d$: the part on $\lvert\omega\rvert\le\frac{\pi}{2}$, where the ramp is at $100\cdot\frac{80\pi-50\pi}{40\pi}=75$, repeated every $2\pi$. $Y_a$: cutoff $50\pi$ rad/s; $Y_a=X_a$ on $\lvert\Omega\rvert\le50\pi$ (1 out to $40\pi$, then down to $\frac34$) and 0 beyond.
> **(b)** $T=\frac{1}{64}$ (below Nyquist). Height 64, flat top to $\frac{5\pi}{8}$, edge at $\frac{5\pi}{4}$; the copy centred at $2\pi$ starts at $2\pi-\frac{5\pi}{4}=\frac{3\pi}{4}$, and on $\frac{3\pi}{4}\le\lvert\omega\rvert\le\pi$ the two ramps add to a constant:
> $$
> X_d(\omega)=\begin{cases}64,&\lvert\omega\rvert\le\frac{5\pi}{8}\\[2pt]64\left(2-\frac{8\lvert\omega\rvert}{5\pi}\right),&\frac{5\pi}{8}\le\lvert\omega\rvert\le\frac{3\pi}{4}\\[2pt]51.2,&\frac{3\pi}{4}\le\lvert\omega\rvert\le\pi.\end{cases}
> $$
> The passband $\lvert\omega\rvert\le\frac{\pi}{2}$ sits inside the flat top: $Y_d=64$ there. $Y_a=1$ on $\lvert\Omega\rvert\le32\pi$, which *is* $X_a$ there: aliased, yet a clean low-pass at 16 Hz.
> **(c)** $T=\frac{1}{40}$. Height 40, and the flat top now reaches exactly $\pi$; the copy centred at $2\pi$ starts at 0 and adds $40\frac{\lvert\omega\rvert}{\pi}$: $X_d(\omega)=40\left(1+\frac{\lvert\omega\rvert}{\pi}\right)$ on $[-\pi,\pi]$. $Y_d$ is that on $\lvert\omega\rvert\le\frac{\pi}{2}$ (40 up to 60). $Y_a(\Omega)=1+\frac{\lvert\Omega\rvert}{40\pi}$ on $\lvert\Omega\rvert\le20\pi$, rising to 1.5 where $X_a$ is flat at 1: the input's 30–40 Hz band has folded onto 0–10 Hz ($f\mapsto40-f$) and is added to it.
> **(d)** Recipe B with a fixed $\omega_c$: $2\pi-80\pi T\ge\frac{\pi}{2}$, so $T\le\frac{3}{160}$ s ($f_s\ge53\tfrac13$ Hz), and the cutoff is $\frac{\pi/2}{3/160}=\frac{80\pi}{3}$ rad/s ($13\tfrac13$ Hz). Consistent with (b) ($\frac{1}{64}\lt\frac{3}{160}$) and (c) ($\frac{1}{40}\gt\frac{3}{160}$).
> (checked: the alias sums on a grid against the three piecewise formulas, $Y_a=T\,H_d\,X_d$ against the stated outputs, and a scan over $T$ for (d).)

> for T in [1 / 100, 1 / 64, 1 / 40]:
>     W = np.array([0, 10, 20, 30, 45]) * np.pi
>     print(f"T = 1/{round(1 / T)}: Y_a =", np.round(Ya(W, T), 3), " X_a =", Xa(W))
> ```
> ```text
> T = 1/100: Y_a = [1.    1.    1.    1.    0.875]  X_a = [1.    1.    1.    1.    0.875]
> T = 1/64: Y_a = [1. 1. 1. 1. 0.]  X_a = [1.    1.    1.    1.    0.875]
> T = 1/40: Y_a = [1.   1.25 1.5  0.   0.  ]  X_a = [1.    1.    1.    1.    0.875]
> ```
> At $T=\frac{1}{100}$ the output matches the input up to the cutoff $50\pi$; at $\frac{1}{64}$ it matches up to $32\pi$ and stops; at $\frac{1}{40}$ it is wrong already at $10\pi$, and the cutoff is $20\pi$.

> [!question] Practice 2 — how slowly may we sample?
> An audio signal $x_a(t)$ is bandlimited to 8 kHz and has energy at every frequency up to 8 kHz. It passes through ideal A/D → $H_d$ → ideal D/A with one $T$.
> (a) We want $y_a$ to be $x_a$ with everything above 3 kHz removed. Find the largest $T$ and the cutoff $\omega_c$.
> (b) A colleague samples at 10 kHz and sets $\omega_c$ for 3 kHz. Which input frequencies corrupt the output, and at which output frequencies do they appear?
> (c) Now keep only the frequencies above 3 kHz. Largest $T$ and $H_d$?
> (d) And only 2–3 kHz?

> [!success]- Solution
> **(a)** Low-pass: $f_s\ge f_c+B=3+8=11$ kHz, so $T=\frac{1}{11000}$ s and $\omega_c=2\pi\cdot\frac{3000}{11000}=\frac{6\pi}{11}$. (The condition: passband edge $2\pi\cdot3000\,T\le$ start of the next copy $2\pi-2\pi\cdot8000\,T$.) The Nyquist rate, 16 kHz, is not needed.
> **(b)** At 10 kHz, $\omega_c=0.6\pi$ and the copy from $2\pi$ starts at $2\pi-1.6\pi=0.4\pi$, inside the passband. An input at $f$ between 7 and 8 kHz lands at $\omega=2\pi\frac{f}{10000}\in[1.4\pi,1.6\pi]\equiv[-0.6\pi,-0.4\pi]$ and comes out at $10-f$ kHz: the 7–8 kHz content appears, reversed, on top of the genuine 2–3 kHz.
> **(c)** The high-pass band reaches $\omega=\pi$, where the overlap starts, so nothing may alias: $f_s\ge16$ kHz, $T=\frac{1}{16000}$ s, and 3 kHz lands at $\frac{3\pi}{8}$: $H_d=1$ for $\frac{3\pi}{8}\lt\lvert\omega\rvert\le\pi$, 0 for $\lvert\omega\rvert\le\frac{3\pi}{8}$.
> **(d)** The band must end below the start of the next copy: $2\pi\cdot3000\,T\le2\pi-2\pi\cdot8000\,T$, the same as (a). $T=\frac{1}{11000}$ s, $H_d=1$ on $\frac{4\pi}{11}\le\lvert\omega\rvert\le\frac{6\pi}{11}$.
> (checked: for each design a scan of $f_s$ in 100 Hz steps finds the smallest rate from which the output is exactly the wanted band of a triangle $X_a$, 11, 16 and 11 kHz; the 7–8 kHz → 3–2 kHz map at 10 kHz.)

> [!question] Practice 3 — a moving average between the converters
> The chain runs at $T=1$ ms with $y[n]=\frac13\big(x[n]+x[n-1]+x[n-2]\big)$.
> (a) Find $H_d(\omega)$ and the equivalent $H_a(\Omega)$ for inputs bandlimited below 500 Hz. Which analog frequency is blocked?
> (b) Find $y_a(t)$ for $x_a(t)=3+2\cos(500\pi t)+4\cos(\frac{2000\pi}{3}t)$.
> (c) Find $y_a(t)$ for $x_a(t)=\cos(1500\pi t)$. Does the chain act as $H_a$ here?
> (d) Find $y_a(t)$ for $x_a(t)=\cos(2000\pi t)$.

> [!success]- Solution
> **(a)** $H_d(\omega)=\frac13\left(1+e^{-j\omega}+e^{-j2\omega}\right)=\frac{e^{-j\omega}}{3}(1+2\cos\omega)$ ([[3-fourier-analysis/15-frequency-response|Lecture 15]]). With $\omega=\Omega T$:
> $$
> H_a(\Omega)=\frac{1+2\cos(\Omega/1000)}{3}\,e^{-j\Omega/1000},\qquad\lvert\Omega\rvert\lt1000\pi,
> $$
> a real amplitude times a 1 ms delay. The zero at $\omega=\frac{2\pi}{3}$ is $\Omega=\frac{2000\pi}{3}$ rad/s, $333\tfrac13$ Hz.
> **(b)** Every tone is below 500 Hz, so $H_a$ applies tone by tone. DC: $H_a(0)=1$, so 3. At 250 Hz, $\omega=\frac{\pi}{2}$ and $H_d(\frac{\pi}{2})=\frac{e^{-j\pi/2}}{3}=-\frac{j}{3}$: $2\cdot\frac13\cos(500\pi t-\frac{\pi}{2})$. At $333\tfrac13$ Hz the gain is 0.
> $$
> y_a(t)=3+\tfrac23\sin(500\pi t).
> $$
> **(c)** 750 Hz is above $\frac{f_s}{2}=500$ Hz: $\omega=1.5\pi\equiv-\frac{\pi}{2}$, and $x[n]=\cos(\frac{\pi}{2}n)$, the samples of a 250 Hz cosine. Then $y[n]=\frac13\cos(\frac{\pi}{2}n-\frac{\pi}{2})$ and the D/A returns $y_a(t)=\frac13\sin(500\pi t)$: a 750 Hz input comes out at 250 Hz. No LTI system changes a frequency, so here the chain is not $H_a$ (which is not even defined at 750 Hz).
> **(d)** 1 kHz is $\omega=2\pi\equiv0$: $x[n]=1$, $y[n]=1$, $y_a(t)=1$. A tone goes in, a constant comes out.
> (checked: $H_d$ against the DTFT of $h=\frac13[1,1,1]$; the outputs by filtering 8000 samples with `np.convolve` and comparing with the samples of the stated $y_a$.)

> [!question] Practice 4 — True or False
> (a) If $T$ is larger than the Nyquist period of $x_a(t)$, the chain ideal A/D → $H_d$ → ideal D/A cannot act on $x_a(t)$ as an LTI system.
> (b) With $H_d(\omega)=1$ for all $\omega$ and $f_s\gt2B$, the chain returns $y_a(t)=x_a(t)$.
> (c) Without aliasing, an ideal digital low-pass with cutoff $\omega_c$ makes the chain an analog low-pass with cutoff $\frac{\omega_c}{2\pi T}$ Hz.
> (d) Doubling the sampling rate with the same $H_d$ (and still no aliasing) halves the analog cutoff frequency.
> (e) A signal bandlimited to 10 kHz can be high-pass filtered to keep 5–10 kHz by this chain at $f_s=15$ kHz.

> [!success]- Solution
> **(a) False.** It stays LTI as long as no alias reaches the passband: FA2019 #9(c) up to $T=\frac{1}{600}$ s, HW7 #4(c), Practice 1(b).
> **(b) True.** That is [[4-sampling-and-reconstruction/18-ideal-digital-to-analog-conversion|Lecture 18]]: perfect reconstruction, $H_a(\Omega)=H_d(\Omega T)=1$ on $\lvert\Omega\rvert\lt\frac{\pi}{T}$, which contains the whole band.
> **(c) True.** $\Omega_c=\frac{\omega_c}{T}$ rad/s, i.e. $\frac{\omega_c}{2\pi T}$ Hz.
> **(d) False.** $\Omega_c=\omega_cf_s$: doubling $f_s$ *doubles* the analog cutoff (it is fixed in $\omega$, and each $\omega$ now means twice as many rad/s).
> **(e) False.** At 15 kHz the band edge lands at $\frac{4\pi}{3}$ and the copies overlap on $\frac{2\pi}{3}\le\lvert\omega\rvert\le\pi$, which is exactly the wanted band ($5$ kHz $\to\frac{2\pi}{3}$). A high-pass needs $f_s\ge20$ kHz (FA2023 #5(b)).
> (checked: the band edges of (e) in `verify/FAM2_practice.py`; (a)–(d) restate results checked above.)
