*Problem family · on all seven past Midterm 2 exams: a stand-alone problem on five (FA2019 #8, SP2021 #8 and #9, SP2023 #8, FA2023 #3, SP2025 #6), the opening parts of every A/D → $H_d$ → D/A problem, one or two True/False items · 2–15 points · uses [[4-sampling-and-reconstruction/17-ideal-analog-to-digital-conversion|Lecture 17]], [[4-sampling-and-reconstruction/18-ideal-digital-to-analog-conversion|Lecture 18]] (what the ideal D/A returns) and [[4-sampling-and-reconstruction/19-aliasing|Lecture 19]] (aliased sinusoids), with the windowing property of [[3-fourier-analysis/14-dtft-properties|Lecture 14]] · concepts: [[concepts/sampling]], [[concepts/nyquist-criterion]], [[concepts/ideal-reconstruction]], [[concepts/aliasing]] · see it: [[demos/sampling-explorer|sampling explorer]] · drills: [[demos/practice-drills|practice drills]]*

> [!abstract] In one breath
> Sampling $x_c(t)$ every $T$ seconds multiplies it by an impulse train, so its spectrum is copied every $\frac{2\pi}{T}$ rad/s and scaled by $\frac1T$; relabelling with $\omega=\Omega T$ puts the copies every $2\pi$ on the DTFT axis: $X_d(\omega)=\frac1T\sum_nX_a\!\left(\frac{\omega-2\pi n}{T}\right)$. Everything the exam asks follows. The copies stay apart exactly when the band edge $\Omega_{\max}T$ is below $\pi$, i.e. $f_s\gt2B$: the **Nyquist rate** is $2B$, the Nyquist period $T_N=\frac{1}{2B}$. A **sketch of $X_d$** is the sketch of $X_a$ with the axis relabelled, the height multiplied by $\frac1T$ and overlapping copies *added*. A sinusoid at $\Omega_0$ becomes one at $\omega_0=\Omega_0T$, defined only modulo $2\pi$, so **several $T$ (or several $\Omega_0$) fit the same samples**. The ideal D/A ([[4-sampling-and-reconstruction/18-ideal-digital-to-analog-conversion|Lecture 18]]) turns each sample into a sinc and returns the one signal with those samples and nothing above $\frac{\pi}{T}$: a sampled sinusoid comes back at its digital frequency folded into $(-\pi,\pi)$, divided by $T$, which is aliasing ([[4-sampling-and-reconstruction/19-aliasing|Lecture 19]]). Lecture 17 writes $x_a(t)$, $X_a(\Omega)$ and $\frac{1}{2B}$; the exams write the same things as $x_c(t)$, $X_c(\Omega)$ and $T_N$ (or $T_{NYQ}$) about as often as they keep $x_a$, $X_a$.

## What it looks like on the exam

Five shapes. **(A)** "Find the Nyquist rate (in Hz) / the Nyquist period": part (a) of almost every sampling problem, 2–3 points. **(B)** "Sketch $X_d(\omega)$ for $T=\dots$, label the axes": the opening of every A/D → $H_d$ → D/A problem, usually at two rates, one at or below Nyquist; the $Y_d$, $Y_c$ and $H_d$ parts that follow are the [[problems/digital-filtering-of-analog-signals|digital filtering of analog signals]] family. **(C)** "Which $T$ / which $\Omega_0$ are consistent with these samples?": a stand-alone 6–12 point problem on SP2023, FA2023 and SP2025, and part (b) of SP2021 #9. **(D)** "What does the ideal D/A return?" ([[4-sampling-and-reconstruction/18-ideal-digital-to-analog-conversion|Lecture 18]], [[4-sampling-and-reconstruction/19-aliasing|Lecture 19]]): the samples behind a reconstructed output (SP2021 #9(a)), the $T$ that turned one tone into another (SP2023 #8(b), SP2025 #6(b)–(c)), or the sinc interpolation of a few samples (FA2019 #8). **(E)** One or two True/False statements.

## The recipes

> [!recipe] A · The Nyquist rate and period
> 1. **Find the highest frequency present,** $\Omega_{\max}$ in rad/s: the band edge of a sketched $X_c$, or the largest $\lvert\Omega_k\rvert$ in a sum of sinusoids. A *product* of sinusoids is a sum in disguise ($\cos a\sin b=\frac12[\sin(a+b)-\sin(a-b)]$): its highest frequency is the **sum**. $x_c(at)$ multiplies $\Omega_{\max}$ by $a$, $x_c(t)\cos(\Omega_ct)$ adds $\Omega_c$, a delay or a filter never raises it.
> 2. **Convert to hertz:** $B=\Omega_{\max}/2\pi$.
> 3. **Answer in the unit asked:** Nyquist rate $f_{\text{Nyquist}}=2B$ Hz ($2\Omega_{\max}$ in rad/s), Nyquist period $T_N=\frac{1}{2B}=\frac{\pi}{\Omega_{\max}}$ s. The criterion itself is $f_s\gt2B$, $T\lt T_N$.
>
> **Check:** at $T=T_N$ the band edge lands at $\omega=\Omega_{\max}T_N=\pi$.

> [!recipe] B · Sketching $X_d(\omega)$ for a given $T$
> $$
> X_d(\omega)=\frac1T\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}X_a\!\left(\frac{\omega-2\pi n}{T}\right),\qquad\omega=\Omega T.
> $$
> 1. **Relabel the axis:** every landmark $\Omega_i$ moves to $\omega_i=\Omega_iT$; the band edge $2\pi B$ lands at $2\pi BT=\pi\,\frac{f_{\text{Nyquist}}}{f_s}$.
> 2. **Scale the height by $\frac1T=f_s$.**
> 3. **Copy every $2\pi$** (centres at $2\pi n$) and compare the edge $\Omega_{\max}T$ with $\pi$: below, a guard band of width $2\pi-2\Omega_{\max}T$; equal, the copies touch; above, they overlap.
> 4. **Where copies overlap, add them.** On $[-\pi,\pi]$ only the copies that reach in matter ($n=0,\pm1$ while $\Omega_{\max}T\le2\pi$). Write each piece as a formula in $\omega$ and add: ramps of opposite slope add to a constant (SP2021 #8, FA2024 #5(b)); an odd spectrum can cancel itself (SP2023 #7(c)).
> 5. **Label** the height $\frac1T$, every break point, $\pm\pi$, and the axis ($\omega$, rad/sample).
>
> **Checks:** the average of $X_d$ over one period is $x[0]=x_c(0)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int X_a(\Omega)\,d\Omega$, whatever $T$ is; a real, even $X_c$ gives a real, even $X_d$.

> [!recipe] C · Which $T$ (or which $\Omega_0$) fit a sampled sinusoid
> 1. **Sample symbolically:** $A\cos(\Omega_0t+\phi)\to A\cos(\Omega_0Tn+\phi)$; the digital frequency $\Omega_0T$ is defined only modulo $2\pi$.
> 2. **Write the congruence.** A plain cosine: $\Omega_0T\equiv\pm\omega_0\pmod{2\pi}$. A cosine with a phase $\phi\ne0$, or any sine: $\Omega_0T\equiv+\omega_0$ only, since $-\omega_0$ gives $\cos(\omega_0n-\phi)$ or $-\sin(\omega_0n)$.
> 3. **Solve:** $T=\dfrac{\omega_0+2\pi k}{\Omega_0}$ for the $k$ with $T\gt0$, or $\Omega_0=\dfrac{\omega_0+2\pi k}{T}$, $k\in\mathbb Z$. Several tones give several congruences; keep the $T$ that satisfy all.
> 4. **Test each candidate** at $n=0,1,2$. A $T$ that makes $\Omega_0T$ a multiple of $\pi$ zeros every sample of a sine.

> [!recipe] D · What the ideal D/A returns for a sampled sinusoid (folding)
> The ideal D/A rebuilds the one signal that has these samples and nothing above $\frac{\pi}{T}$ ([[4-sampling-and-reconstruction/18-ideal-digital-to-analog-conversion|Lecture 18]]). For a sinusoid at $\Omega_0$ ([[4-sampling-and-reconstruction/19-aliasing|Lecture 19]]):
> 1. **Sample:** $\omega_0=\Omega_0T$.
> 2. **Fold** into $(-\pi,\pi)$: $\hat\omega=\omega_0-2\pi k$ for the one integer $k$ that lands there.
> 3. **Rebuild:** $\hat\Omega=\frac{\hat\omega}{T}=\Omega_0-\frac{2\pi k}{T}$; in hertz $\hat f=f_0-kf_s$ with $\lvert\hat f\rvert\lt\frac{f_s}{2}$. The tone comes back unchanged exactly when $\lvert\Omega_0\rvert\lt\frac{\pi}{T}$, i.e. $f_s\gt2\lvert f_0\rvert$.
> 4. **Mind the sign:** a negative $\hat\Omega$ is a positive frequency with the odd part flipped, $\sin(\hat\Omega t)=-\sin(\lvert\hat\Omega\rvert t)$ and $\cos(\hat\Omega t+\phi)=\cos(\lvert\hat\Omega\rvert t-\phi)$.
>
> Lecture 19's example, $\sin(100\pi t)$: at $f_s=160$ and 120 Hz it comes back at $100\pi$; at 80 Hz, $\frac{5\pi}{4}\equiv-\frac{3\pi}{4}$ and $\hat\Omega=-60\pi$, so $-\sin(60\pi t)$; at 40 Hz, $\frac{5\pi}{2}\equiv\frac{\pi}{2}$ and $\hat\Omega=20\pi$. At $\hat\omega=\pm\pi$ exactly a sine's samples are all zero ($\sin(\pi n)=0$), so stay off the edge. **Backwards** (which $T$ produced this output?): write the folding condition for every tone and keep the $T$ that satisfy all of them (SP2023 #8(b), SP2025 #6(c)).

> [!recipe] E · The ideal D/A of a few samples
> One sample, one sinc: $x_a(t)=\sum_nx[n]\,\mathrm{sinc}\!\left(\frac{\pi(t-nT)}{T}\right)$, with Lecture 18's $\mathrm{sinc}(t)=\frac{\sin t}{t}$; written out, each pulse is $\dfrac{\sin(\pi(t-nT)/T)}{\pi(t-nT)/T}$, which also matches the keys that use $\frac{\sin\pi x}{\pi x}$. Each pulse equals $x[n]$ at $t=nT$ and 0 at every other multiple of $T$, so $x_a(nT)=x[n]$; between the samples it is not zero, and it extends to $t\lt0$ (not causal). Never an impulse, never a staircase: the zero-order hold of FA2019 #8(a) is not part of Lecture 18.

> [!example] Python: add the copies before you draw
> ```python
> import numpy as np
>
> # Practice 2(c): trapezoid X_a (1 out to 100pi, down to 0 at 300pi) sampled at T = 1/240 s.
> Xa = lambda W: np.clip((300 * np.pi - np.abs(W)) / (200 * np.pi), 0, 1)
> T = 1 / 240
> w = np.linspace(-np.pi, np.pi, 24001)
> Xd = sum(Xa((w - 2 * np.pi * k) / T) for k in range(-3, 4)) / T   # add the copies, then look
> for wk in [0, 3 * np.pi / 4, np.pi]:
>     print(f"X_d({wk/np.pi:.2f} pi) = {Xd[np.argmin(np.abs(w - wk))]:.1f}")
> print("mean over one period =", round(np.trapezoid(Xd, w) / (2 * np.pi), 3))
> ```
> ```text
> X_d(0.00 pi) = 240.0
> X_d(0.75 pi) = 144.0
> X_d(1.00 pi) = 144.0
> mean over one period = 200.0
> ```
> The overlap on $\frac{3\pi}{4}\le\lvert\omega\rvert\le\pi$ comes out flat, and the period average is $x[0]=x_c(0)=200$.

> [!trap] Where the points go
> - **Strict or not?** Lecture 17 says $f_s\gt2B$. The keys answer "the largest $T$ with no aliasing" with $T=\frac{1}{2B}$ (FA2021 #5(a): $F_s\ge8000$ Hz, $T\le0.125$ ms; FA2024 #5(a): $T_N=1$ ms, the copies "touch") because a sketched $X_c$ is zero at its edge, but $T\lt\frac{1}{2B}$ for a sinusoid (SP2025 #6(b)): $\sin(40\pi t)$ at $T=\frac{1}{40}$ is $\sin(\pi n)=0$. Say which case you are in.
> - **Forgetting the $\frac1T$:** 5000, not 1, in SP2021 #8; 1000 and 500 in FA2024 #5(b).
> - **$B$ in Hz, $2\pi B$ in rad/s.** $1000\pi$ rad/s is 500 Hz, so $T_N=1$ ms, not $\frac{1}{2000}$ s (FA2024 #5(a)); SP2023 #7(a) wants the rate *in Hz*; FA2021 #5(a) wants the period, not the rate.
> - **A sine, or a phase, flips.** $\Omega_0=-200\pi$ gives $-\sin(\frac{\pi}{2}n)$ in FA2023 #3; $T=\frac{7}{400}$ turns $+\frac{\pi}{4}$ into $-\frac{\pi}{4}$ in SP2023 #8(a).
> - **"Consistent with" means modulo $2\pi$.** $T=\frac{1}{40}$ aliases $\sin(100\pi t)$ and still gives $\sin(\frac{\pi}{2}n)$ (SP2025 #6(a) iv); rejecting it "because it aliases" loses the point. Some $T$ always exists for a cosine (SP2021 #1(a), SP2023 #1(a)).
> - **Overlapping copies are added, not drawn side by side** (SP2021 #8, FA2024 #5(b)); they may even cancel (SP2023 #7(c)).
> - **Axis names.** $X_d$ is drawn against $\omega$ in rad/sample (the FA2024 key labels its $X_d$ sketches "$\Omega$", a slip); a larger $T$ *stretches* it (FA2021 #1(e)).
> - **T/F reflexes:** only bandlimited signals have a Nyquist rate (FA2023 #1(d)); $x_c(at)$ multiplies it by $a$ (SP2021 #1(b), SP2023 #1(b)); an ideal D/A never outputs an impulse (FA2023 #1(e), FA2024 #1(c)).
> - **The folded sign.** A sine whose frequency folds to a negative $\hat\omega$ comes back as $-\sin$: $f_s=55$ Hz fails SP2025 #6(c) for that reason alone. A recovered tone must also land below $\frac{f_s}{2}$; $T=\frac{2}{150}$ also sends $300\pi$ to DC in SP2023 #8(b) but moves the $100\pi$ tone.

> (a) Nyquist rate and Nyquist period of $x_c$. (b) The same for $y_c(t)=x_c(3t)$. (c) And for $z_c(t)=x_c(t)\cos(400\pi t)$. (d) Write $x[n]$ when $x_c$ is sampled at exactly $f_s=500$ Hz. What is missing?

> [!success]- Solution
> **(a)** $\cos a\sin b=\frac12[\sin(a+b)-\sin(a-b)]$, so $x_c(t)=\frac12\sin(500\pi t)+\frac12\sin(100\pi t)+3\cos(50\pi t+\frac{\pi}{3})$: tones at 250, 50 and 25 Hz. $B=250$ Hz, $f_{\text{Nyquist}}=500$ Hz, $T_N=2$ ms. Not 100 or 150 Hz: a product's highest frequency is the sum.
> **(b)** Every frequency triples: $B=750$ Hz, $f_{\text{Nyquist}}=1500$ Hz, $T_N=\frac{1}{1500}$ s.
> **(c)** Modulation moves each tone to $f\pm200$ Hz; the highest is $250+200=450$ Hz, so $f_{\text{Nyquist}}=900$ Hz, $T_N=\frac{1}{900}$ s.
> **(d)** $T=\frac{1}{500}$: $x[n]=\frac12\sin(\pi n)+\frac12\sin(\frac{\pi}{5}n)+3\cos(\frac{\pi}{10}n+\frac{\pi}{3})$, and $\sin(\pi n)=0$: the 250 Hz tone is **gone**. Sampling *at* $2B$ loses a sinusoid sitting exactly at $B$; that is why the criterion is strict.
> (checked: FFTs of $x_c$, $y_c$, $z_c$ sampled at 20–40 kHz show tones up to exactly 250, 750 and 450 Hz; the 500 Hz samples match the three-term formula.)

> [!question] Practice 2 — a trapezoid at three rates
> $X_a(\Omega)=1$ for $\lvert\Omega\rvert\le100\pi$, falls linearly to 0 at $\lvert\Omega\rvert=300\pi$, and is 0 beyond. Sketch $X_d(\omega)$ on $[-\pi,\pi]$ for (a) $T=\frac{1}{400}$ s, (b) $T=\frac{1}{300}$ s, (c) $T=\frac{1}{240}$ s, giving every height and break point. (d) What is $x[0]$ in each case?

> [!success]- Solution
> $B=150$ Hz, $T_N=\frac{1}{300}$ s. The flat top ends at $100\pi T$, the ramp at $300\pi T$; the height is $\frac1T$.
> **(a)** $T=\frac{1}{400}$ ($f_s\gt300$): height 400, flat to $\frac{\pi}{4}$, ramp to 0 at $\frac{3\pi}{4}$, zero out to $\pi$; the next copy begins at $2\pi-\frac{3\pi}{4}=\frac{5\pi}{4}$, a guard band of width $\frac{\pi}{2}$.
> **(b)** $T=\frac{1}{300}=T_N$: height 300, flat to $\frac{\pi}{3}$, ramp to 0 at exactly $\pi$; the copies touch.
> **(c)** $T=\frac{1}{240}$ ($f_s\lt300$): height 240, flat to $\frac{5\pi}{12}$, then the ramp $240\,\frac{5\pi/4-\omega}{5\pi/6}$, which reaches 0 only at $\frac{5\pi}{4}$, beyond $\pi$. The copy centred at $2\pi$ rises from $2\pi-\frac{5\pi}{4}=\frac{3\pi}{4}$ as $240\,\frac{\omega-3\pi/4}{5\pi/6}$; on $\frac{3\pi}{4}\le\omega\le\pi$ the two ramps add to $240\cdot\frac{\pi/2}{5\pi/6}=144$, a constant:
> $$
> X_d(\omega)=\begin{cases}240,&\lvert\omega\rvert\le\frac{5\pi}{12}\\[2pt]240\,\dfrac{5\pi/4-\lvert\omega\rvert}{5\pi/6},&\frac{5\pi}{12}\le\lvert\omega\rvert\le\frac{3\pi}{4}\\[2pt]144,&\frac{3\pi}{4}\le\lvert\omega\rvert\le\pi.\end{cases}
> $$
> (At $T=\frac{1}{200}$ s the ramps overlap completely and $X_d=200$ everywhere: $x[n]=200\,\delta[n]$.)
> **(d)** $x[0]=x_c(0)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int X_a\,d\Omega=\frac{400\pi}{2\pi}=200$ in every case, and each sketch averages to 200 over $[-\pi,\pi]$: for (a), $\frac{1}{2\pi}\cdot2\left(400\cdot\frac{\pi}{4}+\frac12\cdot400\cdot\frac{\pi}{2}\right)=200$ ✓.
> (checked: copy sums on a grid against the three piecewise formulas, the constant 200 at $T=\frac{1}{200}$, and the period averages.)

> [!question] Practice 3 — which $T$ give $\cos(3\pi n/4)$?
> $x_c(t)=\cos(60\pi t)$ is sampled to $x[n]=\cos(\frac{3\pi}{4}n)$. (a) Find every sampling period $T$ that does this. (b) Which of $T=\frac{1}{80},\frac{1}{60},\frac{1}{48},\frac{1}{40},\frac{1}{16}$ s are consistent? (c) Repeat (b) for $\sin(60\pi t)\to\sin(\frac{3\pi}{4}n)$.

> [!success]- Solution
> **(a)** $60\pi T\equiv\pm\frac{3\pi}{4}\pmod{2\pi}$: $T=\frac{3/4+2k}{60}=\frac{3+8k}{240}$ ($k\ge0$) or $T=\frac{-3/4+2k}{60}=\frac{8k-3}{240}$ ($k\ge1$): $\frac{1}{80},\frac{1}{48},\frac{11}{240},\frac{13}{240},\frac{19}{240},\frac{21}{240},\dots$
> **(b)** $\frac{1}{80}$: $60\pi T=\frac{3\pi}{4}$ ✓. $\frac{1}{48}$: $\frac{5\pi}{4}\equiv-\frac{3\pi}{4}$ ✓ (aliased, same samples). $\frac{1}{60}$: $\pi$, giving $(-1)^n$ ✗. $\frac{1}{40}$: $\frac{3\pi}{2}\equiv-\frac{\pi}{2}$, giving $\cos(\frac{\pi}{2}n)$ ✗. $\frac{1}{16}$: $\frac{15\pi}{4}\equiv-\frac{\pi}{4}$ ✗. Answer: $\frac{1}{80}$ and $\frac{1}{48}$.
> **(c)** A sine needs $60\pi T\equiv+\frac{3\pi}{4}$, i.e. only $T=\frac{3+8k}{240}$: $\frac{1}{80}$ ✓, but $\frac{1}{48}$ gives $\sin(\frac{5\pi}{4}n)=-\sin(\frac{3\pi}{4}n)$ ✗.
> (checked: brute force over $T=\frac{m}{2400}$, $m\le480$, 240 samples each: exactly $m\in\{30,50,110,130,\dots\}$ for the cosine, $\{30,110,190,\dots\}$ for the sine.)

> [!question] Practice 4 — which $\Omega_0$, with a phase
> $\cos(\Omega_0t+\frac{\pi}{6})$ sampled at $f_s=250$ Hz gives $\cos(\frac{2\pi}{5}n+\frac{\pi}{6})$. (a) Which of $\Omega_0=-900\pi,\ -100\pi,\ 100\pi,\ 400\pi,\ 600\pi,\ 1100\pi$ rad/s are possible? (b) Which would be possible without the phase, $\cos(\Omega_0t)\to\cos(\frac{2\pi}{5}n)$?

> [!success]- Solution
> **(a)** The phase fixes the sign: $\frac{\Omega_0}{250}\equiv+\frac{2\pi}{5}\pmod{2\pi}$, so $\Omega_0=100\pi+500\pi k$: four of the six, $-900\pi$, $100\pi$, $600\pi$, $1100\pi$. $-100\pi$ gives $\cos(-\frac{2\pi}{5}n+\frac{\pi}{6})=\cos(\frac{2\pi}{5}n-\frac{\pi}{6})$, and $400\pi$ gives $\frac{8\pi}{5}\equiv-\frac{2\pi}{5}$, the same wrong sign.
> **(b)** A cosine is even, so $\Omega_0T\equiv\pm\frac{2\pi}{5}$ and $\Omega_0=\pm100\pi+500\pi k$: all six work.
> (checked: 240 samples of each candidate against the target sequence.)

> [!question] Practice 5 — True or False
> (a) A signal with $X_a(\Omega)=0$ for $\lvert\Omega\rvert\ge2\pi B$, sampled at exactly $f_s=2B$, has overlapping spectral copies.
> (b) $\cos(100\pi t)$ sampled with $T=\frac{1}{100}$ s gives $(-1)^n$.
> (c) If $x_c(t)$ is bandlimited to $B$ Hz, the Nyquist rate of $x_c(t)\cos(\Omega_ct)$ is $2B+\Omega_c/\pi$ Hz.
> (d) Doubling $T$ halves the height of $X_d(\omega)$ and doubles the width of each copy.

> [!success]- Solution
> **(a) False.** The edge lands at $\omega=2\pi BT=\pi$: the copies touch at odd multiples of $\pi$ without overlapping (FA2024 #5(b) at $T_N$).
> **(b) True.** $\omega=100\pi\cdot\frac{1}{100}=\pi$ and $\cos(\pi n)=(-1)^n$.
> **(c) True.** Modulation shifts the band edge to $2\pi B+\Omega_c$ rad/s, i.e. $B+\frac{\Omega_c}{2\pi}$ Hz, and twice that is $2B+\frac{\Omega_c}{\pi}$ (Practice 1(c): $500+400=900$ Hz).
> **(d) True.** The height is $\frac1T$ and every landmark sits at $\Omega T$: doubling $T$ halves the one and doubles the other (FA2021 #1(e) in reverse).
> (checked: (b) sample by sample; (c) by an FFT of a two-tone signal with $B=100$ Hz times $\cos(300\pi t)$, highest tone 250 Hz; (d) the Practice 2 trapezoid at $T=\frac{1}{600}$ and $\frac{1}{300}$.)

> [!question] Practice 6 — what the ideal D/A gives back
> $x_a(t)=3\cos(240\pi t+\frac{\pi}{6})+\sin(60\pi t)$ goes through an ideal A/D and an ideal D/A with the same $T$. (a) Find $y_a(t)$ for $f_s=200$ Hz. (b) What is the smallest $f_s$ with $y_a=x_a$? (c) For which $f_s$ between 100 and 240 Hz does the 120 Hz term come out at 40 Hz, and with which phase?

> [!success]- Solution
> **(a)** The 120 Hz term: $\omega_0=\frac{240\pi}{200}=1.2\pi\equiv-0.8\pi$, so $\hat\Omega=-0.8\pi\cdot200=-160\pi$ and it returns as $3\cos(-160\pi t+\frac{\pi}{6})=3\cos(160\pi t-\frac{\pi}{6})$: at $200-120=80$ Hz, phase flipped. The 30 Hz term: $0.3\pi$, unaliased.
> $$
> y_a(t)=3\cos\!\left(160\pi t-\tfrac{\pi}{6}\right)+\sin(60\pi t).
> $$
> **(b)** $f_s\gt240$ Hz, strictly: at 240 Hz the first term samples to $3\cos(\pi n+\frac{\pi}{6})=\frac{3\sqrt3}{2}(-1)^n$, and its amplitude and phase are lost.
> **(c)** $\lvert120-kf_s\rvert=40$ with $40\lt\frac{f_s}{2}$: $k=1$ gives $f_s=80$ or 160 Hz, $k=2$ gives 40 or 80 Hz; only $f_s=160$ Hz is in range. There $\omega_0=\frac{3\pi}{2}\equiv-\frac{\pi}{2}$, $\hat\Omega=-80\pi$, and the term returns as $3\cos(80\pi t-\frac{\pi}{6})$ (the 30 Hz term is still below $\frac{f_s}{2}=80$ Hz).
> (checked: the samples of $x_a$ equal those of the stated $y_a$ at 200 and 160 Hz, both $y_a$ lie below $\frac{f_s}{2}$, and a scan of $f_s$ from 100 to 240 Hz in 0.5 Hz steps finds only 160 Hz.)
