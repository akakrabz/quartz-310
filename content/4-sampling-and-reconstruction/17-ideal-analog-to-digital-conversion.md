> [!abstract] In one breath
> - Sampling = multiplying by an impulse train: the spectrum is copied every $2\pi/T$ rad/s, each copy scaled by $\frac1T$.
> - With $\omega=\Omega T$ the copies sit every $2\pi$: that is the DTFT $X_d(\omega)$.
> - Bandlimited to $B$ Hz: copies stay apart iff $f_s\gt2B$ (Nyquist). Below, they overlap: aliasing.

## 1. Ideal sampling of analog signals

> [!key] Ideal sampling (notes eqs. 1–2)
> $$
> x[n]=x_a(nT),\qquad-\infty\lt n\lt\infty,\qquad f_s=\frac1T .
> $$
> Instantaneous measurement of $x_a(t)$ every $T$ seconds. Notes write $x(t)$; exams also $x_c(t)$, $X_c(\Omega)$.

Key question (slide 3): which $T$ guarantee that $x_a(t)$ can be recovered from $x[n]$?

Sifting property: $x_a(t)\,\delta(t-t_0)=x_a(t_0)\,\delta(t-t_0)$. One impulse measures one value; an impulse train measures all of them.

> [!key] Impulse-train model (notes eqs. 4–5)
> $$
> x_s(t)=x_a(t)\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\delta(t-nT)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}x_a(nT)\,\delta(t-nT)
> $$
> Notes: $x_{\text{sampled}}(t)$. **Still continuous-time**: an impulse of area $x_a(nT)$ at each $t=nT$, zero between. One value per interval of width $T$ (from $\tfrac{3T}{2}$ to $\tfrac{5T}{2}$: only $t=2T$). Storing the areas as a sequence gives $x[n]$ (notes Figure 1).

Subscript $a$: analog spectra; $d$: digital. $\Omega$ in rad/s, $\omega$ in rad/sample. Two transform facts:
- Windowing: $x_a(t)\,w(t)\leftrightarrow\frac{1}{2\pi}X_a(\Omega)*W_a(\Omega)$.
- Impulse train: $\sum_n\delta(t-nT)\leftrightarrow\frac{2\pi}{T}\sum_n\delta\!\left(\Omega-\frac{2\pi}{T}n\right)$.

> [!key] Sampled spectrum (notes eqs. 10–11)
> $$
> X_s(\Omega)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\left[X_a(\Omega)*\frac{2\pi}{T}\sum_n\delta\!\left(\Omega-\frac{2\pi}{T}n\right)\right]=\frac1T\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}X_a\!\left(\Omega-\frac{2\pi}{T}\,n\right)
> $$
> Copies of $X_a$ every $\dfrac{2\pi}{T}$ rad/s ($f_s$ Hz), each scaled by $\dfrac1T$. Overlapping copies add. Last step: convolving with $\delta(\Omega-\Omega_0)$ shifts to $\Omega_0$.

### Example 1: varying T (slide 6)

> [!question] Example 1
> Let $X_a(\Omega)=\operatorname{rect}\!\left(\dfrac{\Omega}{20\pi}\right)$ (height 1 on $|\Omega|\le10\pi$, bandlimit $5$ Hz). Sketch $X_s(\Omega)$ (a) for $T=\frac{1}{20}$ s on $-100\pi\lt\Omega\lt100\pi$; (b) for $T=\frac{1}{10}$ s on $-50\pi\lt\Omega\lt50\pi$.

> [!success]- Answer (a)
> - Spacing $\frac{2\pi}{T}=40\pi$, height $\frac1T=20$.
> - Boxes of height $20$, width $20\pi$, centred at $-80\pi,-40\pi,0,40\pi,80\pi$.
> - **Gap of $20\pi$** between neighbours (e.g. $10\pi$ to $30\pi$). No overlap.

> [!success]- Answer (b)
> - Spacing $\frac{2\pi}{T}=20\pi$, height $10$; centres $0,\pm20\pi,\pm40\pi$.
> - Each box is exactly as wide as the spacing: copies **touch**, no overlap.
> - Sum: $X_s(\Omega)=10$ for all $\Omega$. Box shape gone, yet nothing overlaps: the at-Nyquist case of §2 ($f_s=10$ Hz $=2\cdot5$ Hz).

### 1.1 Converting to a discrete-time sequence

Sample a sinusoid: $x_a(t)=\sin(\Omega t)$ gives $x[n]=\sin(\Omega nT)=\sin(\omega n)$ (slide 7 uses $\cos(\Omega_0t)$).

> [!key] Analog and digital frequency (notes eq. 14)
> $$
> \omega=\Omega\,T\qquad\left(\frac{\text{rad}}{\text{sample}}=\frac{\text{rad}}{\text{s}}\cdot\frac{\text{s}}{\text{sample}}\right),\qquad\Omega=\frac{\omega}{T}.
> $$
> Half the sampling rate, $f=f_s/2$, lands at $\omega=\pi$.

> [!key] DTFT after ideal A/D (notes eqs. 15–16)
> $$
> X_d(\omega)=X_s(\Omega)\Big|_{\Omega=\omega/T}=\frac1T\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}X_a\!\left(\frac{\omega-2\pi n}{T}\right).
> $$
> Copies every $2\pi$; axis scaled by $T$ (edge $2\pi B\to2\pi BT$); height $\frac1T$. Without overlap, $X_d(\omega)=\frac1T\,X_a\!\left(\frac{\omega}{T}\right)$ for $|\omega|\le\pi$.

To sketch $X_d$: scale the axis by $T$, copy every $2\pi$, multiply heights by $\frac1T$, add overlaps, read off $[-\pi,\pi]$.

## 2. Nyquist criteria

Copies sit $2\pi/T$ apart. $T$ too large: they overlap. For bandlimited signals this can be prevented.

> [!key] Bandlimited signal (notes eq. 17)
> $$
> X_a(\Omega)=0\qquad\text{for }|\Omega|\gt2\pi B .
> $$
> $B$: highest linear frequency, in Hz ($B=\Omega_{\max}/2\pi$).

No overlap: right edge of the $n=0$ copy left of the left edge of the $n=1$ copy (notes eqs. 18–20, slide 8):

$$
2\pi B\ \lt\ \frac{2\pi}{T}-2\pi B\quad\Longrightarrow\quad2B\lt\frac1T\quad\Longrightarrow\quad f_s\gt2B .
$$

> [!key] Nyquist criterion and Nyquist rate (notes eqs. 20–21, slide 9)
> $$
> f_s\gt2B\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad T\lt\frac{1}{2B},\qquad\qquad f_{\text{Nyquist}}=2B .
> $$
> Then $x_a(t)$ can be recovered from $x[n]$; otherwise aliasing. Exams call $\frac{1}{2B}$ the **Nyquist period** (interval) $T_N$.

Three regimes (notes Figure 3):
- **Above** ($f_s\gt2B$): a **guard band** between $2\pi B$ and $\frac{2\pi}{T}-2\pi B$.
- **At** ($f_s=2B$): no guard band; copies touch at their edges, no overlap.
- **Below** ($f_s\lt2B$): copies overlap and add into a new shape: **aliasing** (Lectures 18–19).

> [!trap] Traps
> - Label the height $\frac1T$: a height-1 triangle sampled at $1$ kHz has height $1000$.
> - Spacing is $2\pi/T$ in $\Omega$, $2\pi$ in $\omega$. Do not mix the axes.
> - "Bandlimited to $B$ Hz" means $|\Omega|\le2\pi B$; the Nyquist rate is $2B$ Hz ($\Omega_{\max}=1000\pi$ → $1000$ Hz, not $2000\pi$).
> - Nyquist is strict: at $f_s=2B$ the copies touch, with no guard band.
> - Overlapping copies: draw their sum, not the separate copies.
