> [!abstract] In one breath
> - D/A: place $x[n]$ at $t=nT$ and interpolate, $x_a(t)=\sum_nx[n]\,g(t-nT)=x_s(t)*g(t)$.
> - Ideal $g$: low-pass $G_a=T$ on $|\Omega|\le\frac{\pi}{T}$, so $g(t)=\mathrm{sinc}\!\left(\frac{\pi t}{T}\right)$. Keeps the central copy, undoes the $\frac1T$.
> - Exact recovery when the copies do not overlap ($f_s\gt2B$).

## 1. Ideal reconstruction of bandlimited signals

Recap ([[4-sampling-and-reconstruction/17-ideal-analog-to-digital-conversion|Lecture 17]]): $x_s(t)=\sum_nx_a(nT)\,\delta(t-nT)$, $X_s(\Omega)=\frac1T\sum_kX_a\!\left(\Omega-\frac{2\pi}{T}k\right)$. Goal: recover bandlimited $x_a(t)$ from $x[n]$. Re-associate each sample with $t=nT$ (usually the sampling $T$); slide 4 asks what $x_a(t)$ is for $t\ne nT$.

Filling in between samples ("connecting the dots") is **interpolation** (notes Figure 1).

> [!key] Reconstruction formula (notes eq. 4, slide 5)
> $$
> x_a(t)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}x[n]\,g(t-nT).
> $$
> One interpolating pulse $g(t)$ per sample, centred at $nT$, scaled by $x[n]$, all added. Choosing a D/A means choosing $g$.

With $x[n]=x_s(nT)$ and $x_s(\tau)$ nonzero only at $\tau=nT$ (notes eqs. 5–7), the sum is a **continuous-time convolution**: flip and shift $g$, integrate.

$$
x_a(t)=\sum_nx[n]\,g(t-nT)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x_s(\tau)\,g(t-\tau)\,d\tau=x_s(t)*g(t).
$$

> [!key] Interpolation in frequency (notes eqs. 8–9)
> $$
> X_s(\Omega)\,G_a(\Omega)=\frac1T\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}X_a\!\left(\Omega-\frac{2\pi}{T}k\right)G_a(\Omega),\qquad g(t)\ \overset{\mathcal F}{\longleftrightarrow}\ G_a(\Omega).
> $$
> Choose $G_a$ so that this equals $X_a(\Omega)$.

> [!key] Ideal reconstruction filter (notes eq. 10, slide 8)
> $$
> G_a(\Omega)=\begin{cases}T, & |\Omega|\le\dfrac{\pi}{T}\\[4pt] 0, & |\Omega|\gt\dfrac{\pi}{T}\end{cases}\;=\;T\,\operatorname{rect}\!\left(\frac{\Omega}{2\pi/T}\right).
> $$
> Ideal low-pass: cutoff $\frac{\pi}{T}$ rad/s ($\frac{f_s}{2}$ Hz), gain $T$. Keeps the central copy ($k=0$), deletes the others, undoes the $\frac1T$: $X_s(\Omega)G_a(\Omega)=T\cdot\frac1TX_a(\Omega)=X_a(\Omega)$.

> [!key] Ideal interpolating pulse (notes eqs. 11–13)
> $$
> g(t)=\mathrm{sinc}\!\left(\frac{\pi t}{T}\right),\qquad\text{with the course's}\quad\mathrm{sinc}(u)=\begin{cases}1, & u=0\\[2pt] \dfrac{\sin u}{u}, & u\ne0.\end{cases}
> $$
> $g(0)=1$; $g(kT)=\frac{\sin(\pi k)}{\pi k}=0$ for every integer $k\ne0$.

> [!key] Sinc interpolation formula (notes eq. 14, slide 7)
> $$
> x_a(t)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}x[n]\,\mathrm{sinc}\!\left(\frac{\pi(t-nT)}{T}\right).
> $$
> Each sinc is as tall as its sample and has nulls at all other multiples of $T$, so $x_a(nT)=x[n]$. Between samples every sinc contributes.

Assumption: the central copy is separable from the others, i.e. $f_s\gt2B$ (Lecture 17). Below the Nyquist rate the copies overlap: aliasing ([[4-sampling-and-reconstruction/19-aliasing|Lecture 19]]). The sinc is not causal, so the ideal D/A cannot be built exactly.

## 2. Example: sketching ideal D/A conversion (slide 9)

> [!question] Slide 9
> Let $x[n]=3\delta[n-1]+\delta[n-4]$. Sketch $x_a(t)$ if we recover $x[n]$ by ideal sinc interpolation with sampling period $T=2$ seconds.

> [!success]- Answer
> - Samples: $x[1]=3$ at $t=2$ s, $x[4]=1$ at $t=8$ s.
> - One sinc per sample, zero crossings every $2$ s from its centre:
> $$
> x_a(t)=3\,\mathrm{sinc}\!\left(\frac{\pi(t-2)}{2}\right)+\mathrm{sinc}\!\left(\frac{\pi(t-8)}{2}\right).
> $$
> - $x_a(2)=3$, $x_a(8)=1$; $x_a(t)=0$ at all other even $t$.
> - Between samples both contribute:
> $$
> \begin{gathered}
> x_a(1)=\frac{6}{\pi}-\frac{2}{7\pi}=\frac{40}{7\pi}\approx1.82,\quad x_a(3)=\frac{32}{5\pi}\approx2.04,\\
> x_a(5)=-\frac{8}{3\pi}\approx-0.85,\quad x_a(7)=\frac{16}{5\pi}\approx1.02,\quad x_a(9)=\frac{8}{7\pi}\approx0.36
> \end{gathered}
> $$
> - Shape: main lobe of height $3$ over $0\lt t\lt4$, dip near $t=4.9$, second lobe peaking at about $1.15$ near $t=7.5$ (the first sinc's side lobe adds), ripples dying out both ways.

## 3. Overall sampling system example (slides 10–11)

$x_a(t)\to$ ideal A/D ($T$) $\to x[n]\to H_d(\omega)\to y[n]\to$ ideal D/A ($T$) $\to y_a(t)$.

The D/A applied to any $y[n]$: $\sum_ny[n]\,g(t-nT)\leftrightarrow Y_d(\Omega T)\,G_a(\Omega)$, so

> [!key] Ideal D/A in frequency
> $$
> Y_a(\Omega)=\begin{cases}T\,Y_d(\Omega T), & |\Omega|\lt\dfrac{\pi}{T}\\[4pt] 0, & \text{otherwise.}\end{cases}
> $$
> One period of $Y_d$, relabelled $\Omega=\omega/T$, height times $T$.

Three sketches: $X_d$ (Lecture 17), then $Y_d=H_dX_d$, then $Y_a$.

> [!question] Slide 10 — $T=\frac{1}{80}$ s
> $X_a(\Omega)$ is a semicircle of height $1$ out to $\pm60\pi$ rad/s. $H_d(\omega)$ is an ideal low-pass with cutoff $\omega_c=\frac{\pi}{2}$. Sketch (a) $X_d(\omega)$, (b) $Y_d(\omega)$, (c) $Y_a(\Omega)$.

> [!success]- Answer
> **(a)** Edge $60\pi\cdot\frac{1}{80}=\frac{3\pi}{4}$, height $80$. Semicircles at every $2\pi k$; next copy starts at $\frac{5\pi}{4}$: guard band ($80$ Hz above the $60$ Hz Nyquist rate).
>
> **(b)** Keep $|\omega|\le\frac{\pi}{2}$ of every copy: height $80$ at $0$, $\frac{80\sqrt5}{3}\approx59.6$ at the cut.
>
> **(c)** $\Omega=80\,\omega$, height $\times\frac{1}{80}$. Cutoff $\Omega_c=\frac{\pi/2}{1/80}=40\pi$ rad/s:
> $$
> Y_a(\Omega)=\begin{cases}\sqrt{1-(\Omega/60\pi)^2}, & |\Omega|\le40\pi\\ 0, & \text{otherwise,}\end{cases}
> $$
> the original semicircle cut at $\pm40\pi$.

> [!question] Slide 11 — $T=\frac{1}{50}$ s
> Same $X_a$ and $H_d$. Sketch (a) $X_d(\omega)$, (b) $Y_d(\omega)$, (c) $Y_a(\Omega)$.

> [!success]- Answer
> **(a)** Edge $60\pi\cdot\frac{1}{50}=\frac{6\pi}{5}\gt\pi$, height $50$. Copies **overlap on $\frac{4\pi}{5}\le|\omega|\le\frac{6\pi}{5}$** (aliasing; $50$ Hz below $60$ Hz). The two arcs add: corner at $\frac{4\pi}{5}$ (value $\frac{50\sqrt5}{3}\approx37.3$), maximum $\frac{50\sqrt{11}}{3}\approx55.3$ at $\pm\pi$.
>
> **(b)** $\frac{\pi}{2}\lt\frac{4\pi}{5}$: the overlap is in the stopband. $Y_d$ is the clean top of the central semicircle, height $50$, cut at $\pm\frac{\pi}{2}$ (value $\approx45.5$), repeated every $2\pi$.
>
> **(c)** $\Omega=50\,\omega$, height $\times\frac{1}{50}$: the semicircle cut at $\pm25\pi$ rad/s, height $1$. **Undistorted** despite aliasing.

> [!trap] Traps
> - Two sincs: course $\frac{\sin u}{u}$ gives $\mathrm{sinc}\!\left(\frac{\pi t}{T}\right)$; tables and NumPy ($\frac{\sin\pi x}{\pi x}$) write it $\mathrm{sinc}(t/T)$.
> - One sample becomes one sinc, never an impulse; its peak is at $t=nT$.
> - The D/A gain $T$ cancels the sampler's $\frac1T$: $Y_a$ has the heights of $X_a$.
> - $Y_a$ is one copy: zero beyond $\pm\frac{\pi}{T}$, no repetition.
> - Analog cutoff is $\omega_c/T$; do not write the digital $\frac{\pi}{2}$ on the $\Omega$ axis.
