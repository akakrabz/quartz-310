Units 1–3 worked with sequences $x[n]$ and asked nothing about where they came from. Unit 4 goes back to the beginning: a sequence is usually the **samples** of a continuous-time signal, $x[n]=x_a(nT)$, taken every $T$ seconds, and a digital system usually ends by turning a sequence back into a continuous-time signal. The questions are what sampling does to the frequency content of a signal, when the samples hold enough to recover the signal, and what an analog signal looks like after it has been sampled, filtered by an LTI system of Units 2–3 and converted back. Lecture 17 answers the first two: multiplying by an impulse train copies the analog spectrum every $2\pi/T$ rad/s, scaled by $1/T$; renaming the axis by $\omega=\Omega T$ gives the DTFT of the samples with its copies every $2\pi$; and for a signal bandlimited to $B$ hertz the copies stay apart exactly when $f_s=1/T\gt2B$, the **Nyquist criterion**. Lecture 18 goes back: an ideal D/A converter places a sinc at every sample, which in frequency is a low-pass of height $T$ that keeps the central copy, so a signal sampled above the Nyquist rate comes back exactly, and a digital filter between the converters acts on the analog signal like $H_d(\Omega T)$. Lecture 19 samples too slowly on purpose: the copies overlap, the shape is lost, and a sampled sinusoid comes back at a different frequency, its **alias**.

> [!key] The unit on one card (Lectures 17–19)
> - **Ideal sampling** ([[concepts/sampling|sampling]]): $x[n]=x_a(nT)$, sampling period $T$ (s), sampling frequency $f_s=\frac1T$ (Hz). Model: $x_s(t)=x_a(t)\sum_n\delta(t-nT)=\sum_nx_a(nT)\,\delta(t-nT)$, still continuous-time.
> - **The sampled spectrum:** $X_s(\Omega)=\dfrac1T\displaystyle\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}X_a\!\left(\Omega-\frac{2\pi}{T}n\right)$ — copies of $X_a$ every $\frac{2\pi}{T}$ rad/s, each scaled by $\frac1T$, added where they overlap (from $x_a(t)f(t)\leftrightarrow\frac{1}{2\pi}X_a*F_a$ with $\sum_n\delta(t-nT)\leftrightarrow\frac{2\pi}{T}\sum_n\delta(\Omega-\frac{2\pi}{T}n)$).
> - **Analog and digital frequency:** $\omega=\Omega T$ (rad/s × s/sample = rad/sample); a tone at $f$ Hz lands at $\omega=2\pi f/f_s$, and $f_s/2$ lands at $\pi$.
> - **The DTFT of the samples:** $X_d(\omega)=X_s(\Omega)\big|_{\Omega=\omega/T}=\dfrac1T\displaystyle\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}X_a\!\left(\frac{\omega-2\pi n}{T}\right)$: axis scaled by $T$, copies every $2\pi$, heights scaled by $\frac1T$. Without overlap, $X_d(\omega)=\frac1TX_a(\frac{\omega}{T})$ on $\lvert\omega\rvert\le\pi$.
> - **Bandlimited and Nyquist** ([[concepts/nyquist-criterion|Nyquist criterion]]): $X_a(\Omega)=0$ for $\lvert\Omega\rvert\gt2\pi B$ ($B$ in Hz). No overlap $\iff2\pi B\lt\frac{2\pi}{T}-2\pi B\iff f_s\gt2B\iff T\lt\frac{1}{2B}$. Nyquist rate $2B$; Nyquist period $T_N=\frac{1}{2B}$. Above: a guard band; at exactly $2B$: the copies touch; below: they overlap and add — aliasing.
> - **A sampled sinusoid:** $\cos(\Omega_0t)\to\cos(\omega_0n)$, $\omega_0=\Omega_0T$; any $\Omega_0'$ with $\Omega_0'T\equiv\pm\Omega_0T\pmod{2\pi}$ gives the same samples (for a sine the sign matters).
> - **Ideal D/A** ([[4-sampling-and-reconstruction/18-ideal-digital-to-analog-conversion|L18]], [[concepts/ideal-reconstruction|ideal reconstruction]]): $x_a(t)=\sum_nx[n]\,\mathrm{sinc}\!\left(\frac{\pi(t-nT)}{T}\right)$ with the course's $\mathrm{sinc}(t)=\frac{\sin t}{t}$; in frequency a low-pass of height $T$ on $\lvert\Omega\rvert\le\frac{\pi}{T}$, so $Y_a(\Omega)=T\,Y_d(\Omega T)$ there and $0$ outside. Exact recovery when the copies do not overlap ($f_s\gt2B$).
> - **A/D → $H_d$ → D/A:** $Y_d=H_dX_d$; a digital cutoff $\omega_c$ is the analog cutoff $\omega_c/T$; with no aliasing in the passband the chain acts like $H_a(\Omega)=H_d(\Omega T)$ on $\lvert\Omega\rvert\lt\frac{\pi}{T}$ (overlap starts at $\lvert\omega\rvert=2\pi-\Omega_{\max}T$).
> - **Aliasing** ([[4-sampling-and-reconstruction/19-aliasing|L19]], [[concepts/aliasing|aliasing]]): below the Nyquist rate the copies overlap and the shape is lost for good. A tone $\Omega_0$ lands at $\Omega_0T$, reduced mod $2\pi$ into $(-\pi,\pi]$; the D/A returns $\hat\Omega=\hat\omega/T$, in hertz the $f_0-kf_s$ closest to $0$ (a sine keeps the sign: $\sin(100\pi t)$ at $80$ Hz returns $-\sin(60\pi t)$). At exactly $2B$ recovery needs $X_d(\pm\pi)=0$: $\sin(\Omega_0t)$ at $f_s=\Omega_0/\pi$ samples to all zeros.
