> [!abstract] In one breath
> - Sampling maps $\Omega$ to $\omega=\Omega T$; nothing is lost if the band edge lands inside $(-\pi,\pi)$: $f_s\gt2B$.
> - Below the Nyquist rate the copies overlap: **aliasing**. The ideal D/A returns a different signal, for good.
> - A sinusoid comes back at its principal alias $\hat\omega\in(-\pi,\pi]$, i.e. at $\hat\Omega=\hat\omega/T$.

## 1. Nyquist criterion

- $\omega=\Omega T$ maps analog to digital frequency.
- The DTFT is $2\pi$-periodic: everything $x[n]$ holds sits in $\omega\in[-\pi,\pi]$.
- So every frequency of $X_a(\Omega)$ must land inside $[-\pi,\pi]$. Checking the largest, $\Omega_{\max}=2\pi B$, is enough.

> [!key] Nyquist criterion (notes eqs. 1–5)
> $x_a(t)$ is **bandlimited** to $B$ hertz if
> $$
> X_a(\Omega)=0,\qquad|\Omega|\gt2\pi B .
> $$
> It can be recovered from $x[n]=x_a(nT)$ if
> $$
> 2\pi BT\lt\pi\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad T\lt\frac{1}{2B}\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad f_s\gt2B .
> $$
> The **Nyquist rate** is $2B$ (hertz). Otherwise we incur **aliasing**.

## 2. Aliasing effect

- Above the Nyquist rate (Figure 1(b)): copies separate.
- At the Nyquist rate (Figure 1(c)): possible when $X_d(\pm\pi)=0$. That condition is sufficient; what fails is an impulse at $\pm\pi$, i.e. a sinusoid at exactly $B$ hertz (slide 7, §2.1).
- Below it (Figure 1(d)): copies overlap. The shape of $X_a(\Omega)$ is lost in $[-\pi,\pi]$, so ideal D/A returns a corrupted signal.

> [!key] Aliasing (notes §2, slide 8)
> A signal sampled below the Nyquist rate takes on a different appearance, an **alias**: $x[n]$ no longer expresses the original signal. Once a signal is aliased, its original representation cannot be recovered.

Aliasing of a general signal is hard to compute. For sinusoids it is exact.

### 2.1 Aliasing of sinusoidal signals

Sample $x_a(t)=\sin(\Omega_0t)$ (notes eqs. 6–8):

$$
x[n]=\sin(\Omega_0nT)=\sin(\omega_0n),\qquad\omega_0=\Omega_0T .
$$

$B=\frac{\Omega_0}{2\pi}$, so aliasing occurs when $T\gt\frac{1}{2B}=\frac{\pi}{\Omega_0}$, i.e. $\omega_0\gt\pi$.

> [!question] Slide 7 — sampling exactly at the Nyquist rate
> Sample $x_a(t)=\sin(\Omega_0t)$ with $T=\frac{1}{2B}=\frac{\pi}{\Omega_0}$. What is $x[n]$?

> [!success]- Answer (notes eqs. 9–11)
> $$
> x[n]=\sin\!\left(\Omega_0n\frac{\pi}{\Omega_0}\right)=\sin(\pi n)=0\qquad\text{for all }n .
> $$
> Every sample hits a zero crossing; no way to tell what was sampled. A cosine gives $\cos(\pi n)=(-1)^n$, but $\cos(\Omega_0t+\phi)$ gives $\cos\phi\,(-1)^n$: amplitude and phase merge. Hence the strict $f_s\gt2B$.

> [!key] Principal alias and recovered frequency
> $n$ is an integer, so $\sin\big((\omega_0+2\pi k)n\big)=\sin(\omega_0n)$ for every integer $k$ (a cosine also accepts $-\omega_0+2\pi k$). The **principal alias** is the $\hat\omega=\omega_0-2\pi k\in(-\pi,\pi]$. Ideal D/A with the same $T$ returns
> $$
> \hat x(t)=\sin(\hat\Omega t),\qquad\hat\Omega=\frac{\hat\omega}{T}\ \text{rad/s}.
> $$
> No aliasing: $\hat\Omega=\Omega_0$. Aliasing: $\hat\Omega\ne\Omega_0$, possibly negative, $\sin(\hat\Omega t)=-\sin(|\hat\Omega|t)$.

In frequency (notes eq. 13), repeated every $2\pi$:

$$
\sin(\omega_0n)\ \overset{\mathcal F}{\longleftrightarrow}\ -j\pi\big(\delta(\omega-\omega_0)-\delta(\omega+\omega_0)\big).
$$

> [!question] Notes eqs. 12–17, slides 9–13 — $\sin(100\pi t)$ at four rates
> $x_a(t)=\sin(100\pi t)$: $B=50$ Hz, Nyquist rate $100$ Hz. Sample at $f_s=160$, $120$, $80$, $40$ Hz. For each find $\omega_k=\Omega_0T_k$, its principal alias, $x_k[n]$ and the recovered $\hat\Omega_k$.

> [!success]- Answer
> | $f_s$ | $\omega_k=100\pi T_k$ | principal alias $\hat\omega_k$ | $x_k[n]$ | $\hat\Omega_k=\hat\omega_k/T_k$ | $\hat x_k(t)$ |
> |---|---|---|---|---|---|
> | $160$ Hz | $\frac{5\pi}{8}$ | $\frac{5\pi}{8}$ | $\sin\!\left(\frac{5\pi}{8}n\right)$ | $100\pi$ | $\sin(100\pi t)$ |
> | $120$ Hz | $\frac{5\pi}{6}$ | $\frac{5\pi}{6}$ | $\sin\!\left(\frac{5\pi}{6}n\right)$ | $100\pi$ | $\sin(100\pi t)$ |
> | $80$ Hz | $\frac{5\pi}{4}$ | $-\frac{3\pi}{4}$ | $\sin\!\left(\frac{5\pi}{4}n\right)=\sin\!\left(-\frac{3\pi}{4}n\right)$ | $-60\pi$ | $\sin(-60\pi t)=-\sin(60\pi t)$ |
> | $40$ Hz | $\frac{5\pi}{2}$ | $\frac{\pi}{2}$ | $\sin\!\left(\frac{5\pi}{2}n\right)=\sin\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2}n\right)$ | $20\pi$ | $\sin(20\pi t)$ |
>
> - 160, 120 Hz: $\omega_k\lt\pi$, no aliasing.
> - 80 Hz: $\frac{5\pi}{4}-2\pi=-\frac{3\pi}{4}$; $\hat\Omega=-\frac{3\pi}{4}\cdot80=-60\pi$ rad/s, a **30 Hz** sine with its sign flipped. In the DTFT, the arrow at $-\frac{3\pi}{4}$ is the $+\omega_0$ arrow ($-j\pi$) of the copy at $-2\pi$; the one at $\frac{3\pi}{4}$ comes from the copy at $2\pi$ ($+j\pi$).
> - 40 Hz: $\frac{5\pi}{2}-2\pi=\frac{\pi}{2}$; $\hat\Omega=\frac{\pi}{2}\cdot40=20\pi$ rad/s, a **10 Hz** sine. The copies at $\pm4\pi$ also appear, at $\pm\frac{3\pi}{2}$.
> - In hertz: $50$, $50$, $-30$, $10$ Hz, i.e. $50-kf_s$ with $k=0,0,1,1$.

> [!recipe] Where does a sampled tone go?
> 1. $\omega_0=\Omega_0T=2\pi\frac{f_0}{f_s}$.
> 2. Reduce into $(-\pi,\pi]$: $\hat\omega=\omega_0-2\pi k$, $k$ the integer nearest to $\frac{f_0}{f_s}$.
> 3. $\hat\Omega=\frac{\hat\omega}{T}$ rad/s, i.e. $\hat f=f_0-kf_s$ Hz.
> 4. If $\hat\Omega\lt0$: $A\sin(\hat\Omega t)=-A\sin(|\hat\Omega|t)$; $A\cos(\hat\Omega t+\phi)=A\cos(|\hat\Omega|t-\phi)$.

> [!question] Slide 19 — possible frequencies
> $x_a(t)=\cos(\Omega_0t)$ sampled at $f_s=100$ Hz gives $x[n]=\cos\!\left(\frac{\pi}{3}n\right)$. Give two possible $\Omega_0$. (Hint: consider aliasing.)

> [!success]- Answer
> $x[n]=\cos\!\left(\frac{\Omega_0}{100}n\right)$; the cosine is even and $\omega$ counts modulo $2\pi$:
> $$
> \frac{\Omega_0}{100}\equiv\pm\frac{\pi}{3}\pmod{2\pi}\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad\Omega_0=\pm\frac{100\pi}{3}+200\pi k .
> $$
> Two choices: $\boldsymbol{\Omega_0=\frac{100\pi}{3}}$ **rad/s** (no aliasing) and $\boldsymbol{\Omega_0=\frac{500\pi}{3}}$ **rad/s**: $\frac{500\pi}{3}\cdot\frac{1}{100}=\frac{5\pi}{3}$, and $\cos\!\left(\frac{5\pi}{3}n\right)=\cos\!\left(2\pi n-\frac{\pi}{3}n\right)=\cos\!\left(\frac{\pi}{3}n\right)$. Others: $\frac{700\pi}{3},\ \frac{1100\pi}{3},\dots$

> [!trap] Where points go
> - **The sine's sign:** at $80$ Hz the output is $-\sin(60\pi t)$, not $\sin(60\pi t)$.
> - **Exactly at the Nyquist rate is not safe:** a sine there samples to zeros; the criterion is strict, $f_s\gt2B$.
> - **Hz vs rad/s:** $\hat\Omega=-60\pi$ rad/s is $-30$ Hz, not "$60\pi$ Hz".
> - **Aliasing cannot be undone:** $\sin(100\pi t)$ and $-\sin(60\pi t)$ give identical samples at $80$ Hz.
> - **"Consistent" is not "recovered":** aliased $\Omega_0$ fit given samples; the D/A returns only the principal alias.
