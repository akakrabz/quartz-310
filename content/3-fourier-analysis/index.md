Units 1 and 2 described an LTI system by its impulse response $h[n]$ and by its transfer function $H(z)$. Unit 3 asks what the system does to each **frequency**. Lecture 12 is the bridge: one last look at convolution in the time domain, now as a sliding comparison of the input with a template (the course does not test this lecture). Lecture 13 builds the new tool, from the Fourier series of periodic signals to the **discrete-time Fourier transform** (DTFT) of any absolutely summable sequence. Lecture 14 ties it to Unit 2: the DTFT is the z-transform evaluated on the unit circle whenever the ROC contains that circle, and most of its properties are z-transform properties at $z = e^{j\omega}$, joined by a few new ones (Hermitian symmetry, Parseval, modulation, windowing). Lectures 15–16 turn it on systems: the DTFT of $h[n]$ is the **frequency response** $H_d(\omega)$, which scales and shifts every sinusoid that passes through, and its magnitude, phase and group delay say by how much. Lectures 17 onward (ideal filters, sampling and reconstruction, the DFT and FFT) will be added as the course reaches them.

> [!key] The unit on one card
> - **DTFT and its inverse** ([[concepts/dtft|DTFT]]): $X_d(\omega)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}x[n]\,e^{-j\omega n}$ and $x[n]=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}X_d(\omega)\,e^{j\omega n}\,d\omega$. $X_d$ is $2\pi$-periodic, so it is plotted on $[-\pi,\pi]$ with $\omega$ in radians per sample; the sum converges for every $\omega$ when $\sum_n\lvert x[n]\rvert\lt\infty$.
> - **From Unit 2:** $X_d(\omega)=X(z)\big|_{z=e^{j\omega}}$ when, and only when, the ROC contains the unit circle. $2^nu[n]$ (ROC $\lvert z\rvert\gt2$) has no DTFT at all; $u[n]$ (ROC $\lvert z\rvert\gt1$) has one only with an impulse, $\frac{1}{1-e^{-j\omega}}+\pi\delta(\omega)$, which is not $X(e^{j\omega})$.
> - **Pairs** (on $[-\pi,\pi]$, repeated every $2\pi$; [[concepts/dtft-pairs|DTFT pairs]]): $\delta[n-n_0]\leftrightarrow e^{-j\omega n_0}$; $\;a^nu[n]\leftrightarrow\frac{1}{1-ae^{-j\omega}}$ for $\lvert a\rvert\lt1$; $\;u[n]-u[n-L]\leftrightarrow e^{-j\omega(L-1)/2}\,\frac{\sin(L\omega/2)}{\sin(\omega/2)}$; $\;e^{j\omega_0n}\leftrightarrow2\pi\delta(\omega-\omega_0)$; $\;\cos(\omega_0n)\leftrightarrow\pi\big[\delta(\omega-\omega_0)+\delta(\omega+\omega_0)\big]$; $\;\frac{\sin(\omega_cn)}{\pi n}\leftrightarrow 1$ for $\lvert\omega\rvert\le\omega_c$ and $0$ for $\omega_c\lt\lvert\omega\rvert\le\pi$.
> - **Properties** ([[concepts/dtft-properties|DTFT properties]]): $x[n-n_0]\leftrightarrow e^{-j\omega n_0}X_d(\omega)$; $\;e^{j\omega_0n}x[n]\leftrightarrow X_d(\omega-\omega_0)$; $\;x[n]\cos(\omega_0n)\leftrightarrow\frac12X_d(\omega-\omega_0)+\frac12X_d(\omega+\omega_0)$; $\;x[-n]\leftrightarrow X_d(-\omega)$; $\;x^*[n]\leftrightarrow X_d^*(-\omega)$; $\;n\,x[n]\leftrightarrow +j\,\frac{dX_d(\omega)}{d\omega}$ (Lecture 14's table prints $-j$, an erratum); $\;x[n]*h[n]\leftrightarrow X_d(\omega)H_d(\omega)$; $\;x[n]w[n]\leftrightarrow\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}X_d(\theta)\,W_d(\omega-\theta)\,d\theta$. **Parseval:** $\sum_n\lvert x[n]\rvert^2=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\lvert X_d(\omega)\rvert^2\,d\omega$. **Hermitian symmetry:** real $x$ ⇒ $X_d(-\omega)=X_d^*(\omega)$, so the magnitude is even and the phase odd. **Free values:** $X_d(0)=\sum_nx[n]$, $X_d(\pi)=\sum_n(-1)^nx[n]$, $\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}X_d(\omega)\,d\omega=2\pi\,x[0]$.
> - **Systems** ([[concepts/frequency-response|frequency response]]): $H_d(\omega)$ is the DTFT of $h[n]$. BIBO stable ⇔ $\sum_n\lvert h[n]\rvert\lt\infty$ ⇔ the ROC contains the unit circle, and then $H_d(\omega)=H(e^{j\omega})$. Eigenfunctions: $e^{j\omega_0n}\mapsto H_d(\omega_0)\,e^{j\omega_0n}$ (input for all $n$). Real $h$: $A\cos(\omega_0n+\theta)\mapsto A\,\lvert H_d(\omega_0)\rvert\cos\big(\omega_0n+\theta+\angle H_d(\omega_0)\big)$. Any input with a DTFT: $Y_d(\omega)=H_d(\omega)X_d(\omega)$.
> - **Magnitude, phase, group delay** ([[concepts/magnitude-and-phase-response|magnitude and phase response]], [[concepts/group-delay|group delay]]): $H_d(\omega)=\lvert H_d(\omega)\rvert\,e^{j\angle H_d(\omega)}$ with $\lvert H_d\rvert\ge0$ (in dB: $20\log_{10}\lvert H_d\rvert$) and $\angle H_d$ a principal angle in $[-\pi,\pi]$, which jumps by $\pi$ where a real amplitude factor changes sign and wraps by $2\pi$ to stay in range. Group delay $\tau_{gd}(\omega)=-\frac{d\angle H_d(\omega)}{d\omega}$: the delay $\delta[n-n_0]$ has phase $-\omega n_0$ and $\tau_{gd}=n_0$; $h=\delta[n]-\delta[n-2]$ has $H_d=2je^{-j\omega}\sin\omega$, $\lvert H_d\rvert=2\lvert\sin\omega\rvert$ and $\tau_{gd}=1$.
