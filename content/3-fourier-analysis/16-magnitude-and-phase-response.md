> [!abstract] In one breath
> - $H_d=|H_d|e^{j\angle H_d}$: magnitude $\ge0$ (linear or dB), phase a principal angle in $[-\pi,\pi]$.
> - Symmetric FIR: pull out $e^{-jM\omega}$, leaving a real $A(\omega)$. Sign changes of $A$ give $\pm\pi$ jumps; $2\pi$ wraps are bookkeeping.
> - Group delay $\tau_{gd}=-\frac{d\angle H_d}{d\omega}$: linear phase delays all frequencies equally; non-linear phase smears the signal.

## 1. Complex numbers review

$$
z=a+jb=Re^{j\theta},\qquad |z|=R=\sqrt{a^2+b^2}=\sqrt{zz^*},\qquad z^*=a-jb=Re^{-j\theta}.
$$

$\tan^{-1}(b/a)$ alone cannot tell $1+j$ from $-1-j$; slide 3 fixes the quadrant:

$$
\angle z=\begin{cases}\tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{b}{a}\right), & a\gt 0\\[4pt] \tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)+\pi, & a\lt 0,\ b\gt 0\\[4pt] \tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)-\pi, & a\lt 0,\ b\lt 0\end{cases}
$$

On the axes: $+\frac{\pi}{2}$ for $a=0,\ b\gt0$; $-\frac{\pi}{2}$ for $a=0,\ b\lt0$; $\pm\pi$ for a negative real number.

> [!key] Principal angle and $-1=e^{\pm j\pi}$ (eqs. 6–8)
> Phase = principal angle in $[-\pi,\pi]$: add or subtract $2\pi$ until it lands there. Absorb a minus sign with
> $$
> -1=e^{j\pi}=e^{-j\pi}.
> $$
> Notes' example: $z=-2e^{j\pi/4}=2e^{j\pi/4}e^{-j\pi}=2e^{-j3\pi/4}$. Choosing $e^{+j\pi}$ gives $2e^{j5\pi/4}$, out of range. Magnitude $2$, never $-2$.

## 2. Magnitude and phase response

$H_d(\omega)=|H_d(\omega)|\,e^{j\angle H_d(\omega)}$ (eq. 12), both parts plotted on $[-\pi,\pi]$. Real $h$: magnitude even, phase odd.

### 2.1 Magnitude response

> [!key] Magnitude response (eqs. 9–10, slide 4)
> $$
> \lvert H_d(\omega)\rvert=\Big|\sqrt{H_d(\omega)H_d^*(\omega)}\Big|\ \ge 0,\qquad \lvert H_d(\omega)\rvert_{\mathrm{dB}}=20\log_{10}|H_d(\omega)| .
> $$
> - Linear scale: values as they are.
> - dB scale: for responses spanning orders of magnitude (filter design, later). Gain $1$ is $0$ dB, $\tfrac1{10}$ is $-20$ dB, a zero is $-\infty$ dB.

### 2.2 Phase response

> [!key] Phase response (eq. 11, slide 4)
> $$
> \angle H_d(\omega)=\tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{\operatorname{Im}\{H_d(\omega)\}}{\operatorname{Re}\{H_d(\omega)\}}\right)\ \text{(with the quadrant rule)}
> $$
> Plot in $[-\pi,\pi]$; where a formula leaves the strip, shift it by $2\pi$.

> [!recipe] Pull out a linear-phase factor (notes: "a common trick")
> 1. Write $H_d(\omega)=\sum_n h[n]e^{-j\omega n}$.
> 2. Factor out $e^{-jM\omega}$, $M$ = midpoint of the first and last nonzero taps. Pair terms: $e^{jk\omega}+e^{-jk\omega}=2\cos k\omega$, $e^{jk\omega}-e^{-jk\omega}=2j\sin k\omega$.
> 3. Reach $H_d(\omega)=e^{j(\alpha-M\omega)}A(\omega)$ with $A(\omega)$ **real** ($j=e^{j\pi/2}$, $-1=e^{\pm j\pi}$).
> 4. Read off $|H_d(\omega)|=|A(\omega)|$ and
> $$
> \angle H_d(\omega)=\alpha-M\omega+\angle A(\omega),\qquad \angle A(\omega)=\begin{cases}0,& A(\omega)\gt 0\\ \pm\pi,& A(\omega)\lt 0.\end{cases}
> $$
> Wrap into $[-\pi,\pi]$, choosing the sign of $\pm\pi$ that stays in range.

> [!question] Example 1 (slide 5): $h[n]=\{\underset{\uparrow}{1},0,1\}=\delta[n]+\delta[n-2]$
> (a) Determine $H_d(\omega)$, $|H_d(\omega)|$ and $\angle H_d(\omega)$. (b) Plot $|H_d(\omega)|$ and $\angle H_d(\omega)$.

> [!success]- Answer
> Symmetric about $M=1$:
> $$
> H_d(\omega)=1+e^{-j2\omega}=e^{-j\omega}\big(e^{j\omega}+e^{-j\omega}\big)=2e^{-j\omega}\cos\omega .
> $$
> $$
> \lvert H_d(\omega)\rvert=2|\cos\omega|,\qquad \angle H_d(\omega)=-\omega+\angle\cos\omega=\begin{cases}-\omega, & |\omega|\lt\frac{\pi}{2}\\ -\omega+\pi, & \frac{\pi}{2}\lt\omega\le\pi\\ -\omega-\pi, & -\pi\le\omega\lt-\frac{\pi}{2}.\end{cases}
> $$
> - Magnitude $2$ at $0$ and $\pm\pi$; $0$ at $\pm\frac{\pi}{2}$ (zeros $z=\pm j$ of $1+z^{-2}$).
> - Phase: slope $-1$, $\pi$ jumps at $\pm\frac{\pi}{2}$, inside $[-\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2}]$; $H_d(\pi)=2e^{-j\pi}\cos\pi=+2$. Figure 1, top.

> [!question] Example 2 (slide 7) = notes Exercise 1: $h[n]=\{\underset{\uparrow}{1},0,-1\}=\delta[n]-\delta[n-2]$
> Same two questions.

> [!success]- Answer
> Antisymmetric about $M=1$ (eqs. 14–20):
> $$
> H_d(\omega)=1-e^{-j2\omega}=e^{-j\omega}\big(e^{j\omega}-e^{-j\omega}\big)=2je^{-j\omega}\sin\omega=2e^{j(\frac{\pi}{2}-\omega)}\sin\omega .
> $$
> $$
> \lvert H_d(\omega)\rvert=2|\sin\omega|,\qquad \angle H_d(\omega)=\frac{\pi}{2}-\omega+\angle\sin\omega=\begin{cases}\frac{\pi}{2}-\omega, & 0\lt\omega\le\pi\\ -\frac{\pi}{2}-\omega, & -\pi\le\omega\lt 0.\end{cases}
> $$
> - Zeros at $\omega=0,\pm\pi$ (zeros $z=\pm1$ of $1-z^{-2}$).
> - $\sin\omega\lt0$ on $(-\pi,0)$ adds $-\pi$: the phase runs $\frac{\pi}{2}\to-\frac{\pi}{2}$ on each half, with a $\pi$ jump at $\omega=0$ (notes Fig. 1; Figure 1, bottom).

> [!key] Where the $\pm\pi$ jumps are
> A $\pm\pi$ jump at $\omega_0$ needs $A(\omega)$ to change sign there: a zero of $H(z)$ at $z=e^{j\omega_0}$ on the unit circle, of odd multiplicity. Example 1 (zeros $\pm j$) and Example 2 ($\pm1$) jump; Example 3's zeros $\frac{-1\pm j\sqrt2}{3}$ lie inside, so its phase is continuous. A double zero makes $|H_d|$ touch $0$ without a jump.

> [!question] Slide 6 (concept check) — which have $\pm\pi$ jumps in their phase response?
> 1. $H_1(\omega)=\cos(4\omega)$
> 2. $H_2(\omega)=e^{-j2\omega}\big(2+\cos(2\omega)\big)$
> 3. $H_3(\omega)=je^{-j3\omega}\sin(3\omega)$
> 4. $H_4(\omega)=e^{-j2\omega}\big(1-2\cos(\omega)-\cos(2\omega)\big)$
> 5. $H_5(\omega)=e^{-j2\omega}\big(3-2\cos(\omega)-\cos(2\omega)\big)$

> [!success]- Answer: $H_1$, $H_3$, $H_4$
> Only the sign of the real amplitude matters.
> - **$H_1$: yes.** $\cos4\omega$ changes sign at $\pm\frac{\pi}{8},\pm\frac{3\pi}{8},\pm\frac{5\pi}{8},\pm\frac{7\pi}{8}$.
> - **$H_2$: no.** $2+\cos2\omega\ge1\gt0$; phase $-2\omega$ with $2\pi$ wraps at $\pm\frac{\pi}{2}$.
> - **$H_3$: yes.** $\sin3\omega$ changes sign at $0,\pm\frac{\pi}{3},\pm\frac{2\pi}{3}$; $j$ adds $\frac{\pi}{2}$.
> - **$H_4$: yes.** $1-2\cos\omega-\cos2\omega=-2(\cos^2\omega+\cos\omega-1)$: $-2$ at $0$, $+2$ at $\pm\pi$, sign change at $\cos\omega=\frac{\sqrt5-1}{2}\approx0.618$ ($\omega\approx\pm0.29\pi$).
> - **$H_5$: no.** $3-2\cos\omega-\cos2\omega=2(1-\cos\omega)(2+\cos\omega)\ge0$: touches $0$ at $\omega=0$, never changes sign.

> [!trap] Phase-plot traps
> - Magnitude is never negative: $2|\sin\omega|$, with the sign moved into the phase as $\pm\pi$.
> - Example 2's phase is not the single line $\frac{\pi}{2}-\omega$: that is off by $\pi$ on $(-\pi,0)$.
> - $2\pi$ wraps are bookkeeping; $\pm\pi$ jumps are sign changes of $A(\omega)$.
> - Slide 4's "sign changes of $\operatorname{Re}\{H_d\}$" means the sign of $A(\omega)$: $\operatorname{Re}\{e^{-j2\omega}\}=\cos2\omega$ changes sign, yet $e^{-j2\omega}$ has no jump.
> - $|H_d|=0$ alone is no jump: $A$ must cross zero ($H_5$).

#### 2.2.1 Group delay

> [!question] Slides 10–11 — what does the phase do? $H_d(\omega)=e^{-j2\omega}$, $|H_d|=1$, $\angle H_d=-2\omega$
> Response to (a) $e^{j\frac{\pi}{2}n}$, (b) $e^{j\frac{3\pi}{4}n}$, (c) $e^{j\omega_0 n}$? (d) Response to $e^{j\omega_0n}$ for $H_d(\omega)=e^{-jk\omega}$, $k\in\mathbb{Z}$?

> [!success]- Answers
> (a) $H_d(\frac{\pi}{2})=e^{-j\pi}=-1$, so $y[n]=-e^{j\frac{\pi}{2}n}=e^{j\frac{\pi}{2}(n-2)}$.
> (b) $H_d(\frac{3\pi}{4})=e^{-j\frac{3\pi}{2}}=j$, so $y[n]=j\,e^{j\frac{3\pi}{4}n}=e^{j\frac{3\pi}{4}(n-2)}$. Principal angle $+\frac{\pi}{2}$, still a two-sample delay.
> (c) $y[n]=e^{-j2\omega_0}e^{j\omega_0n}=e^{j\omega_0(n-2)}=x[n-2]$: $h[n]=\delta[n-2]$.
> (d) $y[n]=e^{-jk\omega_0}e^{j\omega_0n}=e^{j\omega_0(n-k)}=x[n-k]$. **A linear phase $-k\omega$ is a pure delay of $k$ samples.**

Delay property (eqs. 28–29): $x[n-k]\overset{\mathcal{F}}{\longleftrightarrow}X_d(\omega)e^{-j\omega k}$, a linear phase $f(\omega)=-k\omega$ whose slope is set by the shift.

> [!key] Group delay (eq. 30, slide 12)
> $$
> \tau_{gd}(\omega)=-\frac{d\angle H_d(\omega)}{d\omega}\qquad\text{(in samples)}
> $$
> Differentiate the continuous phase: ignore $\pm\pi$ jumps and $2\pi$ wraps.
> - **Uniform** group delay: linear phase $\alpha-k\omega$, $\tau_{gd}(\omega)=k$. Every frequency delayed by $k$; waveform intact.
> - **Non-uniform** group delay: non-linear phase, $\tau_{gd}$ varies with $\omega$. Frequencies shift by different amounts.

- **Exercise 1 (Example 2):** phase not differentiable at $\omega=0$, slope $-1$ elsewhere, so $\tau_{gd}(\omega)=1$: uniform, one sample. Example 1 likewise.
- **Exercise 2 (Example 3):** smooth but non-linear phase, so non-uniform: $\tau_{gd}=\frac{3c(1+c)}{3c^2+4c+2}$ with $c=\cos\omega$; $\frac23$ at $\omega=0$, $0$ at $\pm\frac{\pi}{2}$, negative for $\frac{\pi}{2}\lt|\omega|\lt\pi$.

**Image denoising (notes Fig. 3, slides 13–15).** Two LTI systems with the same low-pass magnitude response filter a noisy image:
- Uniform group delay (linear phase): clean output, shifted slightly in $x$ and $y$.
- Non-uniform group delay (sinusoidal phase): same frequencies, same strengths, but "shaking" and ringing artifacts. Spatial frequencies (cycles per pixel) moved by different amounts; the image lost its coherence.

Magnitude decides which frequencies survive; phase decides where they land.
