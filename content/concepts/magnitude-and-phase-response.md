> [!key] Definition (Lecture 16)
> For an LTI system with [[concepts/frequency-response|frequency response]] $H_d(\omega)$,
> $$
> H_d(\omega)=|H_d(\omega)|\,e^{j\angle H_d(\omega)},\qquad |H_d(\omega)|=\Big|\sqrt{H_d(\omega)H_d^*(\omega)}\Big|\ge 0,\qquad -\pi\le\angle H_d(\omega)\le\pi .
> $$
> The **magnitude response** $|H_d(\omega)|$ is the gain at frequency $\omega$, plotted on a linear scale or in decibels, $20\log_{10}|H_d(\omega)|$. The **phase response** $\angle H_d(\omega)$ is the shift, plotted as a principal angle (wrapped by $2\pi$ into $[-\pi,\pi]$). Both are plotted for $\omega\in[-\pi,\pi]$. Together they say what the system does to a sinusoid with real $h$: $\cos(\omega_0n+\theta)\mapsto|H_d(\omega_0)|\cos\big(\omega_0n+\theta+\angle H_d(\omega_0)\big)$ ([[3-fourier-analysis/15-frequency-response|Lecture 15]]).

**Reading the two parts off a formula.** For short FIR filters, pull out the linear-phase factor $e^{-jM\omega}$ ($M$ = centre of $h$) and pair the remaining exponentials into cosines or sines ([[3-fourier-analysis/16-magnitude-and-phase-response|Lecture 16]] §3). The goal is

$$
H_d(\omega)=e^{j(\alpha-M\omega)}A(\omega),\quad A(\omega)\ \text{real}\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad |H_d(\omega)|=|A(\omega)|,\quad \angle H_d(\omega)=\alpha-M\omega+\begin{cases}0,&A\gt0\\ \pm\pi,&A\lt0.\end{cases}
$$

The $\pm\pi$ is $-1=e^{\pm j\pi}$: a magnitude cannot be negative, so the sign of $A$ goes into the phase.

|---|---|---|---|

| $2e^{-j\omega}\cos\omega$, $h=\{\underset{\uparrow}{1},0,1\}$ | $2\lvert\cos\omega\rvert$ | $-\omega$, plus $\pm\pi$ where $\cos\omega\lt0$ | $\pi$ jumps at $\pm\frac{\pi}{2}$ |
| $2je^{-j\omega}\sin\omega$, $h=\{\underset{\uparrow}{1},0,-1\}$ | $2\lvert\sin\omega\rvert$ | $\frac{\pi}{2}-\omega$, plus $-\pi$ for $\omega\lt0$ | $\pi$ jump at $0$ |

| $1+2\cos2\omega$ ([[exams/midterm-2/past-exams/fall-2024\|FA2024 MT2]] #3) | $\lvert1+2\cos2\omega\rvert$ | $0$, or $\pm\pi$ where $1+2\cos2\omega\lt0$ | $\pi$ jumps at $\pm\frac{\pi}{3},\pm\frac{2\pi}{3}$ |

## Where the ±π jumps come from

A phase plot can break for two different reasons, and only one of them means anything.

- A **$2\pi$ wrap** is bookkeeping: the line $-k\omega$ is folded back into the strip $[-\pi,\pi]$ every time it leaves it. `np.unwrap` removes these.
- A **$\pm\pi$ jump** means the real amplitude $A(\omega)$ crossed zero: $H_d$ passed *through* $0$ and came out pointing the opposite way. That requires $H_d(\omega_0)=0$, i.e. a **zero of $H(z)$ on the unit circle** at $z=e^{j\omega_0}$, and it requires a *crossing*: a double zero only touches.

So "where does the phase jump by $\pi$?" has a Midterm 1 answer: at the angles of the zeros of $H(z)$ that sit on the unit circle with odd multiplicity. $1+z^{-2}$ has zeros $\pm j$ (jumps at $\pm\frac{\pi}{2}$); $1-z^{-2}$ has zeros $\pm1$ (jumps at $0$ and $\pm\pi$); $3+2z^{-1}+z^{-2}$ has its zeros at radius $\frac{1}{\sqrt3}$ (no jumps); $1+2z^{-3}+z^{-6}=(1+z^{-3})^2$ has *double* zeros at $e^{\pm j\pi/3}$ and $-1$ (no jumps, only $|H_d|$ touching $0$). See [[concepts/poles-and-zeros|poles and zeros]].

> [!trap]
> - **A magnitude is never negative.** $2\sin\omega$ is not a magnitude response; $2|\sin\omega|$ is, and the sign goes into the phase as $\pm\pi$ (Lecture 16, slide 4).
> - **A sign change of $\operatorname{Re}\{H_d\}$ is not a $\pi$ jump.** $\operatorname{Re}\{e^{-j2\omega}\}=\cos2\omega$ changes sign, yet $e^{-j2\omega}$ has no jump. What matters is the sign of the real amplitude *after* the linear phase is pulled out.
> - **Touching zero is not crossing zero.** $e^{-j2\omega}(3-2\cos\omega-\cos2\omega)$ has $|H_d(0)|=0$ but no jump; $e^{-j2\omega}(1-2\cos\omega-\cos2\omega)$ jumps twice (the figure above, Lecture 16's concept check).
> - **The principal value can hide a delay.** $e^{-j2\omega}$ at $\omega=\frac{3\pi}{4}$ is $j$, principal angle $+\frac{\pi}{2}$, although the system delays by two samples. The delay is the slope, not the value: see [[concepts/group-delay|group delay]].
> - **Odd phase is a test for real $h$.** For real $h[n]$ the magnitude must be even and the phase odd; a phase that is not odd means either a complex $h$ (FA2023 MT2 #4, SP2023 MT2 #6) or a sign slip in your sketch.
> - **At a zero of $H_d$ the phase is undefined.** `np.angle(0)` returns $0$, which means nothing; and a real negative value can come back as $+\pi$ or $-\pi$ depending on the sign of a rounding error in the imaginary part. Both are the same angle.
