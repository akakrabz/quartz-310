> [!abstract] In one breath
> - $H_d=|H_d|e^{j\angle H_d}$: magnitude $\ge0$ (linear or dB), phase a principal angle in $[-\pi,\pi]$.
> - Symmetric FIR: pull out $e^{-jM\omega}$, leaving a real $A(\omega)$; $|H_d|=|A|$ and the phase is $-M\omega$ plus $\pi$ where $A\lt0$.
> - Sign changes of $A$ give $\pm\pi$ jumps; $2\pi$ wraps are bookkeeping.

## 1. Complex numbers in brief

$z=a+jb=Re^{j\theta}$, $|z|=\sqrt{a^2+b^2}=\sqrt{zz^*}$, $z^*=Re^{-j\theta}$ (Lecture 16, eqs. 1–5). $\tan^{-1}(b/a)$ alone cannot tell $1+j$ from $-1-j$: add $\pi$ for $a\lt0$, $b\gt0$ and subtract $\pi$ for $a\lt0$, $b\lt0$ (slide 3); $\pm\frac{\pi}{2}$ on the imaginary axis, $\pm\pi$ for a negative real number. Full review: [[0-toolkit/01-complex-numbers|complex numbers]].

> [!key] Principal angle and $-1=e^{\pm j\pi}$ (Lecture 16, eqs. 6–8)
> Phase = principal angle in $[-\pi,\pi]$: add or subtract $2\pi$ until it lands there. Absorb a minus sign with
> $$
> -1=e^{j\pi}=e^{-j\pi}.
> $$
> Notes' example: $z=-2e^{j\pi/4}=2e^{j\pi/4}e^{-j\pi}=2e^{-j3\pi/4}$. Choosing $e^{+j\pi}$ gives $2e^{j5\pi/4}$, out of range. Magnitude $2$, never $-2$.

## 2. Magnitude response

> [!key] Magnitude response (Lecture 16, eqs. 9–10, 12; slide 4)
> $$
> \begin{gathered}
> H_d(\omega)=|H_d(\omega)|\,e^{j\angle H_d(\omega)},\qquad \lvert H_d(\omega)\rvert=\Big|\sqrt{H_d(\omega)H_d^*(\omega)}\Big|\ \ge 0,\\
> \lvert H_d(\omega)\rvert_{\mathrm{dB}}=20\log_{10}|H_d(\omega)| .
> \end{gathered}
> $$
> Both parts plotted on $[-\pi,\pi]$; real $h$: magnitude even, phase odd.
> - Linear scale: values as they are.
> - dB scale: for responses spanning orders of magnitude (filter design, beyond Lecture 23). Gain $1$ is $0$ dB, $\tfrac1{10}$ is $-20$ dB, a zero is $-\infty$ dB.

## 3. Phase response

> [!key] Phase response (Lecture 16, eq. 11, slide 4)
> $$
> \angle H_d(\omega)=\tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{\operatorname{Im}\{H_d(\omega)\}}{\operatorname{Re}\{H_d(\omega)\}}\right)\ \text{(with the quadrant rule)}
> $$
> Plot in $[-\pi,\pi]$; where a formula leaves the strip, shift it by $2\pi$.

> [!recipe] Pull out a linear-phase factor (notes: "a common trick")
> 1. Write $H_d(\omega)=\sum_n h[n]e^{-j\omega n}$.
> 2. Factor out $e^{-jM\omega}$, $M$ = midpoint of the first and last nonzero taps. Pair terms: $e^{jk\omega}+e^{-jk\omega}=2\cos k\omega$, $e^{jk\omega}-e^{-jk\omega}=2j\sin k\omega$.
> 3. Reach $H_d(\omega)=e^{j(\alpha-M\omega)}A(\omega)$ with $A(\omega)$ **real** ($j=e^{j\pi/2}$, $-1=e^{\pm j\pi}$).
> 4. Read off $|H_d(\omega)|=|A(\omega)|$ and
> $$
> \angle H_d(\omega)=\alpha-M\omega+\angle A(\omega),\qquad \angle A(\omega)=\begin{cases}0,& A(\omega)\gt 0\\ \pm\pi,& A(\omega)\lt 0.\end{cases}
> $$
> Wrap into $[-\pi,\pi]$, choosing the sign of $\pm\pi$ that stays in range.

> [!question] Example 1 (slide 5): $h[n]=\{\underset{\uparrow}{1},0,1\}=\delta[n]+\delta[n-2]$
> (a) Determine $H_d(\omega)$, $|H_d(\omega)|$ and $\angle H_d(\omega)$. (b) Plot $|H_d(\omega)|$ and $\angle H_d(\omega)$.

> [!success]- Answer
> Symmetric about $M=1$:
> $$
> H_d(\omega)=1+e^{-j2\omega}=e^{-j\omega}\big(e^{j\omega}+e^{-j\omega}\big)=2e^{-j\omega}\cos\omega .
> $$
> $$
> \lvert H_d(\omega)\rvert=2|\cos\omega|,\qquad \angle H_d(\omega)=-\omega+\angle\cos\omega=\begin{cases}-\omega, & |\omega|\lt\frac{\pi}{2}\\ -\omega+\pi, & \frac{\pi}{2}\lt\omega\le\pi\\ -\omega-\pi, & -\pi\le\omega\lt-\frac{\pi}{2}.\end{cases}
> $$
> - Magnitude $2$ at $0$ and $\pm\pi$; $0$ at $\pm\frac{\pi}{2}$ (zeros $z=\pm j$ of $1+z^{-2}$).
> - Phase: slope $-1$, $\pi$ jumps at $\pm\frac{\pi}{2}$, inside $[-\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2}]$; $H_d(\pi)=2e^{-j\pi}\cos\pi=+2$. Figure below, top.

> [!question] Example 2 (slide 7) = notes Exercise 1: $h[n]=\{\underset{\uparrow}{1},0,-1\}=\delta[n]-\delta[n-2]$
> Same two questions.

> [!success]- Answer
> Antisymmetric about $M=1$ (eqs. 14–20):
> $$
> H_d(\omega)=1-e^{-j2\omega}=e^{-j\omega}\big(e^{j\omega}-e^{-j\omega}\big)=2je^{-j\omega}\sin\omega=2e^{j(\frac{\pi}{2}-\omega)}\sin\omega .
> $$
> $$
> \lvert H_d(\omega)\rvert=2|\sin\omega|,\qquad \angle H_d(\omega)=\frac{\pi}{2}-\omega+\angle\sin\omega=\begin{cases}\frac{\pi}{2}-\omega, & 0\lt\omega\le\pi\\ -\frac{\pi}{2}-\omega, & -\pi\le\omega\lt 0.\end{cases}
> $$
> - Zeros at $\omega=0,\pm\pi$ (zeros $z=\pm1$ of $1-z^{-2}$).
> - $\sin\omega\lt0$ on $(-\pi,0)$ adds $-\pi$: the phase runs $\frac{\pi}{2}\to-\frac{\pi}{2}$ on each half, with a $\pi$ jump at $\omega=0$ (notes Fig. 1; figure below, bottom).

## 4. Jumps and wraps

> [!key] Where the $\pm\pi$ jumps are
> A $\pm\pi$ jump at $\omega_0$ needs $A(\omega)$ to change sign there: a zero of $H(z)$ at $z=e^{j\omega_0}$ on the unit circle, of odd multiplicity. Example 1 (zeros $\pm j$) and Example 2 ($\pm1$) jump; Example 3's zeros $\frac{-1\pm j\sqrt2}{3}$ lie inside, so its phase is continuous. A double zero makes $|H_d|$ touch $0$ without a jump: $1+2z^{-3}+z^{-6}=(1+z^{-3})^2$ has none. A **$2\pi$ wrap** is bookkeeping, the line folded into $[-\pi,\pi]$.

> [!question] Slide 6 (concept check): which have $\pm\pi$ jumps in their phase response?
> 1. $H_1(\omega)=\cos(4\omega)$
> 2. $H_2(\omega)=e^{-j2\omega}\big(2+\cos(2\omega)\big)$
> 3. $H_3(\omega)=je^{-j3\omega}\sin(3\omega)$
> 4. $H_4(\omega)=e^{-j2\omega}\big(1-2\cos(\omega)-\cos(2\omega)\big)$
> 5. $H_5(\omega)=e^{-j2\omega}\big(3-2\cos(\omega)-\cos(2\omega)\big)$

> [!success]- Answer
> **$H_1$, $H_3$, $H_4$.**
>
> Only the sign of the real amplitude matters.
> - **$H_1$: yes.** $\cos4\omega$ changes sign at $\pm\frac{\pi}{8},\pm\frac{3\pi}{8},\pm\frac{5\pi}{8},\pm\frac{7\pi}{8}$.
> - **$H_2$: no.** $2+\cos2\omega\ge1\gt0$; phase $-2\omega$ with $2\pi$ wraps at $\pm\frac{\pi}{2}$.
> - **$H_3$: yes.** $\sin3\omega$ changes sign at $0,\pm\frac{\pi}{3},\pm\frac{2\pi}{3}$; $j$ adds $\frac{\pi}{2}$.
> - **$H_4$: yes.** $1-2\cos\omega-\cos2\omega=-2(\cos^2\omega+\cos\omega-1)$: $-2$ at $0$, $+2$ at $\pm\pi$, sign change at $\cos\omega=\frac{\sqrt5-1}{2}\approx0.618$ ($\omega\approx\pm0.29\pi$).
> - **$H_5$: no.** $3-2\cos\omega-\cos2\omega=2(1-\cos\omega)(2+\cos\omega)\ge0$: touches $0$ at $\omega=0$, never changes sign.

## 5. Midterm 2 quick check

> [!question] Q1 · Four taps, two kinds of zeros
> $h[n]=\{\underset{\uparrow}{1},2,2,1\}$. (a) Write $H_d(\omega)=e^{-jM\omega}A(\omega)$ with $A$ real. (b) Where does $\angle H_d$ jump by $\pi$? Give $\angle H_d$ on $[0,\pi]$. (c) $H_d(\frac{\pi}{2})$ in polar form.

> [!success]- Answer
> **(a)** Symmetric about $M=\frac32$:
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> H_d(\omega)&=e^{-j\frac32\omega}\left(2\cos\tfrac{3\omega}{2}+4\cos\tfrac{\omega}{2}\right)\\
> &=e^{-j\frac32\omega}\,\underbrace{2\cos\tfrac{\omega}{2}\,(1+2\cos\omega)}_{A(\omega)} .
> \end{aligned}
> $$
> **(b)** $A$ changes sign at $\omega=\pm\frac{2\pi}{3}$ (zeros $e^{\pm j2\pi/3}$ of $(1+z^{-1})(1+z^{-1}+z^{-2})$) and at $\pm\pi$ (zero $z=-1$). On $[0,\pi]$:
> $$
> \angle H_d(\omega)=\begin{cases}-\frac32\omega, & 0\le\omega\lt\frac{2\pi}{3}\\ \pi-\frac32\omega, & \frac{2\pi}{3}\lt\omega\le\pi\end{cases}
> $$
> The first piece reaches $-\pi$ at the jump; the second runs $0\to-\frac{\pi}{2}$. Odd on $[-\pi,0]$; $|H_d|=|A|$, $6$ at $\omega=0$; group delay $\frac32$.
>
> **(c)** $H_d(\frac{\pi}{2})=1-2j-2+j=-1-j=\sqrt2\,e^{-j3\pi/4}$, third quadrant (not $\tan^{-1}1=\frac{\pi}{4}$).

> [!question] Q2 · True or False
> (a) For $h=\{\underset{\uparrow}{1},-3,1\}$ the principal phase jumps at $\omega=0$, so $A(\omega)$ changes sign there. (b) $|H_d(\omega_0)|=0.1$ is an attenuation of $10$ dB.

> [!success]- Answer
> (a) **False.** $H_d=e^{-j\omega}(2\cos\omega-3)$ with $A\le-1\lt0$ everywhere: no sign change. The continuous phase is $\pi-\omega$; in $[-\pi,\pi]$ it reads $\pi-\omega$ for $\omega\gt0$ and $-\pi-\omega$ for $\omega\lt0$: a $2\pi$ wrap at $0$, where $H_d(0)=-1$.
> (b) **False.** $20\log_{10}0.1=-20$ dB: a $20$ dB attenuation.

> [!trap]
> - Magnitude is never negative: $2|\sin\omega|$, not $2\sin\omega$; the sign moves into the phase as $\pm\pi$.
> - Example 2's phase is not the single line $\frac{\pi}{2}-\omega$: that is off by $\pi$ on $(-\pi,0)$.
> - $2\pi$ wraps are bookkeeping; $\pm\pi$ jumps are sign changes of $A(\omega)$. $|H_d|=0$ alone is no jump ($H_5$).
> - Slide 4's "sign changes of $\operatorname{Re}\{H_d\}$" means the sign of $A(\omega)$: $\operatorname{Re}\{e^{-j2\omega}\}=\cos2\omega$ changes sign, yet $e^{-j2\omega}$ has no jump.
> - The value is not the delay: $e^{-j2\omega}$ at $\frac{3\pi}{4}$ has principal angle $+\frac{\pi}{2}$; see [[concepts/group-delay|group delay]].
> - Real $h$ ⇒ even magnitude, odd phase; a non-odd phase means complex $h$ ([[exams/midterm-2/past-exams/fall-2023|FA2023 MT2]] #4, [[exams/midterm-2/past-exams/spring-2023|SP2023 MT2]] #6).
