> [!abstract] In one breath
> - Below the Nyquist rate the copies overlap: **aliasing**. $x[n]$ no longer expresses $x_a(t)$ and the ideal D/A returns a different signal, for good.
> - A sinusoid comes back at its principal alias $\hat\omega\in(-\pi,\pi]$, i.e. at $\hat\Omega=\hat\omega/T$ rad/s; in hertz, $\hat f=f_0-kf_s$ closest to $0$.
> - $\sin(100\pi t)$ at $80$ Hz returns $-\sin(60\pi t)$; at $40$ Hz, $\sin(20\pi t)$.

## 1. What aliasing is

Lecture 19, Figure 1 ([[concepts/nyquist-criterion|Nyquist criterion]] §3):
- Above the Nyquist rate (Figure 1(b)): copies separate.
- At the Nyquist rate (Figure 1(c)): possible when $X_d(\pm\pi)=0$; what fails is a sinusoid at exactly $B$ hertz ([[concepts/nyquist-criterion|Nyquist criterion]] §4).
- Below it (Figure 1(d)): copies overlap. The shape of $X_a(\Omega)$ is lost in $[-\pi,\pi]$, so ideal D/A returns a corrupted signal.

> [!key] Aliasing (Lecture 19 §2, slide 8)
> A signal sampled below the Nyquist rate ($f_s\lt2B$) takes on a different appearance, an **alias**: the copies in $X_d(\omega)=\frac1T\sum_kX_a\!\left(\frac{\omega-2\pi k}{T}\right)$ overlap and $x[n]$ no longer expresses the original signal. Once a signal is aliased, its original representation cannot be recovered. The ideal D/A returns
> $$
> \hat X_a(\Omega)=\sum_kX_a\!\left(\Omega-\frac{2\pi k}{T}\right)\ \text{ for }|\Omega|\le\frac{\pi}{T},\qquad 0\ \text{ beyond}.
> $$

Aliasing of a general signal is hard to compute. For sinusoids it is exact.

## 2. Aliasing of sinusoids

Sample $x_a(t)=\sin(\Omega_0t)$ (Lecture 19, eqs. 6–8):

$$
x[n]=\sin(\Omega_0nT)=\sin(\omega_0n),\qquad\omega_0=\Omega_0T .
$$

$B=\frac{\Omega_0}{2\pi}$, so aliasing occurs when $T\gt\frac{1}{2B}=\frac{\pi}{\Omega_0}$, i.e. $\omega_0\gt\pi$. At exactly $T=\frac{\pi}{\Omega_0}$ the samples are $\sin(\pi n)=0$ (slide 7, worked on [[concepts/nyquist-criterion|Nyquist criterion]] §4).

> [!key] Principal alias and recovered frequency
> $n$ is an integer, so $\sin\big((\omega_0+2\pi k)n\big)=\sin(\omega_0n)$ for every integer $k$ (a cosine also accepts $-\omega_0+2\pi k$). The **principal alias** is the $\hat\omega=\omega_0-2\pi k\in(-\pi,\pi]$. Ideal D/A with the same $T$ returns
> $$
> \hat x(t)=\sin(\hat\Omega t),\qquad\hat\Omega=\frac{\hat\omega}{T}\ \text{rad/s}.
> $$
> No aliasing: $\hat\Omega=\Omega_0$. Aliasing: $\hat\Omega\ne\Omega_0$, possibly negative, $\sin(\hat\Omega t)=-\sin(|\hat\Omega|t)$.

| sampled signal | same samples as |
|---|---|
| $\cos(\omega_0n)$ | $\cos\big((\pm\omega_0+2\pi k)n\big)$ |
| $\sin(\omega_0n)$ | $\sin\big((\omega_0+2\pi k)n\big)$; $-\omega_0+2\pi k$ gives $-\sin(\omega_0n)$ |

In frequency (Lecture 19, eq. 13), repeated every $2\pi$:

$$
\sin(\omega_0n)\ \overset{\mathcal F}{\longleftrightarrow}\ -j\pi\big(\delta(\omega-\omega_0)-\delta(\omega+\omega_0)\big).
$$

## 3. A 50 Hz sine at four sampling rates

> [!question] Lecture 19, eqs. 12–17, slides 9–13 — $\sin(100\pi t)$ at four rates
> $x_a(t)=\sin(100\pi t)$: $B=50$ Hz, Nyquist rate $100$ Hz. Sample at $f_s=160$, $120$, $80$, $40$ Hz. For each find $\omega_k=\Omega_0T_k$, its principal alias, $x_k[n]$ and the recovered $\hat\Omega_k$.

> [!success]- Answer
> | $f_s$ | $\omega_k=100\pi T_k$ | principal alias $\hat\omega_k$ | $x_k[n]$ | $\hat\Omega_k=\hat\omega_k/T_k$ | $\hat x_k(t)$ |
> |---|---|---|---|---|---|
> | $160$ Hz | $\frac{5\pi}{8}$ | $\frac{5\pi}{8}$ | $\sin\!\left(\frac{5\pi}{8}n\right)$ | $100\pi$ | $\sin(100\pi t)$ |
> | $120$ Hz | $\frac{5\pi}{6}$ | $\frac{5\pi}{6}$ | $\sin\!\left(\frac{5\pi}{6}n\right)$ | $100\pi$ | $\sin(100\pi t)$ |
> | $80$ Hz | $\frac{5\pi}{4}$ | $-\frac{3\pi}{4}$ | $\sin\!\left(\frac{5\pi}{4}n\right)=\sin\!\left(-\frac{3\pi}{4}n\right)$ | $-60\pi$ | $\sin(-60\pi t)=-\sin(60\pi t)$ |
> | $40$ Hz | $\frac{5\pi}{2}$ | $\frac{\pi}{2}$ | $\sin\!\left(\frac{5\pi}{2}n\right)=\sin\!\left(\frac{\pi}{2}n\right)$ | $20\pi$ | $\sin(20\pi t)$ |
>
> - 160, 120 Hz: $\omega_k\lt\pi$, no aliasing.
> - 80 Hz: $\frac{5\pi}{4}-2\pi=-\frac{3\pi}{4}$; $\hat\Omega=-\frac{3\pi}{4}\cdot80=-60\pi$ rad/s, a **30 Hz** sine with its sign flipped. In the DTFT, the arrow at $-\frac{3\pi}{4}$ is the $+\omega_0$ arrow ($-j\pi$) of the copy at $-2\pi$; the one at $\frac{3\pi}{4}$ comes from the copy at $2\pi$ ($+j\pi$).
> - 40 Hz: $\frac{5\pi}{2}-2\pi=\frac{\pi}{2}$; $\hat\Omega=\frac{\pi}{2}\cdot40=20\pi$ rad/s, a **10 Hz** sine. The copies at $\pm4\pi$ also appear, at $\pm\frac{3\pi}{2}$.
> - In hertz: $50$, $50$, $-30$, $10$ Hz, i.e. $50-kf_s$ with $k=0,0,1,1$.

## 4. The folding recipe

> [!recipe] Where does a sampled tone go?
> 1. $\omega_0=\Omega_0T=2\pi\frac{f_0}{f_s}$.
> 2. Reduce into $(-\pi,\pi]$: $\hat\omega=\omega_0-2\pi k$, $k$ the integer nearest to $\frac{f_0}{f_s}$ (the **principal alias**).
> 3. $\hat\Omega=\frac{\hat\omega}{T}$ rad/s, i.e. $\hat f=f_0-kf_s$ Hz, the value closest to $0$.
> 4. If $\hat\Omega\lt0$: $A\sin(\hat\Omega t)=-A\sin(|\hat\Omega|t)$; $A\cos(\hat\Omega t+\phi)=A\cos(|\hat\Omega|t-\phi)$. Keep the sign for a sine or a phase.

> [!question] Slide 19 — possible frequencies
> $x_a(t)=\cos(\Omega_0t)$ sampled at $f_s=100$ Hz gives $x[n]=\cos\!\left(\frac{\pi}{3}n\right)$. Give two possible $\Omega_0$. (Hint: consider aliasing.)

> [!success]- Answer
> $x[n]=\cos\!\left(\frac{\Omega_0}{100}n\right)$; the cosine is even and $\omega$ counts modulo $2\pi$:
> $$
> \frac{\Omega_0}{100}\equiv\pm\frac{\pi}{3}\pmod{2\pi}\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad\Omega_0=\pm\frac{100\pi}{3}+200\pi k .
> $$
> Two choices: $\boldsymbol{\Omega_0=\frac{100\pi}{3}}$ **rad/s** (no aliasing) and $\boldsymbol{\Omega_0=\frac{500\pi}{3}}$ **rad/s**: $\frac{500\pi}{3}\cdot\frac{1}{100}=\frac{5\pi}{3}$, and $\cos\!\left(\frac{5\pi}{3}n\right)=\cos\!\left(2\pi n-\frac{\pi}{3}n\right)=\cos\!\left(\frac{\pi}{3}n\right)$. Others: $\frac{700\pi}{3},\ \frac{1100\pi}{3},\dots$

- [[homework/hw8|HW8]] #1 is slide 19 at $1000$ samples/s: $\Omega_0=\frac{1000\pi(6k\pm1)}{3}$ rad/s. HW8 #2: $\sin(10\pi t)+\cos(20\pi t)$ gives $\sin(\frac\pi5n)+\cos(\frac{2\pi}{5}n)$ for $T=\frac{1+10k}{50}$ s ($\frac1{50}$, $\frac{11}{50}$, …); the sine fixes the sign, both tones must agree.
- **Avoid it:** sample faster, or (practice, Singer–Munson) an analog anti-aliasing low-pass before the A/D.
- **Allowed aliasing:** exams accept overlap a digital filter will delete, $\omega_c\le2\pi-\Omega_{\max}T$ ([[concepts/digital-processing-of-analog-signals|A/D → filter → D/A]], [[problems/digital-filtering-of-analog-signals|digital filtering of analog signals]]).

## 5. Midterm 2 quick check

> [!question] Q1 · a cosine with a phase
> $x_a(t)=3\cos(1400\pi t+\frac{\pi}{4})$ is sampled at $f_s=1$ kHz and passed through an ideal D/A. (a) $\omega_0$ and its principal alias. (b) The output. (c) Which $f_s$ avoid aliasing?

> [!success]- Answer
> - (a) $\omega_0=\frac{1400\pi}{1000}=1.4\pi$; principal alias $1.4\pi-2\pi=-0.6\pi$.
> - (b) $\hat\Omega=-0.6\pi\cdot1000=-600\pi$: $3\cos(-600\pi t+\frac\pi4)=\boldsymbol{3\cos(600\pi t-\frac{\pi}{4})}$, a $300$ Hz tone ($700-1000$ Hz) with its phase flipped.
> - (c) $f_s\gt1400$ Hz (strict).

> [!question] Q2 · True or false
> (a) At $f_s=200$ Hz, $\sin(260\pi t)$ and $-\sin(140\pi t)$ give the same samples. (b) A digital filter after the A/D can undo aliasing inside its passband. (c) A $1.2$ kHz cosine sampled at $1$ kHz comes back from the ideal D/A at $200$ Hz.

> [!success]- Answer
> - (a) **True:** $130-200=-70$ Hz, and the sine flips sign: $\sin(-140\pi t)=-\sin(140\pi t)$.
> - (b) **False:** the alias and the true component sit at the same $\omega$; nothing after the A/D separates them.
> - (c) **True:** $1200-1000=200$ Hz.

> [!trap] Where points go
> - **The sine's sign:** at $80$ Hz the output is $-\sin(60\pi t)$, not $\sin(60\pi t)$; for a sine only $+\omega_0$ reproduces the samples ([[exams/midterm-2/past-exams/spring-2023|SP2023 MT2]] #8(a), [[exams/midterm-2/past-exams/spring-2025|SP2025 MT2]] #6(c)).
> - **Exactly at the Nyquist rate is not safe:** a sine there samples to zeros; the criterion is strict, $f_s\gt2B$.
> - **Hz vs rad/s:** $\hat\Omega=-60\pi$ rad/s is $-30$ Hz, not "$60\pi$ Hz".
> - **Aliasing cannot be undone:** $\sin(100\pi t)$ and $-\sin(60\pi t)$ give identical samples at $80$ Hz.
> - **"Consistent" is not "recovered":** aliased $\Omega_0$ fit given samples ("which $T$ give these samples" accepts aliased $T$); the D/A returns only the principal alias ([[exams/midterm-2/past-exams/spring-2025|SP2025 MT2]] #6).
> - **Aliasing is the overlap, not the copies;** overlap can sum to a constant ([[exams/midterm-2/past-exams/spring-2021|SP2021 MT2]] #8(b)) or zero ([[exams/midterm-2/past-exams/spring-2023|SP2023 MT2]] #7(c)).
