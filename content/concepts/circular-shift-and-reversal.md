*Lecture 21 (Mon Oct 19) §1 · also [[5-dft-and-fft/20-discrete-fourier-transform|Lecture 20]] §2.2 · family: [[problems/circular-convolution-and-dft-properties|circular convolution and DFT properties]]*

> [!abstract] In one breath
> - DFT and inverse DFT are $N$-periodic: a length-$N$ signal acts as its periodic extension $x[\langle n\rangle_N]$.
> - $\langle n\rangle_N$ = remainder of $n$ divided by $N$, in $\{0,\dots,N-1\}$.
> - Circular reversal keeps $x[0]$ in place and reverses the rest: $x[\langle-n\rangle_N]=x[0],\,x[N-1],\,\dots,\,x[1]$.

## 1. Periodicity in frequency and time

DFT pair ([[concepts/dft|DFT]], Lecture 21 eqs. 1–2):
$$
\begin{gathered}
X[k]=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}x[n]\,e^{-j\frac{2\pi k}{N}n},\ \ 0\le k\le N-1,\\
x[n]=\frac1N\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}X[k]\,e^{j\frac{2\pi k}{N}n},\ \ 0\le n\le N-1 .
\end{gathered}
$$

> [!key] $N$-periodicity and the modulo operation (Lecture 21, eqs. 3–6; slide 5)
> $$
> X[k+mN]=X[k],\qquad x[n+mN]=x[n],\qquad m\in\mathbb Z .
> $$
> - $X[k]$ uses only $x[0],\dots,x[N-1]$; the inverse DFT returns those values, periodically extended (proofs: [[concepts/dft|DFT]] §3).
> - Modulo form: $x[n]=x[\langle n\rangle_N]$, $X[k]=X[\langle k\rangle_N]$ for all $n,k$.
> - $\langle a\rangle_N$ = remainder of $a$ divided by $N$, always in $\{0,\dots,N-1\}$: $\langle7\rangle_4=3$, $\langle-1\rangle_3=2$, $\langle6\rangle_3=0$, $\langle11\rangle_3=2$.

Every DFT belongs to a finite-length signal, periodically extended. Shifts and reversals therefore wrap around the length-$N$ window: **circular**.

## 2. Circular time reversal

> [!key] Circular time reversal (Lecture 21, eq. 19; slide 6)
> $$
> x[\langle-n\rangle_N]=\begin{cases}x[0], & n=0\\ x[N-n], & 1\le n\le N-1\end{cases}
> $$
> Reverse the periodic extension, keep one period: $\{x[0],\,x[N-1],\,x[N-2],\,\dots,\,x[1]\}$.

- **Circular shift** by $m$: $x[\langle n-m\rangle_N]$, samples leaving one end re-enter at the other; length stays $N$ (DFT: $e^{-j\frac{2\pi mk}{N}}X[k]$, [[concepts/dft-properties|DFT properties]]).

> [!question] Example 1 (slide 8) — circular shift and reversal
> $x[n]=\{\underset{\uparrow}{3},\ 1,\ 2,\ 4,\ 5\}$, length 5. Write the length-5 sequences (a) $x[\langle -n\rangle_5]$, (b) $x[\langle n+2\rangle_5]$, (c) $x[\langle 1-n\rangle_5]$.

> [!success]- Answer
> Reduce the index mod 5 for $n=0,\dots,4$:
> - (a) indices $0,4,3,2,1$: $\{\underset{\uparrow}{3},\ 5,\ 4,\ 2,\ 1\}$.
> - (b) indices $2,3,4,0,1$: $\{\underset{\uparrow}{2},\ 4,\ 5,\ 3,\ 1\}$ (circular advance by 2).
> - (c) indices $1,0,4,3,2$: $\{\underset{\uparrow}{1},\ 3,\ 5,\ 4,\ 2\}$ = (a) circularly delayed by 1, since $x[\langle 1-n\rangle_5]=x[\langle -(n-1)\rangle_5]$.

> [!trap] Circular, not linear
> - $x[\langle -n\rangle_N]=\{x[0],x[N-1],\dots,x[1]\}$: $x[0]$ stays put; not the plain flip.
> - Remainders lie in $0,\dots,N-1$: $\langle -1\rangle_3=2$, never $-1$.
> - A circular shift rotates: nothing falls off, nothing is zero-filled; length stays $N$.
> - The inverse DFT is $N$-periodic, not zero outside $0\le n\le N-1$: the inverse DFT of $\{1,2,3,4\}$ has $x[4]=x[0]=2.5$.
> - Only the DFT's implied extension is periodic; the length-$N$ signal itself is not.
