> [!abstract] In one breath
> - D/A: place $x[n]$ at $t=nT$ and interpolate, $x_a(t)=\sum_nx[n]\,g(t-nT)=x_s(t)*g(t)$.
> - Ideal $g$: low-pass $G_a=T$ on $|\Omega|\le\frac{\pi}{T}$, so $g(t)=\mathrm{sinc}\!\left(\frac{\pi t}{T}\right)$. Keeps the central copy, undoes the $\frac1T$.
> - Exact recovery when the copies do not overlap ($f_s\gt2B$).

## 1. Interpolation and the reconstruction formula

Recap (Lecture 18, eqs. 1–3): $x_s(t)=\sum_nx_a(nT)\,\delta(t-nT)$, $X_s(\Omega)=\frac1T\sum_kX_a\!\left(\Omega-\frac{2\pi}{T}k\right)$ ([[concepts/sampling|sampling]]). Goal: recover bandlimited $x_a(t)$ from $x[n]$. Re-associate each sample with $t=nT$ (usually the sampling $T$); slide 4 asks what $x_a(t)$ is for $t\ne nT$.

Filling in between samples ("connecting the dots") is **interpolation** (notes Figure 1).

> [!key] Reconstruction formula (Lecture 18, eq. 4, slide 5)
> $$
> x_a(t)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}x[n]\,g(t-nT).
> $$
> One interpolating pulse $g(t)$ per sample, centred at $nT$, scaled by $x[n]$, all added. Choosing a D/A means choosing $g$.

With $x[n]=x_s(nT)$ and $x_s(\tau)$ nonzero only at $\tau=nT$ (eqs. 5–7), the sum is a **continuous-time convolution**: flip and shift $g$, integrate.

$$
x_a(t)=\sum_nx[n]\,g(t-nT)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x_s(\tau)\,g(t-\tau)\,d\tau=x_s(t)*g(t).
$$

## 2. The ideal reconstruction filter

> [!key] Interpolation in frequency (Lecture 18, eqs. 8–9)
> $$
> X_s(\Omega)\,G_a(\Omega)=\frac1T\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}X_a\!\left(\Omega-\frac{2\pi}{T}k\right)G_a(\Omega),\qquad g(t)\ \overset{\mathcal F}{\longleftrightarrow}\ G_a(\Omega).
> $$
> Choose $G_a$ so that this equals $X_a(\Omega)$.

> [!key] Ideal reconstruction filter (Lecture 18, eq. 10, slide 8)
> $$
> G_a(\Omega)=\begin{cases}T, & |\Omega|\le\dfrac{\pi}{T}\\[4pt] 0, & |\Omega|\gt\dfrac{\pi}{T}\end{cases}\;=\;T\,\operatorname{rect}\!\left(\frac{\Omega}{2\pi/T}\right).
> $$
> Ideal low-pass: cutoff $\frac{\pi}{T}$ rad/s ($\frac{f_s}{2}$ Hz), gain $T$. Keeps the central copy ($k=0$), deletes the others, undoes the $\frac1T$: $X_s(\Omega)G_a(\Omega)=T\cdot\frac1TX_a(\Omega)=X_a(\Omega)$. Exams also say ideal D/C converter or reconstruction filter.

## 3. Sinc interpolation

> [!key] Ideal interpolating pulse (Lecture 18, eqs. 11–13)
> $$
> g(t)=\mathrm{sinc}\!\left(\frac{\pi t}{T}\right),\qquad\text{with the course's}\quad\mathrm{sinc}(u)=\begin{cases}1, & u=0\\[2pt] \dfrac{\sin u}{u}, & u\ne0.\end{cases}
> $$
> $g(0)=1$; $g(kT)=\frac{\sin(\pi k)}{\pi k}=0$ for every integer $k\ne0$.

> [!key] Sinc interpolation formula (Lecture 18, eq. 14, slide 7)
> $$
> x_a(t)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}x[n]\,\mathrm{sinc}\!\left(\frac{\pi(t-nT)}{T}\right).
> $$
> Each sinc is as tall as its sample and has nulls at all other multiples of $T$, so $x_a(nT)=x[n]$. Between samples every sinc contributes (notes Figure 3).

| view | formula |
|---|---|
| time: **sinc interpolation** | one sinc per sample at $nT$, height $x[n]$, zero at all other $kT$; $x_a(nT)=x[n]$ |
| frequency: **low-pass of height $T$** | $Y_a(\Omega)=T\,Y_d(\Omega T)$ for $\lvert\Omega\rvert\lt\frac{\pi}{T}$, $0$ outside |

- **Exact recovery** if $x_a$ is bandlimited to $B$ and $f_s\gt2B$ ([[concepts/nyquist-criterion|Nyquist criterion]]): the central copy is separable from the others, and $x_a(t)=\sum_nx_a(nT)\,\mathrm{sinc}\!\left(\frac{\pi(t-nT)}{T}\right)$.
- **Below $2B$:** the box cuts out overlapping copies: [[concepts/aliasing|aliasing]].
- The sinc is not causal, so the ideal D/A cannot be built exactly.
- A digital filter between the converters acts as one analog filter: [[concepts/digital-processing-of-analog-signals|A/D → filter → D/A]].

> [!example] Two samples: $x[n]=\delta[n]+\delta[n-1]$, $T=1$ ms
> $x_a(0)=x_a(T)=1$, $x_a(kT)=0$ otherwise, $x_a\!\left(\frac T2\right)=\frac{4}{\pi}\approx1.27$. In frequency $X_a(\Omega)=T\left(1+e^{-j\Omega T}\right)$ for $|\Omega|\lt1000\pi$, zero at the band edge since $X_d(\pi)=0$.

## 4. Sketching an ideal D/A (slide 9)

> [!question] Slide 9
> Let $x[n]=3\delta[n-1]+\delta[n-4]$. Sketch $x_a(t)$ if we recover $x[n]$ by ideal sinc interpolation with sampling period $T=2$ seconds.

> [!success]- Answer
> - Samples: $x[1]=3$ at $t=2$ s, $x[4]=1$ at $t=8$ s.
> - One sinc per sample, zero crossings every $2$ s from its centre:
> $$
> x_a(t)=3\,\mathrm{sinc}\!\left(\frac{\pi(t-2)}{2}\right)+\mathrm{sinc}\!\left(\frac{\pi(t-8)}{2}\right).
> $$
> - $x_a(2)=3$, $x_a(8)=1$; $x_a(t)=0$ at all other even $t$.
> - Between samples both contribute:
> $$
> \begin{gathered}
> x_a(1)=\frac{6}{\pi}-\frac{2}{7\pi}=\frac{40}{7\pi}\approx1.82,\quad x_a(3)=\frac{32}{5\pi}\approx2.04,\\
> x_a(5)=-\frac{8}{3\pi}\approx-0.85,\quad x_a(7)=\frac{16}{5\pi}\approx1.02,\quad x_a(9)=\frac{8}{7\pi}\approx0.36
> \end{gathered}
> $$
> - Shape: main lobe of height $3$ over $0\lt t\lt4$, dip near $t=4.9$, second lobe peaking at about $1.15$ near $t=7.5$ (the first sinc's side lobe adds), ripples dying out both ways.
