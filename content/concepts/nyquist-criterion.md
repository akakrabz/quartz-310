> [!abstract] In one breath
> - Bandlimited to $B$ Hz: $X_a(\Omega)=0$ for $|\Omega|\gt2\pi B$. Copies stay apart iff $f_s\gt2B$ (equivalently $T\lt\frac{1}{2B}$, or $2\pi BT\lt\pi$).
> - Nyquist rate $2B$ Hz, Nyquist period $T_N=\frac{1}{2B}$. Above: guard band. At: copies touch. Below: overlap, [[concepts/aliasing|aliasing]].
> - Strict: at exactly $f_s=2B$ a sine at $B$ Hz samples to all zeros.

## 1. Bandlimited signals and the no-overlap condition

Copies of $X_a$ sit $2\pi/T$ apart ([[concepts/sampling|sampling]]). $T$ too large: they overlap. For bandlimited signals this can be prevented.

> [!key] Bandlimited signal (Lecture 17, eq. 17)
> $$
> X_a(\Omega)=0\qquad\text{for }|\Omega|\gt2\pi B .
> $$
> $B$: highest linear frequency, in Hz ($B=\Omega_{\max}/2\pi$).

No overlap: right edge of the $n=0$ copy left of the left edge of the $n=1$ copy (eqs. 18–20, slide 8):

$$
2\pi B\ \lt\ \frac{2\pi}{T}-2\pi B\quad\Longrightarrow\quad2B\lt\frac1T\quad\Longrightarrow\quad f_s\gt2B .
$$

> [!key] Nyquist criterion and Nyquist rate (Lecture 17, eqs. 20–21, slide 9)
> $$
> f_s\gt2B\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad T\lt\frac{1}{2B},\qquad\qquad f_{\text{Nyquist}}=2B .
> $$
> Then $x_a(t)$ can be recovered from $x[n]$; otherwise aliasing. Exams call $\frac{1}{2B}$ the **Nyquist period** (interval) $T_N$.

## 2. Above, at and below the Nyquist rate

Three regimes (Lecture 17, Figure 3):

| sampling rate | copies | $X_d(\omega)$ on $[-\pi,\pi]$ |
|---|---|---|
| $f_s\gt2B$ | separated by a **guard band** between $2\pi B$ and $\frac{2\pi}{T}-2\pi B$ | $\frac1T\,X_a(\omega/T)$, faithful |
| $f_s=2B$ | **touch** at their edges, $\omega=\pm\pi$; no guard band, no overlap | $\frac1T\,X_a(\omega/T)$ fills the period |
| $f_s\lt2B$ | **overlap and add** into a new shape | [[concepts/aliasing\|aliasing]] |

> [!question] Example 2 (slides 10–12) — sampling around the Nyquist rate
> $$
> X_a(\Omega)=\begin{cases}1-\dfrac{|\Omega|}{100\pi}, & |\Omega|\le100\pi\\[4pt] 0, & |\Omega|\gt100\pi\end{cases}
> $$
> Sketch $X_d(\omega)$ for (a) $T=\frac{1}{150}$ s, (b) $T=\frac{1}{100}$ s, (c) $T=\frac{1}{75}$ s.

Edge $100\pi$ rad/s: $B=50$ Hz, Nyquist rate $100$ Hz. So $150$, $100$, $75$ Hz are above, at, below. Each copy: height $\frac1T$, half-width $100\pi T$, centred at multiples of $2\pi$.

> [!success]- Answer (a) $T = 1/150$ s, above
> - Height $150$; half-width $100\pi\cdot\frac{1}{150}=\frac{2\pi}{3}$.
> - Central copy: $X_d(\omega)=150\left(1-\frac{|\omega|}{2\pi/3}\right)=150-\frac{225}{\pi}|\omega|$ for $|\omega|\le\frac{2\pi}{3}$.
> - Next copy starts at $\frac{4\pi}{3}$: **guard band** $\frac{2\pi}{3}\lt|\omega|\lt\frac{4\pi}{3}$, $X_d=0$ there.

> [!success]- Answer (b) $T = 1/100$ s, at
> - Height $100$; half-width $\pi$.
> - $X_d(\omega)=100\left(1-\frac{|\omega|}{\pi}\right)$ for $|\omega|\le\pi$.
> - Triangles **touch** at $\pm\pi,\pm3\pi,\dots$: a zigzag, no guard band, no overlap.

> [!success]- Answer (c) $T = 1/75$ s, below
> - Height $75$; half-width $\frac{4\pi}{3}\gt\pi$: copies overlap on $\frac{2\pi}{3}\le|\omega|\le\frac{4\pi}{3}$.
> - There the falling and rising edges add to a constant:
> $$
> 75\left(2-\frac{\omega+(2\pi-\omega)}{4\pi/3}\right)=75\left(2-\frac{2\pi}{4\pi/3}\right)=75\left(2-\frac32\right)=37.5 .
> $$
> - One period:
> $$
> X_d(\omega)=\begin{cases}75\left(1-\dfrac{3|\omega|}{4\pi}\right), & |\omega|\le\dfrac{2\pi}{3}\\[6pt] 37.5, & \dfrac{2\pi}{3}\le|\omega|\le\pi\end{cases}
> $$
> - A trapezoid, not a triangle: aliasing.

## 3. The same criterion on the digital-frequency axis

Lecture 19 §1 derives it again from the frequency map:
- $\omega=\Omega T$ maps analog to digital frequency.
- The DTFT is $2\pi$-periodic: everything $x[n]$ holds sits in $\omega\in[-\pi,\pi]$.
- So every frequency of $X_a(\Omega)$ must land inside $[-\pi,\pi]$. Checking the largest, $\Omega_{\max}=2\pi B$, is enough.

> [!key] Nyquist criterion via $\omega=\Omega T$ (Lecture 19, eqs. 1–5)
> $x_a(t)$ is **bandlimited** to $B$ hertz if
> $$
> X_a(\Omega)=0,\qquad|\Omega|\gt2\pi B .
> $$
> It can be recovered from $x[n]=x_a(nT)$ if
> $$
> 2\pi BT\lt\pi\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad T\lt\frac{1}{2B}\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad f_s\gt2B .
> $$
> The **Nyquist rate** is $2B$ (hertz). Otherwise we incur **aliasing**.

## 4. Sampling exactly at the Nyquist rate

- At the Nyquist rate (Lecture 19, Figure 1(c)) recovery is possible when $X_d(\pm\pi)=0$. That condition is sufficient; what fails is an impulse at $\pm\pi$, i.e. a sinusoid at exactly $B$ hertz.
- Sample $x_a(t)=\sin(\Omega_0t)$: $x[n]=\sin(\Omega_0nT)=\sin(\omega_0n)$, $\omega_0=\Omega_0T$ (Lecture 19, eqs. 6–8). $B=\frac{\Omega_0}{2\pi}$, so aliasing occurs when $T\gt\frac{1}{2B}=\frac{\pi}{\Omega_0}$, i.e. $\omega_0\gt\pi$.

> [!question] Lecture 19, slide 7 — sampling exactly at the Nyquist rate
> Sample $x_a(t)=\sin(\Omega_0t)$ with $T=\frac{1}{2B}=\frac{\pi}{\Omega_0}$. What is $x[n]$?

> [!success]- Answer (Lecture 19, eqs. 9–11)
> $$
> x[n]=\sin\!\left(\Omega_0n\frac{\pi}{\Omega_0}\right)=\sin(\pi n)=0\qquad\text{for all }n .
> $$
> Every sample hits a zero crossing; no way to tell what was sampled. A cosine gives $\cos(\pi n)=(-1)^n$, but $\cos(\Omega_0t+\phi)$ gives $\cos\phi\,(-1)^n$: amplitude and phase merge. Hence the strict $f_s\gt2B$.

> [!example] Two tones
> $x_a(t)=\cos(2\pi\cdot60\,t)+\cos(2\pi\cdot440\,t)$: $B=440$ Hz, Nyquist rate $880$ Hz.
> - $f_s=1$ kHz: tones at $0.12\pi$ and $0.88\pi$.
> - $f_s=800$ Hz: $440$ Hz lands at $1.1\pi\equiv-0.9\pi$, same as a $360$ Hz tone: aliased.
> - $f_s=880$ Hz: $\sin(2\pi\cdot440\,t)$ samples to $\sin(\pi n)=0$; the criterion is strict.

> [!trap]
> - **$B$ is in hertz:** $\Omega_{\max}=1000\pi$ rad/s means $B=500$ Hz, rate $1000$ Hz (not $2000\pi$), $T_N=1$ ms ([[exams/midterm-2/past-exams/fall-2024|FA2024 MT2]] #5(a)).
> - **Rate or period?** [[exams/midterm-2/past-exams/fall-2021|FA2021 MT2]] #5(a) asks for the largest $T$: $\frac{1}{8000}$ s for a $4$ kHz signal.
> - **Nyquist is strict:** at $f_s=2B$ the copies touch with no guard band, and a sine at $B$ Hz samples to zeros.
> - **Copies are not aliasing;** aliasing is their overlap. Overlapping copies can sum to a flat or zero $X_d$ ([[exams/midterm-2/past-exams/spring-2023|SP2023 MT2]] #7(c)).
> - **Time scaling scales the rate:** $x_c(3t)$ has three times the Nyquist rate ([[exams/midterm-2/past-exams/spring-2023|SP2023 MT2]] #1(b)). A non-bandlimited signal aliases at every $f_s$.
> - Some exams allow aliasing outside a filter's passband and ask for $T\gt T_N$ ([[exams/midterm-2/past-exams/fall-2024|FA2024 MT2]] #5(c); [[concepts/digital-processing-of-analog-signals|A/D → filter → D/A]]).
