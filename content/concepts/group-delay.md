*Lecture 16 (Wed Oct 7) §2.2.1 · [[homework/hw6|HW6]] #3(b), #6, HW7 #1 ([[exams/quiz-2|Quiz 2 page]]) · family: [[problems/magnitude-phase-and-group-delay|magnitude, phase and group delay]]*

> [!abstract] In one breath
> - $H_d(\omega)=e^{-jk\omega}$ sends $e^{j\omega_0n}$ to $e^{j\omega_0(n-k)}$: a linear phase is a pure delay of $k$ samples.
> - $\tau_{gd}=-\frac{d\angle H_d}{d\omega}$, read from the continuous phase, jumps and wraps ignored.
> - Uniform $\tau_{gd}$ keeps the waveform; non-uniform $\tau_{gd}$ moves frequencies by different amounts and smears it.

## 1. What the phase does

> [!question] Slides 10–11: $H_d(\omega)=e^{-j2\omega}$, $|H_d|=1$, $\angle H_d=-2\omega$
> Response to (a) $e^{j\frac{\pi}{2}n}$, (b) $e^{j\frac{3\pi}{4}n}$, (c) $e^{j\omega_0 n}$? (d) Response to $e^{j\omega_0n}$ for $H_d(\omega)=e^{-jk\omega}$, $k\in\mathbb{Z}$?

> [!success]- Answers
> (a) $H_d(\frac{\pi}{2})=e^{-j\pi}=-1$, so $y[n]=-e^{j\frac{\pi}{2}n}=e^{j\frac{\pi}{2}(n-2)}$.
> (b) $H_d(\frac{3\pi}{4})=e^{-j\frac{3\pi}{2}}=j$, so $y[n]=j\,e^{j\frac{3\pi}{4}n}=e^{j\frac{3\pi}{4}(n-2)}$. Principal angle $+\frac{\pi}{2}$, still a two-sample delay.
> (c) $y[n]=e^{-j2\omega_0}e^{j\omega_0n}=e^{j\omega_0(n-2)}=x[n-2]$: $h[n]=\delta[n-2]$.
> (d) $y[n]=e^{-jk\omega_0}e^{j\omega_0n}=e^{j\omega_0(n-k)}=x[n-k]$. **A linear phase $-k\omega$ is a pure delay of $k$ samples.**

Delay property (Lecture 16, eqs. 28–29): $x[n-k]\overset{\mathcal{F}}{\longleftrightarrow}X_d(\omega)e^{-j\omega k}$, a linear phase $f(\omega)=-k\omega$ whose slope is set by the shift.

## 2. Definition

> [!key] Group delay (Lecture 16, eq. 30, slide 12)
> $$
> \tau_{gd}(\omega)=-\frac{d\angle H_d(\omega)}{d\omega}\qquad\text{(in samples)}
> $$
> Differentiate the **continuous** phase: skip the $\pm\pi$ jumps and $2\pi$ wraps of the principal phase.
> - **Uniform** group delay: linear phase $\alpha-k\omega$ (jumps allowed), $\tau_{gd}(\omega)=k$. Every frequency delayed by $k$; waveform keeps its shape.
> - **Non-uniform** group delay: non-linear phase, $\tau_{gd}$ varies with $\omega$. Frequencies arrive at different times; waveform distorted.

| system | continuous phase | $\tau_{gd}(\omega)$ |
|---|---|---|
| $H_d=e^{-j2\omega}$ (slides 10–11) | $-2\omega$ | $2$ |
| $h=\{\underset{\uparrow}{1},0,1\}$, $H_d=2e^{-j\omega}\cos\omega$ (Example 1) | $-\omega$ ($\pi$ jumps at $\pm\frac{\pi}{2}$) | $1$ |
| $h=\{\underset{\uparrow}{1},0,-1\}$, $H_d=2je^{-j\omega}\sin\omega$ (Exercise 1 = Example 2) | $\frac{\pi}{2}-\omega$ ($\pi$ jump at $0$, not differentiable there) | $1$ |
| $y[n]=x[n]+2x[n-3]+x[n-6]$ ([[exams/midterm-2/past-exams/spring-2025\|SP2025 MT2]] #3) | $-3\omega$ | $3$ |
| $h=\{\underset{\uparrow}{3},2,1\}$ (Exercise 2 = Example 3) | $-\tan^{-1}\frac{2\sin\omega+\sin2\omega}{3+2\cos\omega+\cos2\omega}$, smooth but non-linear | $\frac{3c(1+c)}{3c^2+4c+2}$, $c=\cos\omega$: $\frac23$ at $0$, $0$ at $\pm\frac{\pi}{2}$ and $\pm\pi$, negative for $\frac{\pi}{2}\lt\lvert\omega\rvert\lt\pi$ |

The first four are uniform; Example 3 is non-uniform. Worked magnitudes and phases: [[concepts/magnitude-and-phase-response|magnitude and phase response]].

## 3. Why it matters

**Image denoising (notes Fig. 3, slides 13–15).** Two LTI systems with the same low-pass magnitude response filter a noisy image:
- Uniform group delay (linear phase): clean output, shifted slightly in $x$ and $y$.
- Non-uniform group delay (sinusoidal phase): same frequencies, same strengths, but "shaking" and ringing artifacts. Spatial frequencies (cycles per pixel) moved by different amounts; the image lost its coherence.

Magnitude decides which frequencies survive; phase decides where they land.

> [!key] Symmetric or antisymmetric FIR ⇒ generalized linear phase (Singer–Munson §6.4)
> Real $h[n]$, length $N$, centre $M=\frac{N-1}{2}$. Symmetric $h[n]=h[N-1-n]$: $H_d(\omega)=e^{-jM\omega}R(\omega)$; antisymmetric $h[n]=-h[N-1-n]$: $H_d(\omega)=je^{-jM\omega}R(\omega)$; $R$ real. So $\tau_{gd}=M$ everywhere. Causal rational IIR filters cannot have generalized linear phase.

## 4. Midterm 2 quick check

> [!question] Q1 · An antisymmetric FIR
> $y[n]=2x[n]-x[n-1]+x[n-3]-2x[n-4]$. (a) Group delay? (b) $H_d(\frac{\pi}{2})$ in polar form. (c) Output for $x[n]=\cos(\frac{\pi}{2}n)$.

> [!success]- Answer
> **(a)** $h=\{\underset{\uparrow}{2},-1,0,1,-2\}$, antisymmetric about $M=2$:
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> H_d(\omega)&=e^{-j2\omega}\left(2e^{j2\omega}-2e^{-j2\omega}-e^{j\omega}+e^{-j\omega}\right)\\
> &=2j\,e^{-j2\omega}\,(2\sin2\omega-\sin\omega),
> \end{aligned}
> $$
> so $\tau_{gd}=2$ for every $\omega$: the $j$ and the sign of the real factor add only constants and $\pi$ jumps.
>
> **(b)** $2\sin\pi-\sin\frac{\pi}{2}=-1$: $H_d(\frac{\pi}{2})=2j\,e^{-j\pi}(-1)=2j=2e^{j\pi/2}$. Phase $+\frac{\pi}{2}$, delay still $2$.
>
> **(c)** $y[n]=2\cos(\frac{\pi}{2}n+\frac{\pi}{2})=-2\sin(\frac{\pi}{2}n)$.

> [!question] Q2 · True or False
> (a) $y[n]=\frac12y[n-1]+x[n]$ delays every frequency by one sample. (b) Cascading linear-phase FIR filters with group delays $2$ and $3$ gives group delay $5$.

> [!success]- Answer
> (a) **False.** $H_d=\frac{1}{1-\frac12e^{-j\omega}}$ has non-linear phase: $\tau_{gd}(\omega)=\dfrac{\frac12\cos\omega-\frac14}{\frac54-\cos\omega}$, $1$ at $\omega=0$ but $-\frac13$ at $\pm\pi$.
> (b) **True.** Phases add, $-2\omega-3\omega$ plus constants and jumps: $\tau_{gd}=5$. E.g. $\{1,0,0,0,1\}*\{1,2,3,4,3,2,1\}$ is symmetric about $n=5$.

> [!trap]
> - Jumps ($\pi$ or $2\pi$) do not make a phase non-linear; read the slope between breaks.
> - The slope is the delay, not the phase value: $e^{-j2\omega}$ has principal angle $+\frac{\pi}{2}$ at $\frac{3\pi}{4}$, delay $2$.
> - $e^{-j\omega/2}$ is not $\delta[n-\frac12]$: inverse DTFT $\frac{\sin(\pi(n-\frac12))}{\pi(n-\frac12)}$ ([[exams/midterm-2/past-exams/fall-2023|FA2023 MT2]] T/F (b)); its group delay is still $\frac12$.
> - Zero phase is linear phase with slope $0$: $1+2\cos2\omega$ ([[exams/midterm-2/past-exams/fall-2024|FA2024 MT2]] #3) has $\tau_{gd}=0$, non-causal.
