> [!abstract] In one breath
> - A butterfly is a picture of **one pair of merging equations**: inputs $A[k]$ and $B[k]$, outputs $X[k]$ and $X[k+\frac N2]$.
> - It has four branches. From the upper input: weight 1 to both outputs. From the lower input: $W_N^k$ to the upper output, $-W_N^k$ to the lower output.
> - To read any weight in a big diagram: find the **length** the stage merges to (twice the row distance of its butterflies), the **index $k$** inside its group, and **which of the four branches** it is.

> [!key] The four branch weights (Lecture 25, eqs. 16–17, Figure 1)
> Merging two length-$\frac N2$ DFTs $A$, $B$ into the length-$N$ DFT $X$, for one fixed $k$:
> $$
> X[k]=A[k]+W_N^{k}B[k],\qquad X\!\left[k+\tfrac N2\right]=A[k]-W_N^{k}B[k],\qquad W_N^k=e^{-j\frac{2\pi k}{N}}.
> $$
>
> | branch | weight |
> |---|---|
> | upper input → upper output | $1$ |
> | upper input → lower output | $1$ |
> | lower input → upper output | $W_N^{k}$ |
> | lower input → lower output | $-W_N^{k}$ |
>
> - The two outputs of a butterfly are $\frac N2$ rows apart in the merged DFT.
> - Subscript = the length merged **to**; superscript = the index $k$ of the two shorter DFTs being combined.
> - Two ways of drawing the same thing: the lecture puts $W_N^k$ on the $B[k]$ rail before the cross and $-1$ on the last lower branch; homework and exam keys put the combined weight on each branch. Multiply along a path and the four weights above come out either way.

> [!lecture] In lecture — L25, Nov 6
> "Each merging equation is represented by one butterfly, and that's all this diagram is showing … nothing more, nothing less. This is not redefining what the FFT is." His advice is to stop thinking of an arbitrary $k$: "imagine that you're looking at a specific value of $k$", written $k_0$. And the two rails are not always neighbours: "if I'm merging to a length of 16, 16 divided by 2 is going to indicate I have a separation of 8 samples in the merged sequence."

How to read it:

- **Columns are stages.** Stage 1 merges the eight length-1 DFTs (the samples themselves) into four length-2 DFTs $C,D,E,F$; stage 2 merges those into $A$ and $B$; stage 3 merges $A$ and $B$ into $X$. These are the three merging steps of [[concepts/fast-fourier-transform|Example 2]].
- **Rows come in groups.** After stage 2 the top four rows hold $A[0..3]$ and the bottom four hold $B[0..3]$. A butterfly always joins index $k$ of the upper group with the same index $k$ of the lower group.
- **The twiddle factor** at a lower input shows the stage (subscript 2, 4 or 8) and the index (superscript). Follow $A[3]$ and $B[3]$ in stage 3: weight $W_8^3$ into $X[3]$ and $-W_8^3$ into $X[7]$.
- **Input order.** The splitting tree leaves the samples in the order $x_0,x_4,x_2,x_6,x_1,x_5,x_3,x_7$. This is the index written in binary and read backwards ($1=001\to100=4$, $3=011\to110=6$), called bit-reversed order.
- **Cost.** Each butterfly is two multiply-adds, each stage has $\frac N2=4$ butterflies, and there are $\log_28=3$ stages: 24 multiply-adds.

## 3. Reading a branch weight

> [!recipe] Three questions for any branch (Lecture 25, Example 3; makeup recording)
> 1. **Which length does this stage merge to?** Pick any butterfly of the stage and count the rows between its upper and lower rail. That distance is half the merged length: 8 rows apart → length 16; 4 apart → length 8; 2 apart → length 4.
> 2. **Which index $k$?** In the column the stage ends in, mark off groups of that length from the top and count $k=0,1,2,\dots$ inside each group. In the column it starts from, the groups are half as long. A butterfly joins the same $k$ of two neighbouring half-length groups, so read $k$ where the branch starts.
> 3. **Which of the four branches?** Upper → upper: $1$. Upper → lower: $1$. Lower → upper: $W_L^k$. Lower → lower: $-W_L^k$.
>
> Work from the last column backwards: the last stage merges to the full length $N$, the one before to $\frac N2$, and so on.

> [!question] Example 3 (slide 14) — a partial length-16 diagram
> The figure shows part of a length-16 decimation-in-time FFT. Determine the weights $a$, $b$, $c$, $d$, $e$.

> [!success]- Answer
> **Lengths.** The right-hand butterflies join rows 4 and 12, and rows 7 and 15: 8 apart, so that stage merges to length 16. The left-hand butterflies join rows 2 and 6, and rows 11 and 15: 4 apart, so that stage merges to length 8. Middle column: two length-8 groups (rows 0–7 and 8–15). Left column: four length-4 groups.
>
> | weight | branch | stage | $k$ | type | answer |
> |---|---|---|---|---|---|
> | $a$ | left row 15 → middle row 15 | to length 8 | 3 (rows 11 and 15 are both $k=3$ of their length-4 groups) | lower → lower | $-W_8^{3}$ |
> | $b$ | middle row 15 → right row 15 | to length 16 | 7 (rows 7 and 15 are $k=7$ of the two length-8 groups) | lower → lower | $-W_{16}^{7}$ |
> | $c$ | left row 6 → middle row 2 | to length 8 | 2 | lower → upper | $W_8^{2}$ |
> | $d$ | middle row 4 → right row 4 | to length 16 | 4 | upper → upper | $1$ |
> | $e$ | middle row 12 → right row 4 | to length 16 | 4 | lower → upper | $W_{16}^{4}$ |
>
> Simplified, if asked: $c=e=-j$ (they are the same number, $e^{-j\pi/2}$), $a=-e^{-j3\pi/4}=e^{j\pi/4}$, $b=-e^{-j7\pi/8}=e^{j\pi/8}$.

> [!lecture] Makeup recording — posted Nov 6
> "It's most helpful to figure out first, what are the lengths of each stage." Once the groups are marked, "we've actually done the hardest work … the remaining steps are extremely formulaic": which of the four directions, what length are we merging to, and what index $k$. On simplifying: "these two can certainly be simplified further, but this is an equivalent answer, and unless we really ask you to reduce it fully, there's really no need to." He adds that the example should help "not just for remaining homeworks, but also looking ahead to the final exam."

> [!trap]
> - **$k$ restarts in every group.** Row 14 of a 16-row diagram is $k=6$ in the last stage (pair 6 and 14) but $k=2$ in the stage before it (pair 10 and 14, inside the group of rows 8–15).
> - **The subscript belongs to the stage**, not to the whole FFT: only the last stage of a 16-point FFT uses $W_{16}$.
> - **A horizontal branch is not always 1.** On an upper rail it is 1; on a lower rail it is $-W_L^k$.
> - **The sign goes with the destination:** from the lower input, $+W$ to the upper output, $-W$ to the lower output. From the upper input both weights are $+1$.
> - $W_8^2$, $W_{16}^4$ and $-j$ are the same number. Unsimplified answers are accepted when they are equivalent.
