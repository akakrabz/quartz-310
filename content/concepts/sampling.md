> [!abstract] In one breath
> - Sampling = multiplying by an impulse train: the spectrum is copied every $2\pi/T$ rad/s, each copy scaled by $\frac1T$; overlapping copies add.
> - With $\omega=\Omega T$ the copies sit every $2\pi$: that is the DTFT $X_d(\omega)$.
> - Sketch $X_d$: scale the axis by $T$, copy every $2\pi$, heights $\times\frac1T$, add overlaps, read off $[-\pi,\pi]$.

## 1. Ideal sampling and the impulse-train model

> [!key] Ideal sampling (Lecture 17, eqs. 1–2)
> $$
> x[n]=x_a(nT),\qquad-\infty\lt n\lt\infty,\qquad f_s=\frac1T .
> $$
> Instantaneous measurement of $x_a(t)$ every $T$ seconds ($T$: sampling period, $f_s$: sampling frequency). Notes write $x(t)$; exams also $x_c(t)$, $X_c(\Omega)$.

Key question (slide 3): which $T$ guarantee that $x_a(t)$ can be recovered from $x[n]$? Answer: the [[concepts/nyquist-criterion|Nyquist criterion]].

Sifting property (eq. 3): $x_a(t)\,\delta(t-t_0)=x_a(t_0)\,\delta(t-t_0)$. One impulse measures one value; an impulse train measures all of them.

> [!key] Impulse-train model (Lecture 17, eqs. 4–5)
> $$
> x_s(t)=x_a(t)\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\delta(t-nT)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}x_a(nT)\,\delta(t-nT)
> $$
> Notes: $x_{\text{sampled}}(t)$. **Still continuous-time**: an impulse of area $x_a(nT)$ at each $t=nT$, zero between. One value per interval of width $T$ (from $\tfrac{3T}{2}$ to $\tfrac{5T}{2}$: only $t=2T$). Storing the areas as a sequence gives $x[n]$ (notes Figure 1).

## 2. The sampled spectrum

Subscript $a$: analog spectra; $d$: digital. $\Omega$ in rad/s, $\omega$ in rad/sample. Two transform facts (eqs. 6, 9):
- Windowing: $x_a(t)\,w(t)\leftrightarrow\frac{1}{2\pi}X_a(\Omega)*W_a(\Omega)$.
- Impulse train: $\sum_n\delta(t-nT)\leftrightarrow\frac{2\pi}{T}\sum_n\delta\!\left(\Omega-\frac{2\pi}{T}n\right)$.

> [!key] Sampled spectrum (Lecture 17, eqs. 10–11)
> $$
> X_s(\Omega)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\left[X_a(\Omega)*\frac{2\pi}{T}\sum_n\delta\!\left(\Omega-\frac{2\pi}{T}n\right)\right]=\frac1T\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}X_a\!\left(\Omega-\frac{2\pi}{T}\,n\right)
> $$
> Copies of $X_a$ every $\dfrac{2\pi}{T}$ rad/s ($f_s$ Hz), each scaled by $\dfrac1T$. Overlapping copies add. Last step: convolving with $\delta(\Omega-\Omega_0)$ shifts to $\Omega_0$.

> [!question] Example 1 (slide 6) — varying $T$
> Let $X_a(\Omega)=\operatorname{rect}\!\left(\dfrac{\Omega}{20\pi}\right)$ (height 1 on $|\Omega|\le10\pi$, bandlimit $5$ Hz). Sketch $X_s(\Omega)$ (a) for $T=\frac{1}{20}$ s on $-100\pi\lt\Omega\lt100\pi$; (b) for $T=\frac{1}{10}$ s on $-50\pi\lt\Omega\lt50\pi$.

> [!success]- Answer (a)
> - Spacing $\frac{2\pi}{T}=40\pi$, height $\frac1T=20$.
> - Boxes of height $20$, width $20\pi$, centred at $-80\pi,-40\pi,0,40\pi,80\pi$.
> - **Gap of $20\pi$** between neighbours (e.g. $10\pi$ to $30\pi$). No overlap.

> [!success]- Answer (b)
> - Spacing $\frac{2\pi}{T}=20\pi$, height $10$; centres $0,\pm20\pi,\pm40\pi$.
> - Each box is exactly as wide as the spacing: copies **touch**, no overlap.
> - Sum: $X_s(\Omega)=10$ for all $\Omega$. Box shape gone, yet nothing overlaps: the at-Nyquist case ($f_s=10$ Hz $=2\cdot5$ Hz).

## 3. From analog to digital frequency: the DTFT of the samples

Sample a sinusoid (eqs. 12–13): $x_a(t)=\sin(\Omega t)$ gives $x[n]=\sin(\Omega nT)=\sin(\omega n)$ (slide 7 uses $\cos(\Omega_0t)$).

> [!key] Analog and digital frequency (Lecture 17, eq. 14)
> $$
> \omega=\Omega\,T\qquad\left(\frac{\text{rad}}{\text{sample}}=\frac{\text{rad}}{\text{s}}\cdot\frac{\text{s}}{\text{sample}}\right),\qquad\Omega=\frac{\omega}{T}.
> $$
> A tone at $f$ Hz lands at $\omega=2\pi f/f_s$; half the sampling rate, $f=f_s/2$, lands at $\omega=\pi$.

> [!key] DTFT after ideal A/D (Lecture 17, eqs. 15–16)
> $$
> X_d(\omega)=X_s(\Omega)\Big|_{\Omega=\omega/T}=\frac1T\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}X_a\!\left(\frac{\omega-2\pi n}{T}\right).
> $$
> Copies every $2\pi$; axis scaled by $T$ (edge $2\pi B\to2\pi BT$); height $\frac1T$. Without overlap, $X_d(\omega)=\frac1T\,X_a\!\left(\frac{\omega}{T}\right)$ for $|\omega|\le\pi$.

> [!recipe] From $X_a(\Omega)$ to $X_d(\omega)$
> 1. Scale the axis: $\omega=\Omega T$ (band edge $2\pi B\mapsto2\pi BT$; $\omega=\pi$ is $f_s/2$).
> 2. Copy every $2\pi$.
> 3. Scale heights by $1/T$.
> 4. Add overlaps.
> 5. Read off $[-\pi,\pi]$.

> [!example] 2 kHz triangle $X_a(\Omega)=1-\frac{|\Omega|}{4000\pi}$, $|\Omega|\le4000\pi$
> - $f_s=10$ kHz: triangle of height $10\,000$ on $|\omega|\le0.4\pi$, guard band beyond.
> - $f_s=4$ kHz: $X_d(\omega)=4000\left(1-\frac{|\omega|}{\pi}\right)$; copies touch at $\pm\pi$.
> - $f_s=3$ kHz: trapezoid, $3000\left(1-\frac{3|\omega|}{4\pi}\right)$ out to $\frac{2\pi}{3}$, then $1500$ to $\pi$: [[concepts/aliasing|aliasing]].

Lecture 17's triangle at $150/100/75$ Hz is worked on [[concepts/nyquist-criterion|Nyquist criterion]] §2.

- A sampled cosine keeps impulse area $\pi$: $\cos(\Omega_0t)\to\cos(\omega_0n)\leftrightarrow\pi\delta(\omega\mp\omega_0)$, repeated every $2\pi$; the $\frac1T$ cancels against $\delta\!\left(\frac{\omega}{T}\right)=T\delta(\omega)$ ([[homework/hw8|HW8]] #3: $\cos(400\pi t)$ at $T=1$, $2$ ms lands at $\pm0.4\pi$, $\pm0.8\pi$).

## 4. Midterm 2 quick check

> [!question] Q1 · overlap and heights
> $X_a(\Omega)=2$ for $\lvert\Omega\rvert\le600\pi$ rad/s, $0$ otherwise; $f_s=500$ Hz. (a) Nyquist rate? (b) $X_d(\omega)$ on $[-\pi,\pi]$. (c) $Y_a(\Omega)$ from an ideal D/A with the same $T$.

> [!success]- Answer
> - (a) $B=300$ Hz, rate $600$ Hz: $500$ Hz is below it.
> - (b) Edge $600\pi\cdot\frac{1}{500}=1.2\pi$, height $2\cdot500=1000$. The copy at $2\pi$ starts at $0.8\pi$ (the one at $-2\pi$ ends at $-0.8\pi$): $X_d(\omega)=1000$ for $\lvert\omega\rvert\lt0.8\pi$, $2000$ for $0.8\pi\lt\lvert\omega\rvert\le\pi$.
> - (c) $T\,X_d(\Omega T)$ on $\lvert\Omega\rvert\lt\frac{\pi}{T}=500\pi$: $2$ for $\lvert\Omega\rvert\lt400\pi$, $4$ for $400\pi\lt\lvert\Omega\rvert\lt500\pi$, $0$ beyond. Not $X_a$: [[concepts/aliasing|aliasing]].

> [!question] Q2 · True or false (bandlimited $x_a$, above Nyquist)
> (a) Halving $T$ doubles the height of $X_d(\omega)$ and halves the width of its central copy. (b) $X_s(\Omega)$ is $2\pi$-periodic. (c) $x_s(t)$ is a discrete-time signal.

> [!success]- Answer
> - (a) **True:** height $\frac1T$, edge $2\pi BT$.
> - (b) **False:** period $\frac{2\pi}{T}$ rad/s; $X_d(\omega)$ is the $2\pi$-periodic one.
> - (c) **False:** an impulse train in continuous time; $x[n]$ holds its areas.

> [!trap]
> - **Forgetting $1/T$:** heights scale by $f_s$; a height-1 triangle sampled at $1$ kHz has height $1000$ ([[exams/midterm-2/past-exams/fall-2024|FA2024 MT2]] #5(b), [[exams/midterm-2/past-exams/spring-2025|SP2025 MT2]] #5(a)).
> - **Two spacings:** $2\pi/T$ in $\Omega$, $2\pi$ in $\omega$. Do not mix the axes.
> - **Copies add:** draw their sum, not the separate copies; it can be flat ([[exams/midterm-2/past-exams/spring-2021|SP2021 MT2]] #8(b)) or zero ([[exams/midterm-2/past-exams/spring-2023|SP2023 MT2]] #7(c)).
> - **Larger $T$ stretches $X_d$ outward** and lowers it ([[exams/midterm-2/past-exams/fall-2021|FA2021 MT2]] #1(e)).
> - **Frequency known only modulo $2\pi$:** $\cos(\Omega_0t)$ and $\cos\big((\Omega_0+2\pi f_sk)t\big)$ give the same samples ([[exams/midterm-2/past-exams/fall-2023|FA2023 MT2]] #3).
> - $x_s(t)$ is continuous-time; $x[n]$ is the sequence of impulse areas.
