> [!abstract] In one breath
> - A computer cannot evaluate the DTFT: the sum is infinite and $\omega$ is continuous.
> - The DFT keeps $N$ samples and $N$ frequencies: $X[k]=X_d\!\left(\frac{2\pi k}{N}\right)$, $0\le k\le N-1$.
> - $X[k]$ and the inverse DFT are $N$-periodic; in matrix form $\mathbf X=\mathbf W\mathbf x$ with $\mathbf W^{-1}=\frac1N\mathbf W^*$.

## 1. Computing the DTFT

Writing a function that returns (notes eq. 1, slides 3–5)
$$
X_d(\omega)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}x[n]e^{-j\omega n}
$$
runs into two problems.

**(1) The sum is infinite.** Keep $0\le n\le N-1$ (eqs. 2–4):
$$
\begin{gathered}
\hat X_d(\omega)=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}x[n]e^{-j\omega n}=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}x[n]w[n]e^{-j\omega n},\\
w[n]=\begin{cases}1, & 0\le n\le N-1\\ 0, & \text{else}\end{cases}
\end{gathered}
$$
- This **windows** $x[n]$. By the windowing property (eq. 5), $x[n]w[n]\overset{\mathcal F}{\longleftrightarrow}\frac{1}{2\pi}X_d(\omega)*W_d(\omega)$ (periodic convolution), so $\hat X_d(\omega)\ne X_d(\omega)$: [[concepts/window-functions|window functions]].

**(2) $\omega$ is continuous.** Even a length-$N$ signal has $X_d(\omega)$ at infinitely many $\omega$. $X_d$ is $2\pi$-periodic, so sample one period at $N$ regularly spaced frequencies (eq. 6):
$$
\omega_k=\frac{2\pi k}{N},\qquad0\le k\le N-1 .
$$
Both fixes together (eq. 7): $\hat X_k=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}x[n]e^{-j\frac{2\pi k}{N}n}$.
- Almost the [[concepts/fourier-series|DTFS]] analysis equation, with $N$ for $N_0$: $x[n]$ is not assumed periodic, only of length $N$ (by windowing or by nature).
- More than $N$ frequencies are possible: [[concepts/zero-padding|zero padding]].

## 2. Definition

> [!key] DFT (Lecture 20, eqs. 8–9; slide 6)
> For a length-$N$ signal $x[n]$, $x[n]\overset{\text{DFT}}{\longleftrightarrow}X[k]$:
> $$
> X[k]=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}x[n]e^{-j\frac{2\pi k}{N}n},\quad0\le k\le N-1\quad\text{(analysis, forward DFT)}
> $$
> $$
> x[n]=\frac1N\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}X[k]e^{j\frac{2\pi k}{N}n},\quad0\le n\le N-1\quad\text{(synthesis, inverse DFT)}
> $$
> $X[k]$ is defined for $0\le k\le N-1$ only.

### 2.1 The DFT samples the DTFT

For a length-$N$ $x[n]$, $X_d(\omega)=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}x[n]e^{-j\omega n}$ (eq. 10). The DFT is the same sum at $\omega=\omega_k$ (eq. 11).

> [!key] DFT = DTFT samples (Lecture 20, eq. 12)
> $$
> X[k]=X_d(\omega)\Big|_{\omega=\omega_k}=X_d\!\left(\frac{2\pi k}{N}\right),\qquad0\le k\le N-1 .
> $$
> $N$ regularly spaced samples from $\omega=0$ up to, not including, $2\pi$; spacing $\frac{2\pi}{N}$.

|---|---|---|

| on a computer | no | yes |

## 3. N-periodicity

> [!key] $N$-periodicity (Lecture 20, eqs. 13–19; slides 9–10)
> $$
> X[k]=X[k+mN],\qquad m\in\mathbb Z .
> $$
> The inverse DFT is $N$-periodic too: $\bar x[n]=\mathrm{IDFT}\{X[k]\}$ satisfies $\bar x[n+mN]=\bar x[n]$. The DFT implies the **periodic extension** of the length-$N$ signal ([[concepts/circular-shift-and-reversal|circular shift and reversal]]).

> [!derivation]- Proofs (slides 9–10)
> $e^{-j2\pi mn}=e^{j2\pi km}=1$ for integers $k$, $m$, $n$:
> $$
> X[k+mN]=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}x[n]e^{-j\frac{2\pi k}{N}n}e^{-j2\pi mn}=X[k],
> $$
> $$
> \bar x[n+mN]=\frac1N\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}X[k]e^{j\frac{2\pi k}{N}n}e^{j2\pi km}=\bar x[n].
> $$
> Slide 10's "$x[n]=x[k+mN]$" means $x[n]=x[n+mN]$.

## 4. Linear algebra formulation (optional)

- Each $X[k]$ is a dot product (eqs. 32–34): $X[k]=\mathbf v^*\mathbf u$ with $\mathbf u=x[n]$, $\mathbf v=e^{j\frac{2\pi k}{N}n}$, $n=0,\dots,N-1$; $\mathbf v^*$ is the conjugate transpose.
- Example $N=5$, $k=3$ (eq. 35):
$$
X[3]=\begin{bmatrix}1 & e^{-j\frac{2\pi\cdot3}{5}\cdot1} & e^{-j\frac{2\pi\cdot3}{5}\cdot2} & e^{-j\frac{2\pi\cdot3}{5}\cdot3} & e^{-j\frac{2\pi\cdot3}{5}\cdot4}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}x[0]\\ x[1]\\ x[2]\\ x[3]\\ x[4]\end{bmatrix}
$$
- Twiddle factor $W_N=e^{-j\frac{2\pi}{N}}$, so $W_N^{kn}=e^{-j\frac{2\pi k}{N}n}$ (eqs. 37–38; eq. 36's $W=e^{-j2\pi}$ is missing the $N$).

> [!key] DFT matrix (Lecture 20, eqs. 39–45)
> $$
> \begin{gathered}
> \mathbf X=\mathbf W\mathbf x,\qquad\mathbf W=\begin{bmatrix}1 & 1 & 1 & \cdots & 1\\ 1 & W_N & W_N^2 & \cdots & W_N^{N-1}\\ 1 & W_N^2 & W_N^4 & \cdots & W_N^{2(N-1)}\\ \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots\\ 1 & W_N^{N-1} & W_N^{2(N-1)} & \cdots & W_N^{(N-1)(N-1)}\end{bmatrix},\\
> \mathbf W_{kn}=W_N^{kn}.
> \end{gathered}
> $$
> Row $k$ is the complex exponential $e^{-j\omega_kn}$, $\omega_k=\frac{2\pi k}{N}$. Inverse: $\mathbf x=\mathbf W^{-1}\mathbf X$, $\mathbf W^{-1}=\frac1N\mathbf W^*$ (conjugate transpose).

- Why it matters: each $X[k]$ is visibly the dot product of $x[n]$ with one exponential; $\mathbf W$ and $\mathbf W^{-1}$ can be precomputed, and optimized matrix–vector products beat a double for-loop.

> [!question] Lecture exercise
> Discuss how the columns of $\mathbf W$ form an orthogonal set of basis vectors for $\mathbb C^N$.

> [!success]- Answer
> Inner product of columns $n$ and $n'$: $\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\left(W_N^{kn'}\right)^*W_N^{kn}=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}e^{-j\frac{2\pi}{N}k(n-n')}$.
> - $n=n'$: $N$.
> - $n\ne n'$: geometric series with ratio $r=e^{-j\frac{2\pi}{N}(n-n')}\ne1$ and $r^N=1$, so $\frac{1-r^N}{1-r}=0$.
>
> So $\mathbf W^*\mathbf W=N\mathbf I$: $N$ orthogonal nonzero vectors in $\mathbb C^N$, a basis. The same identity gives $\mathbf W^{-1}=\frac1N\mathbf W^*$.

> [!trap] Where points go
> - **Bins past $\frac N2$ are negative frequencies:** $X[k]=X_d\!\left(\frac{2\pi k}{N}-2\pi\right)$.
> - **The inverse DFT is $N$-periodic,** not zero outside $0\le n\le N-1$: the inverse DFT of $\{1,2,3,4\}$ has $x[4]=x[0]=2.5$.
> - **Zero padding never changes $X_d(\omega)$;** it only refines the DFT grid ([[concepts/zero-padding|zero padding]]).
> - $X[0]=\sum_nx[n]$ and, for real $x$, $X[\frac N2]=\sum_n(-1)^nx[n]$: both real.
> - A length-$N$ DFT of a cosine is two clean bins only when $\frac{\omega_0N}{2\pi}$ is an integer ([[concepts/spectral-leakage|leakage]]).
