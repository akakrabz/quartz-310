> [!abstract] In one breath
> - The DFT of $A\cos(\omega_0n)$ on $N$ samples is the two [[concepts/periodic-sinc|periodic-sinc]] lobes sampled every $\frac{2\pi}{N}$.
> - $\omega_0=\frac{2\pi k_0}{N}$ with $k_0$ an integer: the samples hit the peaks and the nulls; only $X[k_0]=X[N-k_0]=\frac{AN}{2}$ survive.
> - Otherwise the samples fall between peaks and nulls: leakage, (almost) every bin nonzero, the tallest bin below $\frac{AN}{2}$.

## 1. DFT of a finite-length sinusoid

> [!key] Lecture 22, eqs. 11–12
> $$
> X[k]=X_d(\omega)\Big|_{\omega=\frac{2\pi k}{N}}=\frac A2\,C\!\left(\frac{2\pi k}{N}-\omega_0\right)+\frac A2\,C\!\left(\frac{2\pi k}{N}+\omega_0\right),
> $$
> $0\le k\le N-1$, with $C$ the periodic sinc of length $N$ (main lobe height $N$, nulls every $\frac{2\pi}{N}$). The length $N$ decides what the DFT looks like.

> [!recipe] Two bins, or leakage?
> $k_0=\frac{\omega_0N}{2\pi}=\frac{N}{\text{period}}$, the number of periods in the window.
> - **Integer:** $X[k_0]=X[N-k_0]=\frac{AN}{2}$ for a cosine, $X[k_0]=-j\frac{AN}{2}$, $X[N-k_0]=j\frac{AN}{2}$ for a sine; every other bin $0$. ($k_0=0$ or $\frac N2$: one bin, $AN$.)
> - **Not an integer:** in general every bin is nonzero, the largest at the bins nearest $\pm\omega_0$.
> - A sum of cosines: one test per term; "exactly $2M$ nonzero bins" needs every frequency on a bin.

- $X_1[k]$: nonzero only at $k=4$ ($\frac{2\pi\cdot4}{24}=\frac\pi3$) and $k=20$ ($\frac{5\pi}{3}\equiv-\frac\pi3$), both $12=\frac N2$. The DFT catches both main-lobe peaks; every other bin sits on a null.
- $X_2[k]$: $k_0=\frac{22}{6}=\frac{11}{3}$, not an integer. Largest at $k=4$ ($\omega_4=\frac{4\pi}{11}\approx0.364\pi$, $\lvert X_2[4]\rvert\approx9.6$) and $k=18$, but 21 of the 22 bins are nonzero: **spectral leakage**, the energy of each peak spreads to adjacent frequencies.

Two readings of the same fact (notes p. 3):

- **Periodic extension.** The DFT implies $N$-periodic extension in time ([[concepts/circular-shift-and-reversal|periodic extension]]). Extending $x_1$ gives a smooth, endless $\cos(\frac\pi3n)$: only $\pm\frac\pi3$ present. Extending $x_2$ leaves a discontinuity at every copy boundary (the fractional period); discontinuities need new frequencies, and the DFT shows them.
- **Sampling the lobes.** With $\omega_0=\frac{2\pi k_0}{N}$ the DFT evaluates $C$ at its peak and at integer multiples of $\frac{2\pi}{N}$ from it, its nulls. Otherwise it samples the main and side lobes away from both.

Can we choose $N$ to avoid leakage? Not in general: real signals carry many components at unknown frequencies ([[concepts/spectral-analysis|spectral analysis]]); a tapered [[concepts/window-functions|window]] reduces it instead.

> [!question] Slide Example 2 — exactly two/four nonzero bins, or more?
> (a) $x_1[n]=\sin\!\left(\frac{\pi}{11}n\right)$, $0\le n\le32$. (b) $x_2[n]=\sin\!\left(\frac{3\pi}{5}n\right)$, $0\le n\le19$. (c) $x_3[n]=\cos\!\left(\frac\pi4n\right)+\cos\!\left(\frac\pi3n\right)$, $0\le n\le47$.

> [!success]- Answer
> - (a) $N=33$: $k_0=\frac{(\pi/11)\cdot33}{2\pi}=1.5$, not an integer: **more than two** (all 33 bins nonzero).
> - (b) $N=20$: $k_0=\frac{(3\pi/5)\cdot20}{2\pi}=6$: **exactly two**, $X[6]=-j10$, $X[14]=j10$.
> - (c) $N=48$: $\frac\pi4\to k_0=6$, $\frac\pi3\to k_0=8$, both integers: **exactly four**, $k=6,8,40,42$, each $24$.

## 3. Midterm 2 quick check

> [!question] Q1 · on-bin or off-bin?
> $x[n]=4\cos\!\left(\frac{\pi}{4}n\right)+2\sin\!\left(\frac{2\pi}{3}n\right)$. (a) For $0\le n\le23$, list every nonzero $X[k]$. (b) For $0\le n\le11$, are there exactly four nonzero bins?

> [!success]- Answer
> - (a) $N=24$: $k_0=\frac{24}{8}=3$ and $\frac{24}{3}=8$, both integers. Cosine: $X[3]=X[21]=\frac{4\cdot24}{2}=48$. Sine: $X[8]=-j\frac{2\cdot24}{2}=-24j$, $X[16]=24j$. All other bins $0$.
> - (b) **No.** $N=12$: the sine sits on bin 4, but the cosine has $k_0=\frac{12}{8}=1.5$ and leaks: all 12 bins are nonzero.

> [!question] Q2 · True or false
> (a) $\cos(0.3\pi n)$, $0\le n\le39$, has exactly two nonzero DFT bins. (b) $\sin\!\left(\frac{\pi}{5}n\right)$, $0\le n\le14$, has exactly two. (c) Zero-padding a leaking cosine to $4N$ leaves two nonzero bins.

> [!success]- Answer
> - (a) **True:** $k_0=\frac{0.3\pi\cdot40}{2\pi}=6$; $X[6]=X[34]=20$.
> - (b) **False:** $k_0=\frac{(\pi/5)\cdot15}{2\pi}=1.5$: all 15 bins nonzero.
> - (c) **False:** padding samples the same leaky DTFT more densely ($\cos(\frac\pi3n)$, $N=22$, padded to 88: 87 nonzero bins).

> [!trap]
> - "Two bins" needs $\frac{\omega_0N}{2\pi}$ to be an integer, not just a "nice" $\omega_0$: $\cos(\frac\pi8n)$ on $24$ samples has $k_0=1.5$ and leaks ([[exams/midterm-2/past-exams/fall-2023|FA2023 #1(g)]]).
> - A leaking cosine's tallest bin is below $\frac{AN}{2}$ and its neighbours are not zero: read $A$ from an on-bin plot only.
> - Leakage comes from truncation, not from aliasing and not from [[concepts/zero-padding|zero padding]]; padding only shows the leaked lobes more finely.
> - Bins $k\gt\frac N2$ are negative frequencies: the mirror peak at $N-k_0$ is the same component, not a second one.
