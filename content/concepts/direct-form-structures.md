> [!abstract] In one breath
> - A flow graph shows the order of computation: a labelled arrowhead multiplies by that gain, $z^{-1}$ delays by one sample, branches that meet at a dot are added.
> - **Direct form I:** the input coefficients on a delay line of $x$, followed by the feedback coefficients on a delay line of $y$.
> - **Direct form II:** the two halves in the other order, sharing **one** delay line that holds an intermediate signal $v[n]$. Same $H(z)$, about half the delays.

## 1. The system and the notation (§3)

> [!key] LCCDE and its transfer function (Lecture 27, eqs. 6–8)
> Any practical digital filter, FIR or IIR, is a causal difference equation ([[concepts/lccde|LCCDE]], Lecture 5 signs):
> $$
> y[n]=\sum_{i=1}^{K}b_i\,y[n-i]+\sum_{j=0}^{M-1}c_j\,x[n-j]\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad H(z)=\frac{\sum_{j=0}^{M-1}c_jz^{-j}}{1-\sum_{i=1}^{K}b_iz^{-i}}.
> $$
> - $c_j$: input (feed-forward) coefficients, the numerator. $b_i$: feedback coefficients; they enter the denominator with a **minus** sign because the $y$ terms move to the left side.
> - Building blocks (slide 9): a triangle or arrowhead with a label $c$ sends $x[n]$ to $c\,x[n]$; one labelled $z^{-1}$ sends $x[n]$ to $x[n-1]$; two branches meeting at a dot give the sum of their signals. An unlabelled branch has gain 1. Lecture 5 drew the same things as boxes and circles ([[concepts/block-diagram|block diagram]]); the homework uses that style.
>
> The lecture's running example has $K=3$, $M=4$ (eq. 7):
> $$
> y[n]=b_1y[n-1]+b_2y[n-2]+b_3y[n-3]+c_0x[n]+c_1x[n-1]+c_2x[n-2]+c_3x[n-3].
> $$

> [!key] Direct form I (Lecture 27 §3.1, slide 10)
> Read the diagram in two halves, through the signal $v[n]$ in the middle of the top rail:
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> v[n]&=c_0x[n]+c_1x[n-1]+c_2x[n-2]+c_3x[n-3]&&\Rightarrow\ V(z)=X(z)\left(c_0+c_1z^{-1}+c_2z^{-2}+c_3z^{-3}\right)\\
> y[n]&=v[n]+b_1y[n-1]+b_2y[n-2]+b_3y[n-3]&&\Rightarrow\ V(z)=Y(z)\left(1-b_1z^{-1}-b_2z^{-2}-b_3z^{-3}\right)
> \end{aligned}
> $$
> Dividing, $\frac{Y(z)}{X(z)}$ is the $H(z)$ of eq. 8. The left half implements the input terms, the right half the feedback terms.
>
> Count for $K=3$, $M=4$: 7 multiplications (not counting the gain 1), 6 additions, **6 delays**. In general $K+(M-1)$ delays.

> [!key] Direct form II (Lecture 27, eqs. 9–13)
> The top node of the delay line carries $v[n]$, and the delay outputs are $v[n-1]$, $v[n-2]$, $v[n-3]$:
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> v[n]&=x[n]+b_1v[n-1]+b_2v[n-2]+b_3v[n-3]&&\Rightarrow\ X(z)=V(z)\left(1-b_1z^{-1}-b_2z^{-2}-b_3z^{-3}\right)\\
> y[n]&=c_0v[n]+c_1v[n-1]+c_2v[n-2]+c_3v[n-3]&&\Rightarrow\ Y(z)=V(z)\left(c_0+c_1z^{-1}+c_2z^{-2}+c_3z^{-3}\right)
> \end{aligned}
> $$
> Dividing gives the same $H(z)=\dfrac{c_0+c_1z^{-1}+c_2z^{-2}+c_3z^{-3}}{1-b_1z^{-1}-b_2z^{-2}-b_3z^{-3}}$.
>
> Count: 7 multiplications, 6 additions, **3 delays**. In general $\max(K,\,M-1)$ delays. (The notes' sentence reads "six additions multiplies"; six additions is meant.)

**Why it is the same system.** Direct form I is two LTI systems in series: first the input part, with transfer function $c_0+\dots+c_3z^{-3}$, then the feedback part, with $\frac{1}{1-b_1z^{-1}-\dots}$. LTI systems in series can be swapped, since $H_1H_2=H_2H_1$ ([[concepts/system-algebra|system algebra]]). Put the feedback part first: now its delay line and the input part's delay line both hold delayed copies of the same signal $v[n]$, so one line can serve both. The notes describe the same step as overlapping the two halves of direct form I along their delay paths, which moves the $c_j$ to the right and the $b_i$ to the left.

## 4. Both directions

> [!recipe] Equation ↔ diagram
> **LCCDE → diagrams.**
> 1. Solve for $y[n]$ alone on the left: $y[n]=\sum_ib_iy[n-i]+\sum_jc_jx[n-j]$. Read off the $c_j$ and $b_i$; a term that is absent has coefficient 0.
> 2. **Direct form I:** a delay line of depth $M-1$ on $x[n]$ with gain $c_j$ on the branch leaving delay $j$; a delay line of depth $K$ on $y[n]$ with gain $b_i$ on the branch leaving delay $i$; everything adds into the top rail.
> 3. **Direct form II:** one delay line of depth $\max(K,M-1)$. On the input side the gains $b_i$ feed back to the top; on the output side the gains $c_j$ feed forward to the output, with $c_0$ on the top branch.
> 4. A coefficient of 0 means no branch, but the delay stays if a deeper branch needs it.
>
> **Direct form II diagram → $H(z)$ → LCCDE.**
> 1. Name the top of the delay line $v[n]$; the outputs of the delays are $v[n-1],v[n-2],\dots$
> 2. Input side: $v[n]=x[n]+\sum(\text{feedback gain})\cdot v[n-i]$, so $X(z)=V(z)\left(1-\sum b_iz^{-i}\right)$.
> 3. Output side: $y[n]=\sum(\text{output gain})\cdot v[n-j]$, so $Y(z)=V(z)\sum c_jz^{-j}$.
> 4. Divide: $H(z)=\dfrac{\sum c_jz^{-j}}{1-\sum b_iz^{-i}}$. Cross-multiply and go back to time for the LCCDE.
>
> A gain on the top rail between $x[n]$ and the delay line multiplies $x[n]$ in step 2 and therefore multiplies all of $H(z)$.

> [!question] Example 2 (slides 12–13) — draw both forms
> $y[n]=y[n-2]-2y[n-3]+3x[n]-x[n-1]+2x[n-2]$. Draw the direct form I and direct form II diagrams. What is $H(z)$, and how many delays does each form use?

> [!success]- Answer
> Input coefficients $c_0=3$, $c_1=-1$, $c_2=2$ ($M=3$). Feedback coefficients $b_1=0$, $b_2=1$, $b_3=-2$ ($K=3$): there is no $y[n-1]$ term, so no branch leaves the first delay.
> $$
> H(z)=\frac{3-z^{-1}+2z^{-2}}{1-z^{-2}+2z^{-3}}.
> $$
> Direct form I: $2+3=5$ delays. Direct form II: $\max(3,2)=3$ delays. Both give the same output; the impulse response starts $\{\underset{\uparrow}{3},\ -1,\ 5,\ -7,\ 7,\ -17,\dots\}$ either way.

> [!question] Reading a direct form II diagram
> A direct form II diagram has two delays. Input side: gain $\frac14$ from the first delay, $-\frac18$ from the second. Output side: gain 2 on the top branch, nothing from the first delay, $-1$ from the second. Find $H(z)$ and the LCCDE.

> [!success]- Answer
> $v[n]=x[n]+\frac14v[n-1]-\frac18v[n-2]$ and $y[n]=2v[n]-v[n-2]$, so
> $$
> H(z)=\frac{2-z^{-2}}{1-\frac14z^{-1}+\frac18z^{-2}},\qquad y[n]=\tfrac14y[n-1]-\tfrac18y[n-2]+2x[n]-x[n-2].
> $$
> The feedback gains $\frac14$, $-\frac18$ appear in the LCCDE as they are and in the denominator with their signs flipped.

> [!trap]
> - **Signs.** A feedback gain $b_i$ in the diagram is $+b_i\,y[n-i]$ in the equation and $-b_iz^{-i}$ in the denominator. In the Lecture 9 form $y[n]+\sum a_ky[n-k]=\dots$ the diagram gain is $-a_k$.
> - **Sides swap.** In direct form I the $c_j$ are on the left and the $b_i$ on the right; in direct form II the $b_i$ are on the input (left) side and the $c_j$ on the output (right) side.
> - $v[n]$ in direct form II is neither the input nor the output, and it is not the $v[n]$ of direct form I.
> - A missing term removes a branch, not a delay.
> - A diagram shows how to compute, not whether the system is well behaved: Example 2 has a pole at $z\approx-1.52$, outside the unit circle, so its causal impulse response grows ([[concepts/bibo-stability|BIBO stability]]).
