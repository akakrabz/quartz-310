> [!abstract] In one breath
> - $A_1\gg A_2$: the strong tone's side lobes ($-13$ dB with a rectangular window) bury the weak tone's main lobe even when the frequencies are well separated.
> - $s[n]=x[n]w[n]\leftrightarrow\frac1{2\pi}X_d*W_d$: each cosine becomes a shifted, scaled copy of $W_d(\omega)$, so design $w[n]$ for low side lobes.
> - Hamming: side lobes $-42$ dB, main lobe twice as wide ($\frac{8\pi}{N}$). Tradeoff: narrow lobe or low side lobes, not both.

## 1. A weak tone beside a strong one (eq. 16, Figure 2)

$$
s[n]=\cos(0.5\pi n)+\frac1{10}\cos(0.625\pi n),\qquad0\le n\le63 .
$$

$\Delta\omega=0.125\pi\gt\frac{4\pi}{64}=\frac{\pi}{16}$: full-lobe separated by the [[concepts/frequency-resolution|criteria]]. ## 2. Finite length is multiplication by a window (eqs. 17–19)

> [!key] Windowing (Lecture 23, eqs. 17–19)
> Writing the support explicitly,
> $$
> s[n]=\big(u[n]-u[n-64]\big)\Big[\cos(0.5\pi n)+\tfrac1{10}\cos(0.625\pi n)\Big],
> $$
> for $-\infty\lt n\lt\infty$: a product of two infinite signals: a rectangle $w[n]$ and the sinusoids $x[n]$. By the windowing property ([[concepts/dtft-properties|DTFT properties]]) and the cosine pair,
> $$
> \begin{gathered}
> x[n]w[n]\overset{\mathcal F}{\longleftrightarrow}\frac1{2\pi}X_d(\omega)*W_d(\omega),\\
> \cos(\omega_0n)\overset{\mathcal F}{\longleftrightarrow}\pi\delta(\omega-\omega_0)+\pi\delta(\omega+\omega_0),
> \end{gathered}
> $$
> so by the identity property of convolution
> $$
> S_d(\omega)=\sum_m\frac{A_m}{2}\Big[W_d(\omega-\omega_m)+W_d(\omega+\omega_m)\Big]:
> $$
> shifted, scaled copies of the window's spectrum, for any signal and any window. The window's spectrum is what we design.

## 3. Rectangular and Hamming windows (eqs. 20–21, Figure 3)

> [!key] The two windows (Lecture 23, eqs. 20–21)
> $$
> \begin{gathered}
> r[n]=\begin{cases}1, & 0\le n\le N-1\\0, & \text{otherwise}\end{cases}\\[4pt]
> h[n]=\begin{cases}0.54-0.46\cos\!\left(\dfrac{2\pi n}{N-1}\right), & 0\le n\le N-1\\0, & \text{otherwise}\end{cases}
> \end{gathered}
> $$
> - **Rectangular:** $R_d(\omega)=C(\omega)$, the [[concepts/periodic-sinc|periodic sinc]]: main-lobe half-width $\frac{2\pi}{N}$ ($\frac{\pi}{32}$ for $N=64$), largest side lobe about $-13$ dB.
> - **Hamming:** a raised cosine; sinc-like spectrum with a main lobe twice as wide (width $\frac{8\pi}{N}$, half-width $\frac{4\pi}{N}=\frac{\pi}{16}$ for $N=64$) and side lobes about $-42$ dB.

## 4. Effect on s[n] (Figure 4)

Multiply sample by sample, $s[n]h[n]$, then take the DTFT or DFT (figure in §1, panel b): side lobes greatly lowered, each main lobe widened, peaks $\frac{A}{2}\sum_nh[n]=\frac{34.1}{2}\approx17.1$ and $\frac{0.1\cdot34.1}{2}\approx1.7$. Both components are now clearly visible.

## 5. The design tradeoff (§3.1, slide 13)

| | rectangular | Hamming |
|---|---|---|
| main lobe width | $\frac{4\pi}{N}$ | $\frac{8\pi}{N}$ |
| side lobe attenuation | $-13$ dB | $-42$ dB |
| weights on samples | all $1$ | taper from $1$ at the centre to $0.08$ at the ends |

- Ideal: narrow main lobes (separate close tones) and low side lobes (do not hide weak tones). Figure 3 says we cannot have both; Hann, Bartlett/triangular and Kaiser windows trade between them. Rectangular or Hamming is usually sufficient.
- Why tapering helps: a window weights the samples it keeps. The DFT's periodic extension puts a discontinuity at the window edges ([[concepts/spectral-leakage|leakage]]); down-weighting the edge samples lowers their influence on the spectrum.
- **Windowing is not an LTI system.** It is multiplication in time, convolution in frequency; convolution in time (filtering) is multiplication in frequency. There is no impulse response and no $H_d(\omega)$ to multiply by.

> [!trap]
> - Hamming does **not** narrow main lobes: twice as wide, with lower side lobes ([[exams/midterm-2/past-exams/spring-2025|SP2025 #1(f)]]).
> - "No window" is the rectangular window: $-13$ dB side lobes are always there.
> - Windowing is not filtering: no LTI system, no $Y_d=H_dX_d$.
> - Amplitude from a windowed DFT: divide by $\sum w[n]$ ($\approx0.54N$ for Hamming), not by $N$.
> - The Hamming cosine's denominator is $N-1$, with $N$ the window length.
